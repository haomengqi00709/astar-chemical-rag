**Internal Handoff Brief: Y-Office Tower Engineering Project**

---

### 1. Project Overview

The Y-Office Tower project for client AE Prime in New Cairo, Egypt, involves the comprehensive engineering design for a high-rise office building spanning a total built-up area of 32,025 m², including 27 office floors and multiple basement levels. The core objective is to deliver a complete LOD-350 Revit model across all disciplines, alongside detailed structural analysis, advanced power systems including MV intake and standby generation, and specialized gas suppression systems. The project is slated for completion by August 1, 2026.

### 2. Discipline Tasks

**Architect:**
The Architectural team is responsible for developing all design documentation, starting with Schematic Architecture Drawings and progressing to detailed Architecture Plans, Elevations, Sections, and various detail drawings (Stair, Wet Areas, Wall Sections). They will also produce 3D visualizations (Shots, Views, Perspectives), a comprehensive Bill of Quantity, and the foundational Architectural Revit Model (LOD 350). Their initial work is contingent on receiving the General Report 1 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0001).

**Structural Engineer:**
The Structural team will undertake the complete structural design and analysis, beginning with Schematic Structural Drawings. This includes detailed Structural Calculation Reports, ETABS and SAFE models, and specific design calculations for columns, shear walls, and retaining walls. Key deliverables include Structural Plans, Columns & Axis Drawings, General Notes and Details, Reinforcement Drawings for cores and slabs, and a comprehensive Structural Revit Model (LOD 350). Inputs required are the Architecture Plans Drawings (PRJ.23026-AR-ARP-1001) and General Report 1 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0001).

**HVAC Engineer:**
The HVAC team is tasked with designing the building's heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems. This involves producing Schematic HVAC Drawings, various calculation reports (Heat Load, Exhaust, Pressurization, Ventilation, Smoke Evacuation), and detailed drawings for ductwork, pipework, riser diagrams, schematics, and system plans. They will also deliver a Bill of Quantity and the HVAC Revit Model. Their work depends on the Architecture Plans Drawings (PRJ.23026-AR-ARP-1001) and General Report 2 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0002).

**Plumbing Engineer:**
The Plumbing team will design all water supply and drainage systems. Their deliverables include Schematic Plumbing Drawings, a Plumbing Calculation Report, and detailed layouts for water supply and drainage systems. They are also responsible for drawings of the water supply pump room and tanks, plumbing schematics, toilet details, general details, and a Bill of Quantity, culminating in the Plumbing Revit Model. Key inputs are the Architecture Plans Drawings (PRJ.23026-AR-ARP-1001) and General Report 2 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0002).

**Fire Fighting Engineer:**
The Fire Fighting team will develop the fire protection and suppression systems. This includes Schematic Fire Fighting Drawings, specific calculation reports for CO2/FM-200 suppression in critical rooms (electrical, low current, generator, transformer), and a Fire Sprinkler Report. They will produce Fire Fighting System Layouts, Schematics, General Details, and drawings for the pump room and tanks, along with a Bill of Quantity and the Fire Fighting Revit Model. This discipline has several dependencies: Architecture Plans Drawings (PRJ.23026-AR-ARP-1001), Electrical Drawing General Notes (PRJ.23026-EL-DGN-5001), Single Line Diagram Drawings (PRJ.23026-EL-SLD-5001), General Report 2 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0002), HVAC Duct Work System Drawings (PRJ.23026-HV-DKS-3001), and the Plumbing Calculation Report (PRJ.23026-PL-CAL-7001).

**Low Current Engineer:**
The Low Current team is responsible for designing all low current systems, starting with Schematic Low Current Drawings. This encompasses detailed system drawings for Fire Alarm, CCTV, Access Control, Public Address, and Structured Cabling. They will also provide Riser Diagrams, Schematics, a Legend, a Bill of Quantity, and various Low Current Revit Models (overall, detailed, and schematic). Their work relies on Architecture Plans Drawings (PRJ.23026-AR-ARP-1001), Fire Fighting System Layout Drawings (PRJ.23026-FF-FFS-4001), General Report 2 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0002), and HVAC Duct Work System Drawings (PRJ.23026-HV-DKS-3001).

**Electrical Engineer:**
The Electrical team will design the entire electrical power distribution and lighting systems. Deliverables include Schematic Electrical Drawings, various calculation reports (LUX, Electrical Load, Cable Tray, Earthing, Lighting, Transformer/MV Sizing), Electrical Drawing General Notes, Schematics, Single Line Diagrams, Panelboard Schedules, Lighting System Layouts (general and emergency), Small Power System, Mechanical Power System, and Cable Routing drawings, along with a Bill of Quantity and the Electrical Revit Model. Key inputs are Architecture Plans Drawings (PRJ.23026-AR-ARP-1001), General Report 2 (PRJ.23026-GE-REP-0002), Heat Load Calculation Report (PRJ.23026-HV-CAL-3001), and Plumbing Calculation Report (PRJ.23026-PL-CAL-7001).

### 3. Risk Flags & Engineering Implications

*   **Medium Voltage (MV) Intake:** The design must account for a 13.8 kV MV intake. This necessitates detailed transformer sizing, selection of MV switchgear/Ring Main Units (RMU), and precise MV cable sizing. The Electrical team must ensure the inclusion of the specific Transformer / MV Sizing Calculation (EL-CAL-5006) as a key deliverable.

*   **Standby Power Required:** Essential and life-safety loads must be segregated onto a dedicated emergency/standby board. The Electrical team is responsible for confirming the appropriate sizing of the standby diesel generator and UPS systems to meet these critical loads, adhering to relevant standards such as ISO 8528 and EN 1838 for emergency lighting.

*   **High-Rise Implications:** The high-rise nature of the Y-Office Tower introduces several design complexities. The Electrical team must address rising busway sizing and voltage drop calculations over vertical runs. Lightning protection design, in accordance with IEC 62305, is also critical. For HVAC, stair pressurization systems are required, and Plumbing must design for pumped water zones to ensure adequate pressure at higher levels.

*   **Deep Basement Implications:** The multiple deep basement levels require specific engineering considerations. The Structural team must prioritize the Retaining Walls Design Calculations (ST-CAL-9003). Plumbing will need to design robust sump and drainage pump systems for effective water management. The HVAC team is responsible for designing the garage CO ventilation system to ensure air quality in these enclosed spaces.
