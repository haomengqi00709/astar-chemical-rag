# Confined Space Entry: Safety Equipment and Practices

Ensuring safety during confined space entry requires the diligent use of appropriate safety equipment and adherence to strict work practices. This includes personal protective equipment (PPE), rescue apparatus, communication systems, and safe handling of tools and materials.

**1. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):**
*   All personal protective apparel and respiratory equipment **shall** be worn when entering any hazardous enclosure as required.
*   All personal protective apparel and respiratory equipment **will be maintained** in good condition.
*   Personal protective apparel and respiratory equipment **should** preferably be stored in ventilated cabinets.
*   Personal protective apparel and respiratory equipment **should** be cleaned or sanitized according to good housekeeping and hygiene standards.
*   Employees **should never** unduly expose the skin when working in vessels. Employees **should** wear full coverage clothing at all times.
*   The minimum PPE to be worn **will be specified** in the [[0-REP-8007b_confined_space_hazard_assessment_form|Confined Space Entry Hazard Analysis]].
*   Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) or certified breathing air for self-contained units **shall** be supplied if the integrity of the atmosphere cannot be guaranteed, or if there is any possibility of harmful air contamination or oxygen deficiency while employees are within a tank.
*   Air-purifying half-mask respirators **may not** provide adequate protection and **should not** be used.
*   For specifically recommended protection from hazards of contaminating materials, refer to the Commissioning HSE Manager. Applicable MSDS sheets **must** be available on Site.
*   Respirators **will be issued** to individuals responsible for their safe and sanitary keeping. Respirators **shall** be sanitized before re-issuance to other individuals.
*   Refer to [[0-prc-8015_personal_protective_equipment_policy]] for general PPE guidelines.

**Entry PPE Required:**
*   Chemical protective suit
*   Respirator (cartridges as required as per TSA)
*   SCBA (as required)
*   Head protection
*   Eye protection
*   Foot protection
*   Hand protection

**2. Lifelines, Harnesses, and Rescue Equipment:**
*   An employee **must** be provided with a body harness with a lifeline attached if potential exposure in the vessel is acute, requires respiratory protection, or rescue may be difficult. It is good practice to use a body harness for any vessel entry work.
*   A safety harness and lifeline **should** be used when the vessel's shape, size, and location prohibit safe emergency removal of workers by standby personnel without requiring entry into the tank.
*   If the vessel's shape, size, and location do not allow safe emergency removal by standby personnel without tank entry, a specific rescue procedure **must** be developed in the pre-planning stage and made a specific part of the vessel entry permits.
*   It is advisable to have a block and tackle positioned on a tripod or otherwise fastened above the manhole in some cases.
*   Manholes large enough to accommodate the man and his safety gear **should** be provided, such as circular manholes of 24" in diameter. Where existing manholes are smaller than 20" in the largest dimension, consideration **shall** be given for possible use of wrist harnesses.
*   The free end of the lifeline **should** be secured to a fixed object and attended by an observer (standby man) who **will** keep the man in the tank always within sight or verbal communication.
*   The observer **may** pass tools but **must** have no other job that takes his attention away from the man in the vessel, interferes with attempts to withdraw the victim by lifeline, or requires leaving the vicinity of the vessel for any length of time.
*   In case of emergency, the outside observer **must never** enter the vessel until relieved at his post. The observer's job is to summon aid immediately, attempt to remove the victim by use of the lifeline, and perform all other necessary rescue functions from outside.
*   Upon arrival of help, the observer **may** enter the vessel for rescue work only when assured that adequate outside assistance is present and he wears all prescribed protective equipment.
*   A safety observer or standby man **should** be well-trained in basic First Aid principles such as rescue breathing techniques.
*   Rescuers entering the vessel **must** be protected with the safety equipment required by the situation, including lifeline, harness, proper personal protective equipment, and respiratory equipment.
*   For rescue purposes, at least one unit of self-contained breathing equipment **should** be located outside the confined space and adjacent to the vessel. Harness, rope, and other indicated emergency equipment **should** also be located outside the confined space and adjacent to the vessel.
*   One additional safety line **will be available** for each person within the vessel. If an entry man's lifeline is fouled on a structural member, the original lifeline **will be** unsnapped, a new lifeline hooked on, and a rescue effected.

**Rescue Equipment Required:**
*   Harness and lifeline for each entrant
*   Lifting tripod

**3. Communication:**
*   A clearly understood signal system **shall** be established prior to the start of the operation.
    *   Signal "Three Pulls" means "Allow more slack in the line".
    *   Signal "Two Pulls" means "Take up slack in line".
    *   Signal "One Pull" means "Emergency, pull man from tank".
*   **No one shall enter a confined space** until a properly trained and equipped standby man is present.
*   In emergency situations, persons working in vessels **should** exit only through the entry point.

**Communication Equipment Required:**
*   Wired two-way communication device
*   Wireless two-way communication device

**4. Tools and Electrical Equipment:**
*   Hand tools **shall** be clean, in good condition, and **shall** be selected carefully according to the uses intended.
*   Electrical tools and equipment such as hand lamps and extension cords **must** be on a 12 volt system and **must** be plugged into a working Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI).
*   In any Class I or Class II exposure (in dangerous flammable dust concentration), use air-operated power tools.
*   Air power tools **shall not** be used in confined spaces if the air system is backed with nitrogen or other inert gases.

**5. Welding and Cutting:**
*   Employees performing electrical welding in tanks **shall** be provided with and required to use such personal protective equipment as may be required.
*   Welding and cutting torches **must not** be taken into the tank until ready to be used and **must** be removed from the tank immediately after use.
*   Cylinders of oxygen or other gas **shall never** be taken into tanks and **should** be turned off at the cylinder valve when not in use.
*   A fully charged and inspected fire extinguisher **must** be readily available.

**6. Site Control:**
*   Ladders used in tanks **should** be lashed at the top and, if possible, at the bottom.
*   Signs posted near the vessel help keep unnecessary people away, ensure that potentially harmful operations will not be started independently nearby, and help guide rescuers to the scene if necessary.
*   Class I locations are those enclosed areas in which flammable, volatile liquids, gases, and vapours or mixtures are or may be present in quantities sufficient to produce explosive or ignitable mixtures.
*   Class II locations are those enclosed areas which are hazardous because of the presence of combustible dust.
*   Open pits and hazardous vessel operations **require barricading**. Barricading **requires** a substantial railing 42" high and toe boards or equivalent protection to prevent kicking objects laying on the floor or ground into the pit.
*   Rope with streamers at the 42" level is recommended for closing aisles in adjacent areas.
*   Flashing lights on barricades are necessary when pits are open at night or in dimly lighted areas inside.

**Equipment Required for Confined Space Entry (Appendix I):**
*   Gas tester (O2, LEL, CO, H2S minimum) - consider other job-specific chemicals for gas testing.
*   Gas tester calibration gas
*   Duct tape
*   Ventilation device(s)
*   Lock out kit
