# Textual Document Structure and Numbering

To maintain clarity, readability, and consistency across all textual documents, specific guidelines for section numbering and overall document structure *must* be rigorously followed. These guidelines ensure that information is presented in an organized and easily digestible format for all users.

**Section Numbering Hierarchy:**
*   **Preferred Level:** Section numbering *must* preferably be limited to a single level. This approach promotes maximum simplicity and ease of navigation throughout the document, making it straightforward for readers to locate specific topics.
*   **Maximum Levels:** Under no circumstances *must* section numbering exceed two levels. For instance, a document *may* utilize "Section 1.0" and "Section 1.1," but the use of a third level, such as "Section 1.1.1," is prohibited. This restriction helps prevent overly complex and cumbersome numbering schemes that can hinder readability.
*   **Further Delineation:** For any additional breakdown or detailed presentation of information beyond the second level of section numbering, bullet points *must* be employed. This method ensures that sub-information is clearly presented without introducing further numerical hierarchy, maintaining the document's clean structural integrity.

**Commentary Section:**
Documents *may* incorporate an unnumbered section specifically titled "Commentary." This dedicated section is intended for providing supplementary explanations, detailed discussions, or contextual background information that enhances understanding of the main content but is not a direct directive or a step within a procedure. The "Commentary" section *must* always begin on a new page and be positioned at the very end of the document. This placement ensures that it does not disrupt the primary flow of instructions or specifications, serving as an optional, yet valuable, resource for deeper insight.

**Section Numbering and Delineation**
Section numbering will preferably be at one level.
Section numbering will be at no more than two levels.
Further delineation beyond two levels will use bullets.

**Commentary Section**
Additional explanations and discussion will be included in an unnumbered Section entitled "Commentary".
The "Commentary" section will begin on a new page at the end of the document.
