# Gas/Gas Heat Exchanger Shell and Baffle Design

The design of the shell and baffles is fundamental to the performance and structural integrity of radial flow gas/gas heat exchangers.

**Shell Passes:**
*   The number of shell passes (NP) **should be balanced** to optimize the heat transfer coefficients (Hi and Ho).
*   Too many shell passes can lead to undesirably high pressure drop and increased noise levels.
*   Conversely, too few shell passes may result in poor shell side heat transfer efficiency and excessively long unsupported tube spans, which could necessitate the addition of dummy baffles.

**Shell Diameter:**
*   The shell diameter **should be** 18”–24” greater than the disc baffle outside diameter (DOBAFF). This additional allowance is specifically for the accommodation of expansion plates.

**Tubesheet Thickness:**
*   Tubesheet thickness is determined during the [[mechanical_calculations|mechanical design]] phase.
*   The tubesheet thickness **should not be less than** ¾”.
*   If mechanical design has already been completed, the actual tubesheet thickness **should be used** in the thermal design calculations.
*   For exchangers featuring different top and bottom tubesheet thicknesses, the average value **should be input** into the design software.

**Baffle Thickness:**
*   Baffle thickness is also determined during [[mechanical_calculations|mechanical design]].
*   Standard baffle thickness for stainless steel baffles is ¼”.
*   Standard baffle thickness for carbon steel baffles is 3/8”.

**Duct Diameters:**
*   Duct diameters are initially determined by the [[tgas_2_thermal_design_software|TGAS/2 program]] based on an assumed velocity of 80 ft/s.
*   These diameters can be subsequently adjusted to suit existing ducting configurations or to match the specifications provided on the [[process_data_sheet_overview|process data sheet]].
