# Regeneration Furnace Process and Design Conditions

The Regeneration Furnace is a critical component designed to decompose spent sulphuric acid and ammonium bisulphate compounds, which originate from an MMA generation process.

The furnace handles a variety of feeds, including:
*   Spent sulphuric acid
*   Ammonium bisulphate
*   Water and organics
*   Molten Sulphur
*   Strong sulphuric acid containing nitric acid (referred to as “candle drips”)
*   Process vent gases containing organics
*   Furnace ash conditioner, specifically liquid “alum” (aluminum sulphate)
*   Burner combustion gases

The furnace is equipped with two types of burners: a fuel oil burner and a combination fuel oil/organics burner. These burners serve multiple functions:
*   Providing heat to dissociate acid compounds.
*   Providing heat to combust organic compounds.
*   Producing an SO2 process gas.
*   Heating combustion products to the required exit conditions.

Conditions at the exit of the furnace are critical for downstream processes, typically ranging from 980 oC to 1100 oC, with an oxygen content of 1.5 % O2 (by volume, wet basis). The SO2 content at the furnace exit is approximately 5 to 6 vol% (wet basis).

Both burners **will** be capable of operating across a range of fuel to air ratios, bounded by 15 % excess air and 55 % excess air. Secondary air is introduced at the front of the furnace to precisely control the % O2 at the furnace exit.

Spent acid **will** be injected into the burner flame through acid guns positioned above the centreline. Other furnace feeds, such as candle drips, sulphur, and process vents, **will** be injected via sprays located at the front of the furnace.
