# Preheat Exchanger Function and Flow

The preheat exchanger is primarily utilized to provide initial heat to the plant during start-up operations. In metallurgical plants, it also serves to supply supplementary heat when the gas strength falls below the autothermal limit, ensuring process continuity. This exchanger functions by using hot combustion gas from a preheat furnace to heat air, which is then blended with the main process gas stream.

**Shell Side Flow:**
*   Hot combustion gas from the preheat furnace enters the shell side of the exchanger.
*   The preheat exchanger shell side combustion gas flows upwards.
*   This combustion gas is then discharged to the atmosphere.

**Tube Side Flow:**
*   The process gas (air, which will be mixed with main process gas) passes through the tube side of the exchanger.
*   The preheat exchanger tube side process gas flows downwards, absorbing heat from the combustion gas.

**Operational Considerations:**
*   The preheat exchanger is not designed for continuous operation and is subject to repeated cycling. The severity of this cycling can vary significantly depending on plant operation.
*   To minimize differential thermal expansion stresses caused by this cycling, the preheat exchanger unit **should be kept as short as practical**.
*   If Bunker C (#6 fuel oil) is used as the fuel, the process flows **should be switched** such that the combustion gas is on the tube side. Additionally, the exchanger **should be arranged horizontally** to facilitate cleaning and maintenance.
