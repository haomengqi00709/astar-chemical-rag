# Dampers Inspection

Dampers are mechanical devices used in ductwork and piping systems to regulate or stop the flow of gases or liquids. Their proper functioning is critical for process control, isolation, and safety. Inspection of dampers is a key activity during commissioning and maintenance.

**Inspection Requirements:**
*   All dampers in piping or ductwork connected to an exchanger or other process equipment **must** be inspected to ensure they move freely and operate as intended. This inspection is particularly critical as a "last chance to check these" before system startup or sealing.
*   **Actuation Check:** Verify that the damper can be fully opened and fully closed, either manually or via its automated actuator (pneumatic, electric, hydraulic).
*   **Range of Motion:** Confirm that the damper blade(s) or disc(s) achieve their full range of motion without obstruction or binding.
*   **Sealing Integrity:** For isolation dampers, check that they provide an adequate seal in the closed position. This may involve visual inspection of sealing surfaces or, in some cases, leak testing.
*   **Linkages and Bearings:** Inspect all linkages, bearings, and shafts for wear, corrosion, or damage that could impede smooth operation. Lubricate as necessary.
*   **Indicators:** Verify that any position indicators (local or remote) accurately reflect the actual damper position.
*   **Control System Interface:** For automated dampers, confirm proper integration with the [[control_system_architecture_block_diagram]] and that control signals correctly actuate the damper.
*   **Material Condition:** Inspect the damper body and blades for corrosion, erosion, or other material degradation, especially in aggressive process environments.

Any deficiencies found during damper inspection **must** be addressed immediately to prevent operational issues, safety hazards, or inefficient process performance.
