# Acid Resistant Brick Lining Installation Procedures

The installation of acid resistant brick lining requires meticulous attention to detail to ensure structural integrity, chemical resistance, and long-term performance in corrosive environments.

**Mortar Application and Brick Placement:**
*   All brick bonding surfaces **will** be thoroughly and evenly buttered with mortar to ensure complete contact and adhesion.
*   The resulting brick joints **will** be completely full and solid, eliminating any voids that could compromise the lining.
*   Bedding layers **will** be completely full and solid, providing a stable and continuous base for the brick courses.
*   Ceramic rope packed joints **will** also be completely full and solid, ensuring a tight seal where used.
*   When placing bricks into position, a sliding movement **will** be used to remove any voids and ensure proper mortar distribution.
*   Bricks **will** be pounded into their final position using a rubber mallet to achieve thin, uniform joints, which are crucial for the lining's chemical resistance and structural stability.

**Course Alignment and Joint Staggering:**
*   All brick courses **must** be straight and level, maintaining the aesthetic and functional integrity of the lining.
*   All courses **must** be tightly wedged into place to prevent any movement or shifting after installation.
*   Joints between layers and between courses **will** be staggered for maximum strength, enhancing the overall structural integrity and resistance to cracking of the lining system.

**Work Continuity and Brick Handling:**
*   A course of brickwork, once started, **must** be finished before the mortar begins to set. This ensures monolithic curing and prevents cold joints.
*   No more than one brick course **will** be worked on at any given time to ensure quality, proper curing, and consistent workmanship.
*   Bricks **must** be handled carefully to avoid chipping and cracks, which could lead to defects in the finished lining.
*   Damaged or chipped bricks **will** only be used for cuts, minimizing waste and maintaining the quality of the primary lining.
*   Brick cutting **will** be performed with a brick saw outside the vessels to be lined to prevent contamination of the vessel interior and ensure a clean working environment.
*   Cuts **will** be smooth and straight, allowing for tight and uniform joints.
*   All residual water from the saw **will** be wiped from the brick before installation to prevent moisture ingress into the mortar.

**Post-Installation Protection and Acid Washing:**
*   The mortar **must** be protected against moisture ingress and condensation while lining work is taking place through to acid plant start-up. This is crucial for the long-term integrity and chemical resistance of the mortar.
*   The completed lining **will** be acid washed to protect the mortar against moisture ingress and condensation. This process helps to condition the mortar and enhance its resistance.
*   Acid washing **should** be completed as soon as possible after the vessel lining passes its final visual inspection to maximize its protective effect.
