# Hazardous Area Classification Principles

The process of classifying hazardous areas is a critical step in ensuring electrical safety in plants handling flammable materials. This involves a collaborative effort and a comprehensive understanding of plant conditions and material properties.

**Responsibilities:**
The responsibility for area classification rests jointly with the process and electrical departments, as well as any other department responsible for plant safety and operation. This inter-departmental approach ensures all relevant expertise and operational insights are considered.

**Preliminary Step: Determining Flammable Atmosphere:**
The preliminary step in classifying a plant is to determine the existence of a flammable atmosphere. This assessment requires detailed information, including:
*   Properties and process conditions of materials.
*   Volumes and dispositions of materials.
*   Possibilities of escape of flammable material.
*   Practical experience regarding conditions likely during start-up and normal running.

The overall basis for classification **will be** provided by information on material properties, process conditions, volumes, dispositions, escape possibilities, practical experience, design and construction details, proposed ventilation, drainage, plant topography, and other relevant factors.

**Role of Ventilation and Dispersal:**
A crucial question to ask when defining the extent of a hazardous area is: "How well is the plant ventilated?" The dispersal of a flammable atmosphere is influenced by several factors:
*   The quantity escaping.
*   The velocity of escape.
*   The gas or vapour density and diffusion rate.
*   The ventilation air currents.
*   The topographical features of the plant involved.

**Behavior of Gases/Vapours:**
*   **Lighter than Air:** Gases or vapours lighter than air (e.g., hydrogen in chloralkali and sodium chlorate plants), escaping at low velocity, **will** disperse fairly rapidly upwards in a well-ventilated situation.
*   **Heavier than Air:** Gases or vapours heavier than air (e.g., gases/vapours in NCG plants), escaping at low velocity, **will** tend to flow downward and may travel long distances over the ground before safe dispersal by atmospheric diffusion.

Understanding these principles is fundamental to accurately defining and managing hazardous locations. For specific classification types, refer to [[hazardous_location_classification_types]].
