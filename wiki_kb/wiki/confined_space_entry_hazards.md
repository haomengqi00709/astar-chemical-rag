# Confined Space Entry: Identified Hazards

Entry into confined spaces presents a variety of significant hazards that **must** be thoroughly assessed and controlled to ensure personnel safety. These hazards can be categorized as atmospheric, physical, chemical, and mechanical.

**Atmospheric Hazards:**
*   **Toxic Vapours:** Unhealthy or fatal concentrations can arise from residues, leakages, cleaning solvents, welding, cutting, or other processes. These can be present even after initial cleaning.
*   **Flammable Gases or Dust:** The presence of flammable gases or combustible dusts creates a high potential for fire or explosion.
*   **Oxygen Deficiency:**
    *   Toxic vapours can replace oxygen, leading to an oxygen-deficient atmosphere.
    *   Inert gases, such as nitrogen used to purge air from piping and vessels prior to start-up, can displace oxygen.
    *   Air in clean tanks closed for an extended period can become deficient in oxygen due to oxidation of the tank’s metal.
    *   Lack of oxygen may also result from improper or inadequate ventilation during tank work.
    *   Atmospheric oxygen concentration below 19.5 percent or greater than 23.5 percent is considered hazardous.

**Physical Hazards:**
*   **Electric Shock:** This can result from portable lights, tools, or improperly grounded electrical equipment.
*   **Mechanical Equipment:** Defective or incorrectly locked out mechanical equipment (e.g., agitators, mixers) can cause injury.
*   **Difficulty in Worker Removal:** The confined nature of the space can make it difficult to remove workers who are seriously injured or lose consciousness, complicating [[emergency_procedures_and_first_aid|first aid]] and rescue efforts.
*   **Slipping, Falling, or Falling Objects:** These are common physical hazards within confined spaces due to limited visibility, uneven surfaces, or work being performed above.

**Chemical Hazards:**
*   **Direct Contact with Corrosive Chemicals:** Residues or active chemicals can cause severe burns or other injuries upon contact.
*   **Dermatitis-Producing Chemicals:** Certain chemicals can cause skin irritation or allergic reactions.

**Flammability and Toxicity Thresholds:**
*   Entry **must** be restricted into a confined space where flammable vapor or gas is greater than 10 percent of the Lower Explosive Level (LEL).
*   Entry **must** be restricted into a confined space where toxic values are above the threshold value in accordance with OSHA 29 CFR Part 1910; Subpart Z - Toxic and Hazardous Substances, including supplements of General Safety and Health Regulations.

Before any entry, a thorough [[confined_space_entry_pre_entry_procedures|hazard assessment]] and [[confined_space_entry_permit_system|permit issuance]] process **must** be completed, considering all potential hazards. Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) for any chemicals present or used in the space **must** be reviewed by the Entry Supervisor.
