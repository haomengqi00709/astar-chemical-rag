# Clad Steel Weld Preparation and Assembly

Thorough preparation and precise assembly are fundamental to achieving high-quality welds in austenitic stainless clad steel fabrications.

**Surface Cleaning and Contamination Control**
The surface and edges of any austenitic steel to be heated **must** be cleaned free from all traces of foreign matter (e.g., marking paint, oil, etc.). This cleaning **must** be performed immediately prior to the application of heat. Acceptable cleaning methods include grinding, wire brushing with a stainless steel brush, and/or non-chlorinated solvent cleaning.

The Fabricator **must** implement necessary controls to prevent contamination of weldments at all stages of fabrication. These controls **must** include the segregation of wire brushes, grinding wheels, and other tools for exclusive use on stainless steels. Immediately prior to welding, the fusion and adjacent metal (both internal and external) **must** be free from moisture, scale, paint, grease, or other foreign matter.

**Alignment and Fit-Up**
Alignment of welding edges at butt joints **must** be in accordance with the relevant code, specification, etc.
*   For plates up to and including 1/4" (6mm) in thickness, the maximum misalignment (High - Low) is 1/16" (1.5mm).
*   For plates above 1/4" (6mm) in thickness, the maximum misalignment (High - Low) is 25% of the plate thickness or 1/8" (3mm), whichever is smaller.

The specific welding detail for clad plate shown in [[4-SPC-0016_appendix_b_clad_base_metal_detail]] **must** be adhered to by the Fabricator.

**Holding and Support**
Parts to be welded **must** be securely held in position by mechanical means, welded-on bridge pieces, or by tack welding to maintain specified alignment and gap during welding.
*   Where a jig or other suitable device is used, its construction **must** allow adequate access for welding and cleaning.
*   Jig construction **must** be such that external loading is not applied to a joint during welding.
*   Welded-on bridge or support spiders **will** be made of austenitic steel on the surface welded in contact with the vessel or heat exchanger.
*   Where welded-on bridge pieces or support spiders are used, the plate surface **will not** be left in a damaged condition after their removal.

**Tack Welding**
Tack welds employed for assembly purposes may form part of the completed weld, provided that complete fusion is effected at the root of the weld. The welding procedure used for tack welds **must** be the same as that employed for the remainder of the root run. Sound tack welds **must** be thoroughly fused into the root run of the main weld. Where necessary, tack welds **should** preferably be at least one inch (25mm) long. It is sometimes necessary to thin down the ends of tack welds to ensure complete fusion into the root run. Cracked and unsound tack welds **will** be cut out prior to welding.

**Welding Sequence**
Substantially all carbon steel welding **will** be completed in each joint before stainless welding on the joint is started.

**Weld Alignment and Misalignment**
Alignment of welding edges at butt joints **must** be in accordance with relevant code, specification, etc.
*   Maximum misalignment (High - Low) for plates up to and including 1/4" in thickness is 1/16".
*   Maximum misalignment (High - Low) for plates above 1/4" in thickness is 25% of the plate thickness or 1/8", whichever is smaller.

**Work Holding and Jigs**
Parts to be welded **must** be securely held in position by mechanical means, welded-on bridge pieces, or by tack welding to maintain specified alignment and gap during welding.
Where a jig or other suitable device is used, its construction **must** allow adequate access for welding and cleaning, and **must** be such that external loading is not applied to a joint during welding.
Welded-on bridge or support spiders **will** be of austenitic steel on the surface welded in contact with the vessel or heat exchanger. Where used, the plate surface **will not** be left in a damaged condition after they are removed.

**Tack Welding**
Tack welds employed for assembly purposes may form part of the completed weld provided that complete fusion is effected at the root of the weld. The welding procedure used for tack welds **must** be the same as that employed for the remainder of the root run. Sound tack welds **must** be thoroughly fused into the root run of the main weld. Where necessary, tack welds should preferably be at least one inch long. Cracked and unsound tack welds **will** be cut out prior to welding. It is sometimes necessary to thin down the ends of tack welds to ensure complete fusion into the root run.
