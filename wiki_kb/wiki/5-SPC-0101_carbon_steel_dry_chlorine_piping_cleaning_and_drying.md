# Carbon Steel Dry Chlorine Piping - Cleaning and Drying Procedures (5-SPC-0101)

Cleaning and drying of carbon steel piping for dry chlorine service are critical steps to prevent violent reactions, corrosion, and system failure. Appendix 1 of 5-SPC-0101, titled "Cleaning & Testing of Piping - Dry Chlorine Service," provides detailed procedures for both initial installation and future maintenance.

**General Requirements**
Carbon steel piping for dry (H2O free) chlorine gas and liquid **must** be cleaned before use, as chlorine can react violently with cutting oil, grease, and other foreign materials. Cleaning **must** be followed by drying to ensure all moisture is removed, preventing the formation of aggressive acid that causes corrosion and failure. Cleaning **must not** be done with alcohols, hydrocarbons, or other compounds that react with chlorine.

The intent of the cleaning procedures is a two-stage system:
1.  **First Stage Cleaning**: During pipe spooling.
2.  **Second Stage Cleaning**: During installation.

Procedures for dry chlorine piping cleaning do not necessarily apply to Tanks, Heat Exchangers, or other associated dry chlorine equipment.

**Component Cleaning and Drying (Instruments, Valves, Inline Items)**
*   All vendor-supplied protective packaging for instruments, valves, or other inline items for "Dry Chlorine" piping **must** be maintained upon receipt at the jobsite.
*   Items received without protective packaging **must** be cleaned and dried prior to installation.
*   All internal surfaces of items contacting "Dry Chlorine" **must** be cleaned and dried.
*   Items **must** be thoroughly disassembled for cleaning and drying.
*   Cleaning **should** be done using Trichloroethane.
*   Drying **should** be done in an oven.
*   Items **should** be reassembled while hot and stored in vapour tight packages with internal drying aids (desiccant bags).
*   Components requiring grease **must** be treated with chlorine-compatible grease (chloro-fluorinated grease). No other grease or lubricant is acceptable.
*   Instruments requiring bench calibration **should** be calibrated prior to drying and packaging, in an environment as clean as possible, minimizing exposure to oils, greases, and lubricants.
*   Immediately prior to use, items **should** be visually checked for absence of moisture or other contaminants.

**On-Site Pipe and Fitting Cleaning**
*   Prior to installation, protective coverings **should** be removed, and the pipe or fitting **should** be visually inspected.
*   Oil, grease, or movable dirt **should** be cleaned with a dry rag. If insufficient, sand blasting is recommended.
*   Cleaning using a solvent such as trichloroethane is required. A solvent "bath" can be made on site by blocking off a 25 foot (8 meter) section of trench, lining it with FRP, and filling it with solvent. Piping sections can be dipped and allowed to air dry.
*   Dry and solvent-cleaned pipe can be installed and welded. Grease used for cutting and welding, or burrs and shavings, **must not** be left inside the pipe.
*   After installation of each pipe section (with temporary pipe spools), air cleaning is required to remove metal burrs or shavings. The air supply **must** be oil-free. Pipe sections to be cleaned **must** be kept relatively short, and high air flowrates (velocities) **will** be required.

**Steam Cleaning (Post-Hydrostatic Test)**
*   Steam cleaning of all pipes **must** start immediately after [[5-SPC-0101_carbon_steel_dry_chlorine_piping_pressure_and_leak_testing|hydrostatic testing]] is complete.
*   Steam cleaning removes oil, grease, and loose scale.
*   Piping system cleaning **must** be carried out using low pressure steam.
*   Condensate **must** be able to drain from each pipe section during steam cleaning. Dead-ended sections **must** be opened to permit steam flow.
*   Steam cleaning of each main pipe section **should** continue for at least 24 hours, or until a white target at the pipe section outlet shows no signs of rust particles or millscale.

**Drying with Air (Post-Steam Cleaning)**
*   The chlorine dry-air padding system and the steam supply system **must** be completed and operating before piping can be steamed and dried.
*   A dewpoint analyzer **should** be available prior to any inline cleaning and drying. Trace water levels are measured at -40°C dewpoint.
*   Dry, clean air (padding air) **must** be blown through the hot pipe immediately after steaming.
*   Heating dry air to 93°C prior to entry into chlorine piping aids drying.
*   Dry air flow rate **should** start at high volume rates, then be reduced to allow equilibration.
*   A positive flow of dry air **should** be maintained through the piping system during installation of pre-spooled pipe, valves, instruments, and inline devices. Dry air **should** be allowed to bleed from all connections.
*   For dryness testing of exiting air, the inlet flow rate **should** be reduced to an absolute minimum. A Dew Point of -40°C **must** be achieved in the exiting air. Exit air dryness testing **should** be done at multiple exit points.
