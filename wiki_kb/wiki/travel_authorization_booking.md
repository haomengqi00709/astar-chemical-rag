# Travel Authorization and Booking

Proper authorization and timely booking are critical components of the Group Travel Procedure (11.1 PR 2) to ensure both cost-effectiveness and compliance.

### Authorization Requirements
Only journeys that have been properly authorized may be undertaken. This applies to all modes of transport, including road, rail, and air. Line Management is responsible for authorizing travel and related items such as car and hotel requirements, within the limits set by this procedure.

Each Company **shall** designate a small number of nominated individuals (no more than 6-8) who possess the authority to approve travel options that incur a higher cost than the standard offerings from the travel agency, particularly when special requirements or circumstances dictate such a choice. The Travel Agency is explicitly instructed **not** to book a more expensive travel option without prior approval from one of these nominated representatives within the relevant Business Area.

### Advance Notice for Travel and Accommodation
Employees are requested to provide notice of their travel and accommodation requirements as far in advance as possible. Ideally, this notice **should** be given no less than seven days before the intended travel date. This allows sufficient time for securing cost-effective options and proper authorization.

### Special Travel Arrangements
Any special travel arrangement, such as the chartering of an aircraft, **must** be justified on a case-by-case basis. Such arrangements **must** receive prior written approval from the Business Area EVP or the local Company President/Managing Director. This ensures that extraordinary expenses are thoroughly vetted and approved at a high level.

### Booking with Nominated Travel Agencies
All travel reservations **must** be booked through the Company's nominated travel agencies. These agencies are authorized to select and recommend the most cost-effective airlines and routings that meet the traveller's demands, while also maximizing cost-saving opportunities for the Company. They are expected to act as a full-service bureau for all travel needs and provide service levels that are equal to or better than any other external source.

### Pre-Travel Considerations
Employees should make a commercial judgement as to whether the objective of the visit can be met without the need for travel.

### General Travel Principles
The number of employees involved in any journey must be kept to a minimum. Travel to meetings involving only Group employees is to be discouraged, reinforcing the consideration of alternative communication methods.

### Cost Authorization Exceptions
Each Company will specifically name a small number of nominated individuals, no more than 6-8, who are authorised to accept a higher cost than that offered by the travel agency where special requirements or circumstances exist. The Travel Agency is instructed not to book a more expensive travel option unless and until they receive approval from one of these nominated representatives at the Business Area concerned.
