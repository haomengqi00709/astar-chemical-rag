# NCG Plant Hazardous Areas

Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Non-Condensable Gas (NCG) plants, which handle reduced sulphur gases (TRS), are generally considered non-hazardous based on CEC, NEC, NFPA 497 codes, and operational experience. However, specific exceptions exist where TRS gases may be present, necessitating hazardous area classifications.

**TRS Gas Handling:**
*   In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics NCG plants, TRS gases in concentrated form are collected, motivated with steam ejectors, and eventually destroyed in incinerators, lime kilns, or boilers.
*   TRS gases are contained in vessels and piping, except at specific points that are classified as hazardous areas.

**Specific Hazardous Locations (Class I, Division 1 or 2):**

*   **Sample Points:** These are equipped with a valve and are normally plugged or **will have** a gauge mounted. They are classified as hazardous areas.
*   **Seal Leg Drains:** On sealed sewer systems, these are normally sealed with water. High points of seal leg drains are vented outside. These are classified as hazardous areas.
*   **Rupture Disks:** These are normally intact. The downstream side of rupture disks is vented to the outside of the building. These are classified as hazardous areas.
*   **Pressure/Vacuum Breakers (PVB's):** These **will be** normally sealed. PVB vent lines are located outside of the building. These are classified as hazardous areas.
*   **Vent Stacks:** Every time an NCG system shuts down, the vent valve opens, and TRS gases **will be** present at the vent stack(s). NCG vent stacks are always located outside and high up. The area around the top of the NCG vent stack(s) is classified as Class I, Division 1 for a 1.5 m (5’-0”) radius sphere.

**Ventilation Requirements:**
*   Good natural building ventilation **must** be ensured at all times around the NCG ejector station.

**Gas Composition for Assessment:**
A list of gases and their percentages **will be** used for assessing areas where TRS gases may be present.
