# Process Flow Diagram (PFD) Overview

The Process Flow Diagram (PFD) is a fundamental document in chemical engineering, serving as a graphical representation of a defined system. This page outlines its core purpose, content, and general conventions.

**Purpose of the PFD:**
The primary purpose of the PFD is to record the "steady state" balanced flows, temperatures, and pressures of the defined system. It provides a high-level overview of the process, focusing on the main process streams and equipment.

**Definition of "Steady State":**
The term "steady state" is understood to apply for "instantaneous design conditions" such as maximum operation, minimum turndown, or autothermal conditions. These conditions represent the operational points under which it is necessary to establish mass and energy balances for the plant.

**Usage:**
The PFD is utilized by the process engineer as a starting point for producing other detailed project documents, including:
*   Process Data Sheets (PDS)
*   Process Data Lists (PDL)
*   Line Lists

**Content Requirements:**
*   The PFD **must** show the steady state mass balance at the design capacity of the plant.
*   Flows, pressures, and temperatures for all significant streams, including utilities, **must** also be shown on the PFD.
*   Normally, the only physical property that needs to be shown on the PFD is density. Other physical properties are typically detailed on relevant PDS, PDL, or Line Lists.

**What is NOT Shown on a PFD:**
*   Pump kick-back lines
*   Drain lines
*   Other minor process details that would clutter the diagram and detract from its primary purpose of showing the main process flow.

**Additional PFDs:**
In certain complex cases, such as strong acid plants, additional Process Flow Diagrams may be required to fully define the plant capacity and/or operation.
