# Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) and Task Safety Awareness (TSA) Integration

The company's safety framework integrates [[0-PRC-8007_job_hazard_analysis]] (JHA) with [[task_safety_awareness_overview]] (TSA) to provide a robust approach to hazard identification and control. While both are crucial for safety, their application differs based on the nature and prior assessment of a task.

**Relationship and Application:**
*   A supervisor assigning a task **shall** identify necessary instructions and precautions from any prior JHA. These instructions and precautions **shall** provide the maximum level of safety for each employee.
*   The site HSE manager or supervisor **will** review existing JHA documents with employees as part of the pre-task TSA, ensuring that all relevant hazards and controls are understood.
*   In situations where a full JHA has been prepared immediately prior to a job in conjunction with the work team, a separate TSA is not generally required. The JHA itself, developed collaboratively, serves the immediate pre-task review purpose.
*   However, if a generic JHA is available for a task, a TSA is **necessary** to ensure that all specific hazards likely to be present during the particular execution of that task are thoroughly considered. This ensures site-specific conditions and immediate circumstances are accounted for.
*   For new or complicated tasks, the HSE Manager **will determine** if a full JHA is required. When a full JHA is mandated, employees **will be led** by the site HSE Manager (or other qualified personnel such as a fire watch, hole watch, or attendant) in performing it.
*   The supervisor **must ensure** employees have understood the safe working plan, whether derived from a JHA, a TSA, or both.
*   A TSA **will be necessary** if conditions change or if new workers become involved in a task, even if a prior JHA or TSA was conducted. This ensures continuous adaptation to evolving circumstances and personnel.

This integrated approach ensures that all tasks, from routine to complex, are subjected to appropriate levels of hazard assessment before work commences, as detailed in [[task_safety_awareness_implementation_and_requirements]].

*   The site HSE manager will also review existing Job Hazard Analysis with employees prior to the start of a task.
*   In new or complicated tasks, the HSE Manager will determine if a full Job Hazard Analysis is required. Where a full Job Hazard Analysis is required, employees will be led by the site HSE Manager (or other qualified personnel) in performing it.
*   Employees will use the TSA method to analyse common tasks performed repeatedly. They will give more consideration to the TSA of tasks performed less frequently.
