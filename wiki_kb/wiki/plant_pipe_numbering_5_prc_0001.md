# Plant Pipe Identification Procedure (5-PRC-0001)

This procedure, documented as 5-PRC-0001, outlines the requirements for establishing and maintaining standard identification numbers for plant pipelines. This system ensures consistency across projects and facilitates efficient referencing and re-use of design information.

**Scope and Application**
Standard identification numbers for plant lines **shall** be used for all assigned technologies and recorded on [[piping_and_instrumentation_drawings]]. This numbering system also serves as a foundational element for the development of all related numbering systems, including [[document_numbering_0_prc_0002]].

The standard identification numbers **shall** be applied to all projects unless specific project requirements necessitate an alternative system. In such cases, the project team **must** maintain a dual system within the [[line_list_5_lst_0001]] to provide a comprehensive cross-reference.

For parallel systems, piping within the first system **shall** be assigned the standard serial numbers. Piping in any subsequent parallel systems **shall** be assigned unused serial numbers as available, with a cross-reference maintained in the project line list to the standard serial numbers.

**Responsibilities**
The responsible entity **shall** develop and maintain standard identification numbers and codes for plant lines specific to their assigned technologies. This includes assigning identical sequence numbers to utility lines and using identical codes for common services (e.g., utilities, commodity chemicals) as reflected in the Standard Piping and Instrument Diagrams.

**Benefits of the Numbering System**
The implementation of this standard numbering system offers several key advantages:
*   **Consistency:** Provides a consistent numbering system for future estimating and effective referencing in technical service reports.
*   **Design Basis:** Establishes a clear referencing base for design procedures.
*   **Re-use:** Facilitates the re-use of past project designs and documentation.
*   **Scalability:** The structure is designed to accommodate future growth through the insertion of new numbers.
*   **Standardization:** Line numbers are intended to remain identical for similar services across different projects. For example, a caustic distribution header **should** retain the same number regardless of whether it is supplied from a head tank or a pump tank and pump.
