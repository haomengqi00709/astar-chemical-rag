# Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) Procedure Overview

The 0-PRC-8007 procedure outlines the comprehensive process for the systemic analysis of hazards during job or task execution. The primary goal of this procedure is to establish methods and specify means to eliminate or minimize all identified hazards, thereby preventing injury, illness, or loss.

A **Hazard** is defined as an existing condition, or a condition created during an activity, that if left uncontrolled, can result in injury, illness, or loss. [[0-PRC-8007_job_hazard_analysis]] is the systematic process of analyzing job tasks to identify these hazards before they occur. It focuses on the intricate relationship between the worker, the task, the tools, and the environment. The ultimate purpose of JHA is to identify and implement the best methods, tools, and safety equipment to remove the risk of injury or environmental damage.

This procedure mandates the application of JHA in all medium to extreme risk tasks across all operational areas. Task selection guidelines are detailed in step 1 of section 6.2. JHA must be applied at the worksite during the job planning phase. For efficiency, jobs with similar characteristics and identical task steps may be grouped together for the purpose of performing a JHA.

Projects shall continuously emphasize the concepts, principles of application, and importance of JHA in all Safety topics and meetings. Each project **must** maintain a record of all JHA reports.

A Job Hazard Analysis is broken down into 7 major steps, which are detailed further in [[0-PRC-8007_job_hazard_analysis_process_steps]].
