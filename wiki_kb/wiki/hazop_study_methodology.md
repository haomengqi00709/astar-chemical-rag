# HazOp Study Methodology

The Hazard and Operability (HazOp) Study employs the "Guide Word" method to systematically identify potential deviations from design intent. This rigorous process is led by a trained study leader and aims to comprehensively explore the consequences of these deviations.

**Objectives of the HazOp Study:**
*   Identify all possible deviations from the design intent.
*   Explore the consequences of these identified deviations.
*   Pinpoint potential hazards within the plant design.
*   Uncover operability problems that could arise during plant operation.

The study is conducted by reviewing design intent statements and detailed information pertaining to discrete parts of the plant. These discrete parts are specifically designated on [[piping_and_instrumentation_drawings]]. The ultimate goal of the HazOp Study is to facilitate the development of solutions that either remove or contain identified problems, thereby ensuring a competent and safe design.

For more information on the "Guide Word" method, refer to [[hazop_guideword_deviations]].

The study is conducted on the basis of reviewing design intent statements.
