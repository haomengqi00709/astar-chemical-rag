# Hazardous Area Extent and Documentation

Defining the physical extent of a hazardous area is a critical step in ensuring appropriate electrical installations and safety measures. This process relies on comprehensive documentation and sound engineering judgment.

**Required Documentation for Defining Extent:**
To accurately define the extent of a hazardous area, the following documentation is required:
*   P&ID drawings (Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams).
*   Plant layout drawings, which **must** show the locations of all possible sources of hazards, including all piping containing hazardous gases or vapours (preferably in isometric projection). Plant layout documentation for defining hazardous area extent **should** include an attached example.
*   Site layout drawings.
*   Civil / Structural drawings.
*   Drainage system information.

**Determining the Extent:**
The extent of a hazardous area depends on the estimated or calculated distance which a flammable atmosphere is capable of travelling before it disperses to a concentration in air below its lower flammable limit. This assessment considers factors such as the quantity and velocity of escaping material, gas/vapour density, diffusion rate, ventilation, and plant topography, as outlined in [[hazardous_area_classification_principles]].

**Guidance from NFPA 497:**
NFPA 497 (1997) Recommended Practice provides typical examples in diagram form for the extent of hazardous areas in Class I, Division 1 & Division 2 situations in chemical plants. The extent of the classified location **may be** defined by applying, with sound engineering judgement, the distances recommended in the NFPA 497 (1997) diagrams.

**Final Step: Area Classification Drawing:**
The last step in the classification process is to produce an area classification drawing. This drawing **must** clearly show the different classified areas and their extent. If the complexity of the plant or process warrants it, a written document to supplement the area classification drawing **should be** prepared.

For definitions of Class I, Division 1, and Division 2 locations, refer to [[hazardous_location_classification_types]].
