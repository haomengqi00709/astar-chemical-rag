# Hazardous Area Classification - Chloralkali, NaClO3, ClO2 Plants

In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics plants, such as Chloralkali (NaOH), Sodium Chlorate (NaClO3), or Chlorine Dioxide (ClO2) facilities, specific considerations apply to hazardous area classification due to the presence of hydrogen and other flammable materials.

**Hydrogen-Related Hazards**
Hydrogen containing water vapour is produced in the electrolytic cells. In NaClO3 and ClO2 plants, small amounts of oxygen and chlorine are also produced. These gases are piped to their final destination (e.g., boiler, HCl unit) or vented to the atmosphere.

Based on the [[iec_79_10_hazardous_area_classification_standard|IEC publication # 79-10]] and Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' previous experience, these plants are generally considered non-hazardous. Hydrogen gas is contained (i.e., non-classified) in the vessels and transported in seamless welded pipes, except for specific areas where hydrogen is or may be present.

**Specific Areas of Concern for Hydrogen:**
*   **Hydrogen Sample Points:** In Chloralkali, NaClO3, and ClO2 Plants, these **will normally** be isolated with either blind flanges or double valves.
*   **Seal Leg Drains (Chloralkali Plants):** These **will** be sealed with water.
*   **Electrolyzer Cells Connecting Flanges:** These are areas where hydrogen is or may be present. The electrolyzer body is **not** included as an area where hydrogen is or may be present.
*   **PRV Valve on Catholyte Tank (Chloralkali Plants):** This valve **will** be piped outside the building, **will** be normally closed, and functions like a rupture disk.
*   **Main Vent Stack (Chloralkali, NaClO3, and ClO2 Plants):** This is an area where hydrogen **will** be released continuously. It is piped outside and classified as [[hazardous_area_classification_definitions_iec_79_10|Zone 1]] for a 1.5m (5’-0”) radius sphere around it. These vents **must** be located outdoors.
*   **Tail Gas Scrubber Vent (if an HCl unit is attached, Chloralkali, NaClO3, and ClO2 Plants):** This is an area where hydrogen is or may be present. It is piped outside and classified as [[hazardous_area_classification_definitions_iec_79_10|Zone 1]] for a 1.5m (5’-0”) radius sphere around it. These vents **must** be located outdoors.
*   **Hydrogen Vent after Hydrogen Scrubber (NaClO3 and ClO2 Plants):** This is an area where hydrogen **will** be released continuously. It is piped outside and classified as [[hazardous_area_classification_definitions_iec_79_10|Zone 1]] for a 1.5m (5’-0”) radius sphere around it. These vents **must** be located outdoors.
*   **Rupture Disks:** These **will normally** be intact.
*   **Spark Producing Devices:** No spark producing device **will** be located closer than one (1) metre (3’-0”) from any hydrogen pipe flange. No spark producing in-line devices **will** be used.

**Ventilation and Electrical Equipment:**
*   **Lighting on Degasifier Floor (Chlorate Plant):** Lighting **should** be vapour-tight and installed with the top of the fixture 300 mm min. (1’-0”) spacing from the roof.
*   **Boilers, HCl Units, and Immediate Areas:** These are classified as non-hazardous with respect to electrical equipment and instruments.
*   **Building Ventilation:** Good natural building ventilation **must** be ensured at all times for Chlorate or Chlor-Alkali cell ventilation. In the case of an enclosed building, the wall louvres **must** be open at the same time as cell ventilation. If roof vents can be closed, an interlock to the process **should** be provided to prevent the Transformer / Rectifier from operating.

**Chlorate Dust Hazards:**
In chlorate plants, chlorate crystal and dust are flammable. In areas where chlorate dust is present, the equipment **must** be dust-tight.

**Zone 2 Classification:**
The area from the degasifier and reactor rupture disc elevations to the building roof is classified as [[hazardous_area_classification_definitions_iec_79_10|Zone 2]]. Except for lighting fixtures, no electrical equipment **will** be located in Zone 2 areas (from degasifier and reactor rupture disc elevations to building roof). In the event of a rupture disc blowing and releasing some hydrogen gas, the plant **will** automatically shut down, and the hydrogen system **will** be purged with inert nitrogen to atmosphere.
