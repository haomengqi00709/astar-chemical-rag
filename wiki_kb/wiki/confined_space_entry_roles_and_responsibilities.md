# Confined Space Entry: Roles and Responsibilities

Effective management of confined space entry requires clear roles and responsibilities for all involved personnel. This ensures that all safety protocols are strictly adhered to, and potential hazards are mitigated.

**Commissioning HSE Manager:**
*   **Must** administer the Confined Space Entry Permit system.
*   **Must** issue permits or, when applicable, obtain permits from the client issuing authority.
*   **Must** establish and maintain a program that adequately informs Chemetics’ personnel of the existence, purpose, and procedures of the Confined Space Entry system.
*   **Must** sign a valid permit for this program, along with the Entry Supervisor.
*   **Must** evaluate and ensure that any client or third-party rescue service has the capability to respond in a timely manner and perform the rescue for the specific type of confined space work.
*   **Must** prepare a written [[0-REP-8007b_confined_space_hazard_assessment_form|Hazard Assessment]] for the space of interest before entry.
*   Is responsible for determining the environmental condition inside the vessel.
*   Is responsible for prescribing the necessary precautions required for safe vessel entry.
*   **Must** define the maximum number of men permitted to work within a vessel at any one time for every particular case.
*   **Must** be consulted by supervisors contemplating entry of personnel into vessels.
*   **Must** approve any deviations from the safety requirements specified in these instructions.

**Commissioning Manager:**
*   **Shall** take over the administration of the Confined Space Entry system if the Commissioning HSE Manager is temporarily away from site.

**Entry Supervisor:**
*   **Must** know hazard/possible hazardous condition(s) and possible behavioral effects of exposure.
*   **Must** review Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) of chemicals found in the process.
*   **Must** request and obtain an entry permit from the Commissioning HSE Manager.
*   **Must** complete a pre-entry check prior to the commencement of entry work.
*   **Must** ensure that atmosphere has been tested to determine the oxygen level and concentration of flammable vapors, gases, and toxic contaminants.
*   **Must** ensure that isolation/lock, tag, try procedures have been accomplished.
*   **Must** restrict entry into a confined space where flammable vapor or gas is greater than 10 percent of the Lower Explosive Level (LEL).
*   **Must** restrict entry into a confined space where toxic values are above the threshold value in accordance with OSHA 29 CFR Part 1910; Subpart Z - Toxic and Hazardous Substances, including supplements of General Safety and Health Regulations.
*   **Must** restrict entry into a confined space where atmospheric oxygen concentration is below 19.5 percent or greater than 23.5 percent.
*   **Must** terminate entry upon completion of work, expiration of permit, or if a condition arises in or near the permit space that places occupants at risk.
*   **Must** ensure permits are reissued or updated before authorizing re-entry into the confined space.
*   **Must** authorize entrants to enter space with respect to work scope and assist in prevention of entrance by unauthorized persons into confined space.
*   **Must** verify all authorized entrants and standby attendants understand company policy, procedures, and the nature of the hazards of entry into the confined space.
*   **Must** ensure that all personnel are confined space trained and trained in the use of retrieval equipment and evacuation and rescue procedures.
*   **Must** assure signage and/or tape-off of openings when not in use.

**Authorized Entrants:**
*   **Shall** understand company policy, procedures, and the nature of the hazards of entry into confined spaces.
*   **Must** obtain authorization from the Entry Supervisor to assure that he/she is an Authorized Entrant and to obtain authorization to enter a confined space.
*   **Must** know the hazards that may be encountered during entry and know the mode, signs or symptoms, and consequences of any potential exposure.
*   **Must** ensure that an entry permit has been issued for the confined space and that all requirements of the permit have been met.
*   **Must** know how to use provided retrieval equipment.
*   **Must** know procedures for communication with the attendants.

**Attendants (Standby Persons):**
*   **Shall** understand company policy, procedures, and the nature of the hazards of entry into confined spaces.
*   **Must** know the hazards that may be encountered during entry including information on the mode, signs or symptoms, and consequences of any potential exposure.
*   **Must** maintain continuous count of authorized entrants in the confined space through the use of sign in/out rosters or tracking systems.
*   **Must** collect ID Cards of personnel in Confined Space until their exit.
*   **Must** tend lifelines, keep tools and materials immediately available, and pass materials to entrants in the confined space.
*   **Must** remain outside the confined space to monitor the entrants (through voice or other means of communication), the confined space, and surrounding area for possible change in conditions that may have the potential for increased hazard.
*   **Must** monitor ventilation equipment and atmospheric monitoring equipment, if necessary, to ensure that contaminated air does not enter the confined space.
*   The standby attendant **will never** enter the confined space to perform rescue.
*   The attendant is to summon help (an air horn blast or other alarm) and proceed with rescue efforts from outside the confined space until help arrives.
*   **No entry for rescue will be done** until trained rescue personnel arrive, and person or persons effecting rescue have donned appropriate safety equipment for confined space rescue.
*   **Shall** keep an updated account of how many persons are in the vessel at all times.
*   **Shall** observe the operation from outside the tank while personnel are inside.
*   **Shall** have entry safety equipment available.
*   **Shall** be aware of the condition of personnel within the vessel at all times.
*   **Shall not** leave his position until the vessel is vacated or other suitably attired and certified personnel relieve him.

**"Competent Person" (as defined in the procedure):**
*   It is the responsibility of the designated “Competent Person” to perform the task safety analysis, which may involve using a [[0-REP-8007a_job_hazard_analysis_worksheet|Job Hazard Analysis Worksheet]].
*   If there is an existing confined space entry protocol available that is applicable, that plan **must** be reviewed by the “Competent Person” on the confined space entry team.

**Supervisors (of the area and other work):**
*   **Shall** review this instruction to ensure compliance with all applicable paragraphs.
*   **Shall** coordinate and regulate operations of their organizations to minimize hazards to personnel entering such vessels.
*   **Must** discuss the project in detail with those entering the vessel.
*   **Should** review the hazards of the product or other materials to which they may be exposed.
*   **Must** document this training.
*   **Shall** then review and complete the authorization form jointly with supervisors of all men involved.
*   **Must** ascertain by personal investigation immediately before vessel entry that the entry and incidental work will be safe.
*   **Must** ascertain by personal investigation before signing the authorization that the entry and incidental work will be safe.
*   **Must** reinvestigate the site before the job can be allowed to resume if a break of appreciable duration occurs.
*   Commencement of a new shift **will always** require reinvestigation and endorsement of a new authorization by new shift supervision.
*   **Must** be alerted before and during the job to detect and immediately correct new and different hazards.
*   **Must** stop the work until new hazards are corrected.
