# Piping Stress, Support, and Vibration

**Stress and Movement Analysis**
Piping **must** be analyzed for stress and movement to ensure structural integrity and operational reliability.

**Pipe Supports**
Piping **must** be supported in accordance with Aker Kvaerner Chemetics standard 5-SPC-xxxx: Pipe Support Specification.

**Flexibility and Thermal Expansion**
The use of expansion joints in pressurized lines **must** be minimized. Wherever possible, piping flexibility **must** be utilized to accommodate thermal movement of equipment. Hoses **must** be used to accommodate large thermal and load deflection movements of thermoplastic tanks (e.g., polyethylene).

**Flange Installation**
Flat face flanges **must** be installed against flat face white cast iron valves and equipment.

**Vibration and Other Loads**
In addition to flexibility analysis, piping systems **must** be reviewed for potential issues such as sanding up, loads applied by spilled materials, flow-induced vibration, pressure pulse-induced vibration, equipment-induced vibration, and hydraulic-surge.

**Stress and Movement Analysis**
Piping **must** be analyzed for stress and movement to ensure structural integrity and operational reliability. In addition to flexibility analysis, piping systems will be reviewed for the possibility of sanding up, loads applied by spilled materials, flow induced vibration, pressure pulse induced vibration, equipment induced vibration and hydraulic-surge.

**Pipe Supports**
Piping **must** be supported in accordance with Aker Kvaerner Chemetics standard 5-SPC-xxxx: Pipe Support Specification.

**Flexibility**
Wherever possible, piping flexibility will be used to accommodate thermal movement of equipment. The use of expansion joints in pressurized lines will be minimized. Hoses will be used to accommodate the large thermal and load deflection movements of thermoplastic (i.e. polyethylene) tanks.

**Vibration Prevention**
To avoid flow-induced vibration, check valves will be located a minimum of 6 pipe diameters downstream of the “expander” on pump discharges.
