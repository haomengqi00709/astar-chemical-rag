# Hazardous Area Classification - NCG Plants

In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Non-Condensable Gas (NCG) plants, specific considerations for hazardous area classification apply due to the presence of reduced sulphur gases (TRS).

**TRS Gas Handling:**
Reduced sulphur gases (TRS) in concentrated form are collected, motivated with steam ejectors, and eventually destroyed in incinerators, lime kilns, or boilers. TRS gases are contained in vessels and piping, except for specific areas where TRS gases will or may be present.

**Specific Areas of Concern for TRS Gases:**
*   **NCG Sample Points:** These are equipped with a valve and are normally plugged or will have a gauge mounted.
*   **NCG Seal Leg Drains (on sealed sewer systems):** These are normally sealed with water. High points of NCG Seal Leg Drains are vented outside.
*   **NCG Rupture Disks:** These are normally intact. The downstream side of NCG Rupture Disks is vented to the outside of the building.
*   **Pressure/Vacuum Breakers (PVB's):** These will be normally sealed, and their vent lines are located outside of the building.
*   **NCG Vent Stack Operation:** Every time an NCG system shuts down, the vent valve opens, and TRS gases will be present. This NCG vent stack operation during shutdown is considered a normal operation. NCG vent stack(s) are always located outside and high up.

**Classification and Ventilation:**
A list of the gases and the percentages of each will be used for assessing NCG areas. NCG plants are considered non-hazardous, based on [[iec_79_10_hazardous_area_classification_standard|IEC code]] and Aker Kvaerner Chemetics experience, with one exception:
*   The area around the top of the NCG vent stack(s) is classified as [[hazardous_area_classification_definitions_iec_79_10|Zone 1]] for a 1.5 m (5’-0”) radius sphere.

Good natural building ventilation **must** be ensured at all times around the NCG ejector station.
