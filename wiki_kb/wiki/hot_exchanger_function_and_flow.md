# Hot Exchanger Function and Flow

The hot exchanger performs the final stage of preheating the process gas before it enters bed 1 of the converter. Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' standard practice is to utilize an internal hot exchanger, situated within the converter itself. This design choice effectively eliminates the problematic hot duct between the outlet of bed 1 and the shell side of an external exchanger, a section historically prone to frequent failures.

**Tube Side Flow:**
*   Process gas, having been partially preheated in the shell side of the [[cold_exchanger_function_and_flow|cold exchanger]], enters the tube side of the hot exchanger.
*   Within the hot exchanger, the tube side gas flows downwards, where it is heated to the precise strike temperature required for the catalyst in bed 1.
*   After reaching the desired temperature, the hot exchanger tube side gas exits the tubes and proceeds directly through bed 1 of the converter.

**Shell Side Flow:**
*   Gas exiting bed 1 of the converter then enters the shell side of the hot exchanger.
*   The hot exchanger shell side gas flows upwards.
*   This gas is subsequently discharged into bed 2 of the converter.

**Central Bypass Duct:**
*   The hot exchanger is equipped with a central bypass duct. This feature allows a controlled portion of the gas exiting the [[cold_exchanger_function_and_flow|cold exchanger]] to bypass the tube side of the hot exchanger. This bypass mechanism is crucial for precise temperature control, ensuring the gas enters bed 1 at the optimal strike temperature.
