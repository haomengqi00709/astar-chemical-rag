# Carbon Steel Dry Chlorine Piping - Pressure and Leak Testing (5-SPC-0101)

Pressure and leak testing are critical steps to ensure the integrity and safety of carbon steel dry chlorine piping systems.

**Visual Inspection**
Chlorine piping **shall** be visually inspected after cleaning, pressure testing, drying, or leak testing operations. A full visual inspection **must** be performed per [[ASME_b31_3_chemical_plant_and_petroleum_refinery_piping]] paragraph 341.4.1 (a).

**Hydrostatic Testing**
Piping **shall** be hydrostatically tested per Appendix 1 of 5-SPC-0101 and [[ASME_b31_3_chemical_plant_and_petroleum_refinery_piping]]. Test pressures **shall** be obtained from the [[line_list]]. If hydrostatic testing is required, pressure gauges, relief valves, automatic control valves, and other potentially damaged components **must** be removed. Temporary pipe spools or blinds **must** be installed in place of removed components for hydrostatic testing. Disposable gaskets **should** be used where valves, instruments, and inline devices **will** be replaced.

After installation and required testing, the piping system **should** have all valves, instruments, and inline devices removed. Pipe spools or blind flanges **should** be installed in place of removed valves, instruments, and inline devices. Disposable gaskets **should** be used where pipe spools or blind flanges have been installed.

**Pneumatic Leak Testing (Air or Nitrogen)**
A separate and subsequent leak test **will** be required because some components (valves and overpressure protection devices) are not installed at the time of pressure testing.
*   Leak testing **shall** be performed using compressed air or other suitable gas.
*   Leak testing **shall** use a soapy water solution.
*   Leak testing **shall** be in accordance with Appendix 1 of 5-SPC-0101 and [[ASME_b31_3_chemical_plant_and_petroleum_refinery_piping]].
*   For leak testing, pressure **must** be gradually elevated to 1.7 Bar (g) or one-half the test pressure, whichever is lower, per [[ASME_b31_3_chemical_plant_and_petroleum_refinery_piping]] paragraph 345.5.5.
*   A wash bottle with a plastic tube **must** be used to wet all flanges and threaded connections. Presence of bubbles indicates leaking flanges or fittings.
*   A pneumatic test **should** be conducted by pressurizing piping to 110% of design pressure or 10 bar (g) (whichever is lower) and locking in compressed air for 30 minutes. A pressure reduction of no more than 5% of initial pressure is the acceptance criteria for the pneumatic test.
*   After leaks are corrected, the piping system can be insulated as required, except around flanges.

**Chlorine Leak Testing (Ammonia Vapour)**
*   Internal pressure of piping system **should** be reduced to 0.1-0.2 Bar(g).
*   A small volume of chlorine **should** be introduced into the piping system. Sufficient chlorine **must** be used for a test with an ammonia bottle.
*   Pressure for leak testing **should** be progressively increased to 1.7 Bar (g) or 110% design pressure, whichever is lower, using dry inert gas.
*   Each exposed and unexposed flange or threaded connection **should** be checked for chlorine presence (white cloud) with an ammonia bottle. Only ammonia vapour **should** be used to check for leaks; liquid ammonia does not need to be drained onto connections.
*   Masking tape can be used to wrap exposed flanges to improve leak detection, and can be pierced once to provide a single check point.
*   Valves **should** be stroked or modulated as part of the checking procedure if possible.
*   Exposed flanges and fittings can be insulated once all leaks have been corrected.
*   An elevated pressure **should** be maintained in the system prior to going online.
