# Electrical Conductor Identification

Proper identification of individual conductors within both single and multi-conductor cables is essential for electrical safety, installation, and maintenance. All conductors **shall** be identified with a unique number at each end to ensure clear traceability throughout the electrical system.

**Conductor Identification Number Tag Components (E-F):**
Conductor identification number tags are comprised of two primary components:

*   **Component E: Equipment Identification**
    *   This component refers to the identification number of the equipment to which the conductor is connected (e.g., a motor, a control device, or a terminal block).
    *   *Example:* For a conductor connected to a motor identified as "M-101", Component E would be "M-101".

*   **Component F: Terminal Number**
    *   This component indicates the specific terminal number of the motor starter, control device, or terminal strip where the conductor is terminated.
    *   *Example:* If the conductor connects to terminal 'T1' of the motor starter, Component F would be "T1".

**Example Conductor Tag Structure:**
A conductor connected to motor M-101 at terminal T1 of its starter might be tagged as: M-101-T1.

**Rules for Conductor Numbering Continuity:**
*   **Continuous Length:** A conductor of one continuous length, without any splices or intermediate terminations, **shall** carry a single, consistent conductor number throughout its entire run.
*   **Change of Potential:** The conductor number **shall** change whenever there is a change in electrical potential. This is crucial for safety and circuit identification.
*   **Terminal Strip Changes:** Conductor numbers may change on the other side of terminal strips. This typically occurs when there is a change in the associated equipment identification or circuit designation across the terminal strip.
*   **Spliced Conductors:** In specific cases, spliced conductors may carry the same conductor number, provided that the splice occurs between two terminal strips and does not involve a change in potential or equipment identification.

This detailed conductor identification system is an integral part of the overall [[9-PRC-0021_electrical_numbering_and_identification_procedure|electrical numbering and identification procedure]], ensuring clarity and safety in all electrical installations.
