# Hazardous Location Classification Types

Hazardous locations are classified based on the type of combustible material present and the likelihood of an ignitable mixture existing. This procedure primarily focuses on Class I locations, but also briefly defines Class II and Class III.

**Class I Hazardous Locations:**
These are locations in which the combustible material is gas or vapour. Class I locations are further subdivided into divisions:

*   **Division 1 Locations:** An ignitable mixture is likely to be present continuously or intermittently under normal conditions of operation.
*   **Division 2 Locations:** An ignitable mixture is likely to be present only under abnormal operating conditions, such as failure of process equipment or flange leakage.

**Non-Classified Locations:**
Areas can be considered non-classified under specific conditions:
*   **Adequately Ventilated:** Where combustible materials are contained within suitable, well-maintained, closed piping systems.
*   **Not Adequately Ventilated (Specific Conditions):** Provided piping systems are without flanges, valves, fittings, and similar accessories prone to leaks.

**Adequate Ventilation Criteria (NFPA 497, 1997 edition):**
Adequate ventilation is defined as a ventilation rate that affords either:
*   6 air changes per hour, OR
*   1 cubic foot per minute (cfm) per square foot of floor area, OR
*   Other similar criteria.

This ventilation rate **must** prevent the accumulation of significant quantities of vapor-air concentrations from exceeding 25% of the lower flammable limit.

**"Sufficient Quantities" Thresholds:**
"Sufficient quantities" of vapour/air mixtures are defined as concentrations over 25% of the lower flammable limit. However, for specific gases, lower thresholds apply:
*   For Hydrogen: < 1%
*   For NCG (Non-Condensable Gases): < 0.5%

**Other Hazardous Location Classes (Briefly Defined):**
*   **Class II Hazardous Locations:** Those in which the combustible material is dust.
*   **Class III Hazardous Locations:** Those in which the combustible material is easily ignitable fibres or flyings.

For information on how to define the physical extent of these classified areas, refer to [[hazardous_area_extent_and_documentation]].
