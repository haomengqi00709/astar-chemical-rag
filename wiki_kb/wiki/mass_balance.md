# Mass Balance

A mass balance is a fundamental principle in chemical engineering used to account for the conservation of mass within a system. It involves quantifying the total mass of all inputs and outputs, as well as any accumulation or depletion of mass within the system boundaries.

**Purpose**
*   **Process Design**: Essential for designing new processes and equipment, determining required feed rates, and predicting product yields.
*   **Process Optimization**: Used to identify inefficiencies, reduce waste, and improve overall process performance.
*   **Troubleshooting**: Helps diagnose operational problems by comparing actual mass flows with design specifications.
*   **Environmental Compliance**: Crucial for tracking emissions and waste streams to ensure compliance with environmental regulations.

**Key Components**
A typical mass balance equation for a steady-state system (no accumulation) is:

*   **Mass In = Mass Out**

For systems with reactions or accumulation, the equation expands to:

*   **Mass In + Mass Generated = Mass Out + Mass Consumed + Mass Accumulated**
