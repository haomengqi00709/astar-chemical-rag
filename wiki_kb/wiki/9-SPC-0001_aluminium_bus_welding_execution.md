# Aluminium Bus Welding Execution

The Aluminium Bus Welding Standard (9-SPC-0001) specifies detailed requirements for the execution of welding processes to ensure high-quality fabrication.

**Permitted Welding Processes**
The preferred welding methods for Aluminium or its alloys are Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW) and Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW). Manual metallic arc welding process is **not acceptable**.

**Gas Tungsten Arc Welding (GTAW)**
*   The preferred electrode for GTAW is thoriated tungsten as per [[ASME_sfa_5_12_tungsten_arc_welding_electrodes|AWS-A5.12]], Class EWTh-2.
*   Consumable electrodes **shall** fall within filler metal category F21 of [[ASME_section_ix_welding_and_brazing_qualifications|Section IX of the ASME Code]].
*   High frequency (HF) voltage **shall** be used to start the arc.
*   Where necessary, a back-up gas **shall** be used to prevent root pass oxidation and contamination. This back-up gas may be argon, helium, or a mixture of the two.
*   Shielding gas and flow rate **shall** be specified for GTAW.

**Gas Metal Arc Welding (GMAW)**
*   GMAW may be used for butt welds or fillet welds.
*   Wire spools **must** be tagged for easy identification for GMAW.
*   Shielding gas and flow rate **shall** be specified for GMAW.

**Preheat and Interpass Temperature**
Preheat is not normally required. However, when adverse weather conditions cause condensation or wetness, material **shall** be dried prior to welding by heating to a temperature just sufficient to prevent condensation. Preheat may also be necessary to facilitate the elimination of cold starts (poor penetration). Heat input during welding should be kept to a minimum, and the interpass temperature should **not exceed 250°F**.

**Welding Environment and Position**
As far as practical, all welds **shall** be made in the downhand (flat) position. Vertical welds **shall** be made vertical up (uphand). Suitable enclosures **shall** be provided to keep welds and welders free from drafts, rain, or other inclement weather. Welding **shall not** be performed on wet surfaces.

**Arc Strikes and Tungsten Contamination**
Every effort **shall** be made to confine arc strikes to the surface on which weld metal is to be deposited. If an arc strike occurs outside the weld area, the spot **will require** to be ground, then [[9-SPC-0001_aluminium_bus_welding_inspection_and_nde|PT examined per Para. 10.3]], and repaired if necessary. Burn marks from grounding clamps **shall** be treated as arc strikes.

If a tungsten electrode accidentally makes contact with the work, the section of weld and the tip of the electrode should be examined. Where tungsten loss has occurred, the tungsten pick-up in the weld **shall** be ground out, and the electrode **shall** be reground to the correct profile before re-starting.

**Weld Run Management**
Each run of weld metal **shall** be completely fused and thoroughly cleaned of all slag and foreign matter before the next run is deposited. On re-starting, care **shall** be taken to ensure proper fusion with the weld metal previously deposited. The arc **shall** be broken gradually to eliminate craters and cracks. Peening of weld runs and hammering of finished welds are **not permitted**.

**Permitted Welding Processes**
The manual metallic arc welding process is not acceptable.

**GTAW Specific Requirements**
The preferred electrode for GTAW is thoriated tungsten as per AWS-A5.12, Class EWTh-2. Shielding gas and flow rate **shall** be specified for GTAW.

**GMAW Specific Requirements**
GMAW may be used for butt welds or fillet welds. Fabricator **must** provide satisfactory evidence of continuous shop use of GMAW process in fabrication of Aluminum or its alloys. Wire spools **must** be tagged for easy identification for GMAW. Shielding gas and flow rate **shall** be specified for GMAW.

**General Welding Execution**
Consumable electrodes **shall** fall within filler metal category F21 of Section IX of the ASME Code. High frequency (HF) voltage **shall** be used to start the arc. Where necessary, a back-up gas **shall** be used to prevent root pass oxidation and contamination. Back-up gas may be argon, helium, or a mixture of the two.

**Preheat and Interpass Temperature**
Preheat is not normally required. When adverse weather conditions cause condensation or wetness, material **shall** be dried prior to welding by heating to a temperature just sufficient to prevent condensation. Preheat may be necessary to facilitate elimination of cold starts (poor penetration). Heat input during welding should be kept to a minimum. Interpass temperature should not exceed 250F.

**Welding Position and Environment**
As far as practical, all welds **shall** be made in the downhand (flat) position. Vertical welds **shall** be made vertical up (uphand). Suitable enclosures **shall** be provided to keep welds and welders free from drafts, rain or other inclement weather. Welding **shall not** be performed on wet surfaces.

**Joint Preparation and Fit-up**
Misalignment of welding edges at butt joints **shall** be held to a minimum. Fit-up **must** be accurate and checked frequently to minimize possible distortion and weld penetration difficulties. Considerable care is essential in joint preparation. Bevel angles and thicknesses **shall** be accurate and true to facilitate good welding.

**Holding and Tack Welding**
Parts to be welded **shall** be securely held in position by mechanical means, welded-on bridge pieces or by tack welding to maintain specified alignment and gap. Tack welds may form part of completed weld if complete fusion is effected at the root and welding procedure is same as for remainder of root run. Sound tack welds **must** be thoroughly fused into the root run of the main weld. Where thinning of tack weld ends is necessary, tack welds should preferably be at least one inch long. Cracked and unsound tack welds **shall** be cut out prior to welding.

**Back-up Rings and Inserts**
Use of permanent back-up rings or strips or consumable joint inserts is not permitted unless specifically approved in writing by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' Q.A. and Materials Engineering.

**Arc Strikes and Run-off Pads**
Every effort **shall** be made to confine arc strikes to the surface on which weld metal is to be deposited. If an arc strike occurs outside the weld area, the spot will require to be ground, PT examined per Para. 10.3, and repaired if necessary. Burn marks from grounding clamps **shall** be treated as arc strikes. Run-off pad material **shall** be of the same material designation as the parent material.

**Weld Runs and Cleaning**
Each run of weld metal **shall** be thoroughly cleaned of all slag and foreign matter before the next run is deposited. On re-starting, care **shall** be taken to ensure proper fusion with the weld metal previously deposited. The Arc **shall** be broken gradually to eliminate craters and cracks.

**Tungsten Contamination**
If a tungsten electrode accidentally makes contact with the work, the section of weld and the tip of the electrode should be examined. Where tungsten loss has occurred, the tungsten pick-up in the weld **shall** be ground out, and the electrode **shall** be reground to the correct profile before re-starting.

**Post-Weld Treatment Restrictions**
Peening of weld runs is not permitted. Hammering of finished welds is not permitted.

**Butt Weld Execution**
Welding of butt joints **shall** alternate from both sides in order to minimize distortion. Back gouging of root passes of butt welds **shall** be performed whenever possible. Grinding or gouging **shall** be carried out in such a manner as not to reduce the adjacent base metal thickness.

**Interpass Cleaning**
Welds **shall** be thoroughly cleaned between passes by wire brushing. Power brushing is preferred for cleaning welds between passes.
