# Hazardous Area Classification - Process and Extent

The process of classifying hazardous areas involves several steps, from initial assessment to determining the extent of classified locations. This procedure is crucial for ensuring the safe design and operation of plants handling flammable materials.

**Preliminary Assessment**
The preliminary step in classifying a plant is to determine the existence of a flammable atmosphere. This requires a thorough understanding of the materials and processes involved. When assessing the area classification of a plant, the following information **must** be made available:
*   Properties and process conditions of the materials to be handled, stored, processed, or evolved.
*   Volumes and dispositions of these materials.
*   Possibilities of escape of flammable material.

The process engineer **must** identify the equipment that may contain flammable gases, vapours, and/or liquids. All possible sources of a leak to atmosphere **must** be identified once equipment and piping containing flammable gases, vapours, and/or liquids have been identified. Each source of a leak **must** be reviewed and graded as continuous, primary, or secondary, and its release rate **must** be studied and classified.

**Required Documentation for Review**
The review and classification of the plant requires input from process, mechanical, and electrical engineering personnel familiar with the plant and equipment layout. The following drawings and documentation **must** be reviewed to determine the area classification of new plant and new equipment in an existing plant:
*   [[piping_and_instrumentation_drawings|P&ID drawings]]
*   Plant layout showing locations of all possible sources of hazards (preferably in isometric projection), including all piping containing hazardous gases or vapours.
*   Plant site layout.
*   [[civil_works_design_criteria_2_prc_0001|Civil / Structural drawings]].
*   Drainage system information.
*   Owner’s existing plant area classification.
*   Plant site prevailing wind direction and mean speed.
*   Owner’s plant insurance company guidelines and requirements.
*   Studies done by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for similar existing plants.

**Determining the Extent of Hazardous Areas**
The extent of a hazardous area depends on the estimated or calculated distance a flammable atmosphere is capable of travelling before it disperses to a concentration in air below its [[hazardous_area_classification_definitions_iec_79_10|Lower Explosive Limit (LEL)]]. Dispersal is influenced by:
*   The quantity escaping.
*   The velocity of escape.
*   The gas or vapour density and diffusion rate.
*   The ventilation air currents.
*   The topographical features of the plant involved.

For gases or vapours lighter than air, an escape at low velocity will disperse fairly rapidly upwards in a well-ventilated situation. Conversely, with gases or vapours heavier than air, an escape at low velocity will tend to flow downward and may travel long distances over the ground before being safely dispersed by atmospheric diffusion. The extent of the classified location may be defined by applying, with sound engineering judgement, the distances recommended in the diagrams shown in the [[iec_79_10_hazardous_area_classification_standard|IEC publication # 79–10]], clause # 4, titled, ‘Area classification procedure’.

**Impact of New Equipment and Re-classification**
When new equipment and/or piping is added to an existing plant, the area classification of the existing plant **must** be reviewed. New equipment and/or piping may change the existing plant area classification from non-hazardous to a hazardous area or alter the existing zone within a hazardous area.

If there are any changes to the existing plant area classification and the affected areas have been re-classified, then the electrical and instrumentation devices and equipment **must** be reviewed as to their suitability in the new area classification. Any devices and equipment found not suitable for the new area classification **must** be replaced with approved units. In locations where the costs are excessive to replace the electrical and instrumentation devices and equipment with approved units, or where the devices and equipment are not manufactured to meet the new area classification, then the mechanical layout and selection of equipment **should** be reviewed, and action taken as necessary.
