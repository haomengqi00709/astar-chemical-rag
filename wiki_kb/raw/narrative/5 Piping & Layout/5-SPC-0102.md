# 5-SPC-0102 — Specification


[TABLE: 5-SPC-0102_table_1]


## Purpose
This Standard defines the requirements for leak testing and flushing of piping systems prior to commissioning.  It is important to note that leak testing is only intended to demonstrate that a system is suitable for operation at the design pressure; it does not relieve the contractor of the responsibility to provide quality materials, good workmanship and effective quality control.
The test requirements listed and referenced in this Standard are the minimum acceptable requirements.  If additional or different tests are deemed necessary, the contractor will advise Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.
Reference Specifications
The referenced paragraphs of the following specifications form an integral part of this Standard.
ASME B31.1 - 1989 "Power Piping" hereinafter referred to as B31.1.
ASME/ANSI B31.3 - 1987 and 1988 Addendum "Chemical Plant and Petroleum Refinery Piping" hereinafter referred to as B31.3.

## Scope
This Standard applies to piping handling all fluids, and to all types of service, as defined in B31.1 para 100.1.1 and B31.3 para 300.1.1 with the following clarifications:
In special cases where the fluid service has been classified as Category D or Category M (as defined below), the special considerations of B31.3 will apply (see also B31.3 Appendix M for a guide to classifying fluid services).
In cases where the fluid service has been classified as "Pulp & Paper", the requirements of this Standard may be relaxed as described in the supplemental MoDo-Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Standard for Leak Testing of Piping, Spec. No. LT1.

## Required Leak Test
The primary steam line from the plant battery limits to the first pressure reducing station will be hydrostatically tested in accordance with B31.1 para 137.4.
Lines handling reduced pressure steam (<15 psig), cooling water, condensate, process water, potable water, instrument air, nitrogen or similar systems handling Category D fluids will be subjected to an initial service leak test in accordance with B31.1 para 137.7 or B31.3 para 345.7.
Unless otherwise specified, lines handling Category M or flammable fluids will be leak tested in accordance with B31.3 para M345.1.  Lines open to atmosphere, such as vents, overflows or drains downstream of the last shutoff valve, need not be tested unless specified.
Lines not designed to support the weight of a test liquid or lines handling fluids which, together with residual water from a hydrostatic test will create a highly corrosive environment, will be subjected to a pneumatic leak test in accordance with B31.3 para 345.5.  Special procedures for preparation and cleaning may also be specified.

## Preparation For Testing
Wherever possible, piping should be tested as systems and in sections as the installation work progresses.  Each line and associated equipment must be prepared for testing and the approval of the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative received prior to application of the test.
All threaded, segmented, fusion welded joints and bolted connections in the system to be tested will be exposed.  Do not paint, insulate or cover a line until after the tests have been accepted.
The piping contractor will provide and install the necessary blinds where lines are installed but not connected to equipment and before the connections to equipment that are not part of the test.  The contractor will remove all blinds used for testing after completion of tests.  All blinds used will be numbered and accounted for at the completion of tests.  In-line valves may be used to isolate lines for testing instead of blinds.
Pumps and other equipment containing pressurized shaft seals will not be pressure tested as part of a system and must be blanked off with removable blinds.  Glass components should be excluded from testing, otherwise they will be fully shielded.
All piping systems which are hydraulically tested will be vented at the high point and the system purged of air before the test pressure is applied.
Prior to the introduction of water, piping and equipment must be inspected and all dirt, debris and loose material removed.
Whenever possible, lines will be tested with all in-line equipment such as control valves, flow transmitters etc. in place.  Control valves must be placed in the fully open position unless the valve is being used to terminate a section for test.  Branch valves to pressure gauges, differential pressure transmitters and similar branches will be closed to prevent the entry of dirt.  Expansion joints, relief valves and rupture discs should be removed or blanked off for test.  Orifice plates, spray nozzles and similar restrictions should normally be installed after completion of tests.  Each line will be reviewed prior to test with the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative to identify those items which must be removed, blanked off or supported.
With the drains open, water should be flushed through the system to be tested at the maximum rate available from the supply (often a hose) until the water leaving the drains appears to be clean.  The drains may then be closed and the system filled for testing.
After completion of testing, the system will be drained at the fastest rate possible through the drain connections and the system inspected for debris and cleaned.  For pump suctions, the line between the pump and the first valve will be dismantled, all debris removed and the temporary startup strainer installed.  For pump discharges, the line between the pump and the first valve will be dismantled and all debris removed.  All blinds used to isolate the system must be removed and accounted for.
After pressure testing has been completed, all equipment removed or disconnected for the purpose of the test will be replaced or reconnected by the Contractor and all the joints made good to the satisfaction of the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative. Pipes which have been tested will be completely drained and vents and drains which have been added for the purpose of testing will be sealed by means approved by the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative.

## Washing/Degreasing
Where de-greasing of lines are required to remove cutting oils and greases and solvent washing is not an option, an alkaline wash based on but not restricted to the one listed below should be used.  Many commercially available cleaning agents are based on this general chemistry and the actual amounts of constiuent components may vary between suppliers.
A 3% solution (in water) of the following chemicals:
38% Sodium Hydroxide
36% Sodium carbonate
12% Sodium metasilicate (as anhydrous chemical)
9% Tetrasodium Pyrophosphate
3% Fatty Acid Esters
2% Ethoxylated Alkylphenol
The first three components are caustics to saponify the oils, the last three are emuslifers and wetting agents.
Washing efficiency will be enhanced by using warm water.  After washing with this, rinse with clean water and air blow the lines dry.

## Pressure Testing
The Erector will ensure that his operator responsible for applying the test pressure is absolutely clear as to the required test pressure, and when pumping up by hand understands that it must not be exceeded.
If it is intended that the final application of pressure be applied by a mechanical pump, or from a higher pressure system, a pressure relief device will be fitted set to ensure that the required test pressure is not exceeded.
Accurate gauges will be provided by the Erector, and submitted to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative for checking, prior to the start of pressure testing, and at any time thereafter.  The gauges will be of a minimum face size of 150mm (6 in) diameter, and ranged to approximately twice the test pressure.
Testing will be carried out in the presence, and to the satisfaction of, the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.
Test pressures may be taken at any point on the system being tested, provided a correction for static head is agreed with the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative.  Where static head is significant, the datum elevation will be the lowest point being tested.
The test pressure will be maintained for a minimum period of 30 minutes or when necessary, for a time sufficient to allow the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative to complete his inspection.
Non-return or check valves will have the test pressure applied simultaneously on both sides of the valves.  On completion of the test, the Erector will ensure the release of the pressure on the downstream side of the valves.
Any leaks will be made good to the satisfaction of the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative.  The pressure test will be re-applied if any repairs have been carried out.
The timing and manner of draining the test fluid will be agreed with the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative.  Care will be taken when draining the test fluid to avoid damage or other items of equipment, and attention will be paid to opening top vents.
When the test has to be maintained for a period of time during which thermal expansion of the test medium might occur, the Erector will ensure that the test system is not subjected to a pressure greater than the specified test pressure.
During frosty or potentially frosty weather, the greatest care will be taken to prevent damage to the pipework by the formation of ice.  Under these circumstances the system will be completely drained before the end of work each day, even if testing of the system is not complete.

## Initial Service Testing
Where an initial service test has been specified for a system or line, the test will be applied in accordance with ANSI B31.1, Section 137.7, with the following additions.
Preparation for the initial service test will be the same as for a hydrostatic test.
Prior to the application of the service pressure for liquid services, each line will be flushed by opening drains or removing pipe sections near the end of each run, and allowing the liquid to flush at as high a rate as possible for approximately 10 minutes or longer until the discharge appears clear.  The flow will then be stopped and the line closed.  The line will then be slowly filled while venting air and pressure slowly applied and inspected for leaks.
Prior to the application of the service pressure for vapour (low pressure steam) air and nitrogen, each line will be blown free of dirt and debris by opening connections near the end of each run and allowing the gas or vapour to blow at as high a rate as is possible without exceeding 25 psig for approximately 10 minutes or longer until the discharge appears clear.  The flow will then be stopped and the line closed.  The line should then be slowly pressurized in stages while inspecting for leaks.

## Preparation For Water Batch Flushing
Prior to water batch flushing, the plant or section of the plant must be prepared to the satisfaction of Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.  Items to be reviewed include:
Completion of pressure testing.
Removal of all blinds and reinstallation of equipment removed for tests.
Inspection of the pipe support system.
Installation of all temporary strainers.
Equipment checked and cleaned of debris.
Control valve functions verified.
Adequate provision of seal fluid pressure and flow.

## Records
Records will be made for each piping system in accordance with B31.3 para 346 and para 345.2.7.
