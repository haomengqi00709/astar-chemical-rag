# 5-SPC-0101 — Specification


[TABLE: 5-SPC-0101_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this Engineering Standard is to define the special requirements for the installation and testing of carbon steel piping used for dry chlorine service and to present a guide for the preparation of the piping system for operation.

## Scope
This Standard applies when referenced on purchase orders, drawings or other documents.
Piping shall satisfy the requirements of ANSI B31.3 where applicable.  Cases of conflict or doubt between the requirements of Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' drawings and Standards and these codes shall be referred to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' engineer for interpretation.

## General
Dry chlorine is considered to be a toxic irritant, generally non-flammable which can exist both as a liquid or a gas.  When released into the environment, chorine gas in low concentrations is a disagreeable irritant; this inbuilt warning helps considerably in preventing people from being seriously harmed while unaware they are exposed to significant danger.
Serious incidents can occur if significant quantities of chlorine gas is released to the environment.  The potential source of the most serious incidents leading to the release of significant quantities of chlorine is from liquid stored in bulk by failure of the vessel holding the liquid chlorine or by failure of the pipe work connected to the vessel.
During normal plant operation, some trivial escapes of chlorine will occur such as making or breaking connections, inserting isolation slip plates for maintenance or the relief of liquid pressure on flanged joints in certain classes of pipe work. These trivial releases are not the result of equipment or pipe work failure.
Safety in any system is affected by many factors including design, layout, materials, quality of workmanship and maintenance and operational practices. Flushing, blowing, cleaning and drying are intended to prepare the system for operation.  Inspection, including pressure and seal testing is intended to demonstrate that the system is suitable for operation under the design conditions.

## Drawings
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide the piping erector with a detailed piping isometric for each line.  These isometrics will show dimensions, spool numbers, complete bills of material and pipe support references.
(Occasionally, for small diameter field run piping usually in yard service, a piping arrangement drawing will be substituted for an isometric drawing).
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide equipment layout drawings, piping plans and plant system isometric drawings.  These drawings are intended to show the general arrangement of the piping system.  Dimensional information shown on the piping isometric drawing shall govern in cases of conflict.
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide a piping and instrumentation drawing (P & I's) and line Piping list index listing each line together with the appropriate references for pipe specification, test pressure, insulation requirements and painting requirements.

## Materials
All materials received at the plant site shall be stored in a manner to prevent damage and facilitate identification.
The erector shall use an effective system to identify different materials and components and to prevent substitution.  This is particularly important when dealing with Low Temperature Carbon Steel piping.
The erector shall institute effective practices to control the issue of piping materials from storage and to recover components issued to the work site which are not utilized for the work in hand.

## Installation
Piping shall be assembled and erected so that a minimum of unintended cold springing and cold forcing occurs.  In the case where designed layout dimensions differ from field dimensions to such an extent that even after using the field trim allowances cold springing or cold forcing would be required, the erector shall make field modifications after receiving instructions and approval from the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.
Piping shall be installed to permit free expansion and contraction except where the drawings specifically indicate an anchor or guide.
The standard tolerance for pipe installation is ± 1/2 inch, unless approved by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.
Where a slope is indicated without dimensional values, it is intended that the line be generally horizontal, without visible sags or reverse slopes, thus aiding drainage.
Pipe supports, hangers, guides, etc. wherever possible should be installed before the piping is erected.  Temporary support shall be used where required.
The pipe erector shall ensure that no debris, weld rod stubs, paper and plastic products or any other material remains in any piping system.  Each line shall be inspected and cleaned before any closing joint is assembled. Temporary covers shall be installed over open connections when the work is not being immediately continued.
All bolts shall be tightened with suitable wrenches only.  Hammering and bumping shall not be permitted.  In tightening joints, care shall be taken to ensure uniform pressure and avoid overstressing bolts or dishing flanges. Gaskets shall be carefully centred on the flange before tightening.
Bolt lengths shall be approximately 1/4 inch past the nut after final tightening of the assembled joint.  In no case shall bolt projection less than one exposed complete thread be accepted.
To facilitate subsequent pressure testing, degreasing and drying operation, "dummy" sections shall be installed at selected locations for valves and other items as shown on the piping isometric drawings.  These temporary items shall be removed and replaced with the final item at a time indicated in Appendix 1.
The pipe erector should anticipate and shall install some additional pipe supports, not shown on the drawings which may be required as identified by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative, particularly to accept loads during hydrostatic testing.

## Inspection
Dry liquid or gaseous chlorine piping systems installed within the plant shall be subjected to 20 percent radiography of all circumferential butt welds, fabricated laps and branch connections in accordance with ANSI/ASME B31.3 para 341.4.  This shall be interpreted to 100 percent radiography requirements of ANSI/ASME B31.3 Table 341.3.2A.
Where the metal temperature is less than -29 C (-20F) Charpy testing of piping & welds shall be necessary.
Chlorine piping shall be visually inspected after cleaning, pressure testing, drying or leak testing operations.

## Pressure Testing
Pressure testing is intended to demonstrate that the components of a piping system are suitable for operation at the design pressure.  Pressure testing is not a substitute for quality material and good workmanship as assured by quality control and inspection of the work in progress.
Because some components (such as valves and overpressure protection devices) are not installed at the time of pressure testing, a separate and subsequent leak test will be required.
Piping shall be hydrostatically tested per Appendix 1 and ANSI/ASME B31.3 with test pressures obtained from the line list.  Leak testing shall be performed using compressed air or other suitable gas using a soapy water solution all in accordance with Appendix 1 and ANSI/ASME B31.3.

## Preparation for Operation
Following the successful completion of the pressure testing, cleaning, drying and leak testing operations, the systems shall be prepared for operation. APPENDIX 1: Cleaning & Testing of Piping - Dry Chlorine Service
Preamble
This document is primarily to be used as an aid for the first installation of the Carbon steel chlorine piping but it should also be reviewed and used by any maintenance personnel working with the Chlorine piping in the future.
Carbon Steel piping for dry (H2O free) chlorine gas and liquid must be cleaned before use, because chlorine can react violently with cutting oil, grease, and other foreign materials. Cleaning of the Carbon Steel piping must be followed by Drying of the piping to ensure that any moisture introduced into the piping is removed prior to Chlorine entering the piping. Chlorine will dissolve in water to form an aggressive acid which will cause corrosion and failure of welds, valves, hoses and fittings and piping.
Cleaning of the piping must not be done with alcohol’s, hydrocarbons or other compounds that will react with chlorine.
As the Carbon steel pipe will be supplied to the job site in random lengths with loose fittings the intent of the Cleaning procedures identified here will be a two stage cleaning system, the first stage to take place during pipe spooling and the second during installation.
Careful storage of any pipe spools, not yet installed in the piping system, must be observed. All pipe stubs should be covered with plastic sheets to minimize ingression of moisture and dirt/debris.  All pipes, fittings and inline items should be stored in a covered dry area prior to the installation.
Removal of all valves, inline items, instruments etc. from installed pipe prior to any cleaning procedure can not be repeated too strongly. Mill scale, rust, debris loosened during cleaning and flushed from the piping can do irreparable damage or can become lodged in any inline devices which are not removed prior to cleaning of the pipes.
Careful storage of all valves, inline fittings, instruments before installation in and after removal from the Chlorine piping must be observed. All vapour tight packaging and internal drying aids, supplied by the vendor, must be left in place until the item is to be installed. Valve bodies should be enclosed in plastic bags, diaphragm transmitter faces should be covered with plastic sheets etc. In general all attempts to keep the inline items as clean and moisture free as possible should be followed.
The chlorine dry-air padding system and the steam supply system must be completed and operating before the piping can be steamed and dried.
A dewpoint analyzer should be available prior to any inline cleaning and drying. This will ensure a correct measurement of trace water levels (-40 C dewpoint).
The procedures described for cleaning of Dry Chlorine Piping do not necessarily apply to cleaning of Tanks, Heat Exchangers or other items of Equipment associated with the Dry Chlorine system.

Dry Chlorine Piping Cleaning Procedure
Instruments, Valves, Inline Items
Upon receipt of Instruments, Valves or other Inline items to be used in the "Dry Chlorine" piping at the jobsite ensure that all protective packaging supplied by the Vendor is maintained and not discarded. In most cases the Vendor will have been advised by the Designer that the item will be used in a "Dry Chlorine" service. Extra precautions are taken to ensure moisture and other contaminants are prevented from contacting the item. Removal of the protective packaging is not only unnecessary but can lead to ingress of moisture into the equipment before it is installed in the piping system.
Instruments, Valves or other Inline items which are received without protective packaging will need to be cleaned and dried prior to installation in the piping. All internal surfaces of the items which come in contact with the "Dry Chlorine " will need to be cleaned and dried so all items will need to be thoroughly disassembled.
Cleaning should be done using Trichloroethane.
Drying should be done in an oven, and then the items should be reassembled while hot and stored in vapour tight packages with internal drying aids (desiccant bags).
The components requiring grease must be treated with a grease which is compatible with chlorine (chloro-fluorinated grease). No other grease or lubricant is acceptable for use in Dry Chlorine service.
Instruments which require bench calibration should be calibrated prior to drying and packaging in an environment that is as clean as possible. All attempts should be made to minimize exposure to oils, greases, lubricants.
All items to be installed in "Dry Chlorine" piping should be warehoused in an enclosed, dry, storage area. Immediately prior to use in the piping system the item should be visually checked again to confirm the absence of moisture or other contaminants.
Random Length Piping Supply
Upon receipt of piping ,fittings at the work site ensure that all pipe stubs ,flanges, exposed process surfaces etc. are covered with a moisture resistant material (ie plastic). Storage in a dry covered area must be provided.
Prior to the installation of any pipe or fitting into a piping system the protective coverings should be removed and the pipe or fitting should be visually inspected. All oil, grease or movable dirt should be cleaned with a dry rag. If this procedure is insufficient sand blasting is recommended. Cleaning using a solvent such as trichloroethane is then required. A "bath" can be made on site by blocking off a 25 foot (8 meter) section of trench. The blocked off section of trench can be lined with FRP and then filled with solvent. Piping sections can be dipped in the "bath" and then allowed to air dry.
The intent is to remove as much loose contaminants from the pipe as possible (both external and internal) prior to pipe installation.
Dry and solvent cleaned pipe can then be installed and welded. Care should be taken to ensure any grease used for cutting and welding or burrs and shavings are not left inside the pipe.
After installation of each pipe section, complete with temporary pipe spools in place of valves and other inline items, air cleaning is required to dislodge and remove any metal burrs or shavings. The air supply must be oil free to avoid contamination of the piping. Sections of pipe to be cleaned must be kept relatively short to ensure dislodged material can be swept out. High air flowrates (velocities) will be required.
Hydraulic testing, as required, should be done at this point.
When hydrostatic testing of piping systems is required, ensure that all pressure gauges, relief valves, automatic control valves and any other components which may be damaged are removed and temporary pipe spools or blinds are installed in the appropriate location. Disposable gaskets should be used where valves, instruments and inline devices will need to be replaced.
When the piping system is installed and all required testing is finished, the piping system should be completed with all valves, instruments and inline devices removed and pipe spools or blind flanges installed in their place. Disposable gaskets should be used where pipe spools or blind flanges have been installed. Steam cleaning of all pipes must be started immediately after Hydrostatic testing is complete, to ensure that moisture does not remain in the pipes for a prolonged period of time.
Steam cleaning of the piping will remove oil, grease and loose scale. The cleaning of the piping system is to be carried out using low pressure steam. Condensate must be able to drain from each pipe section. Dead ended sections must be opened to permit flow of steam.
Steam cleaning of each main pipe section should be continued for at least 24 hours or until a white target located at the outlet of the pipe section does not show signs of rust particles or millscale.
Care must be taken during the steam cleaning process as uninsulated pipes will have high surface temperatures and because the noise associated with steam cleaning will be excessive.
Dry, clean air (padding air) must be blown through the hot pipe immediately after steaming to remove any moisture. Heating of the dry air to 93o C prior to entry into the chlorine piping will aid considerably in the drying process. The dry air flow rate should be started at high volume rates to sweep moisture out of the system and then at reduced flowrates to allow equilibration between the exiting gas and the moisture remaining in the piping.
All pre-spooled pipe sections that have been kept in dry storage should now be installed complete with required operational gaskets.
All valves, instruments and inline devices should be installed complete with required operational gaskets. All temporary pipe spools and gaskets should be removed and discarded.
During installation of pre-spooled pipe, valves, instruments, and inline devices complete with proper gaskets a positive flow of dry air should be maintained through the piping system. Dry air should be allowed to bleed from all connections to ensure that there are no dead spots.
When testing the dryness of the exiting air the inlet flow rate should be reduced to an absolute minimum. This will allow adequate time for the dry air to reach equilibrium before a Dew Point Test is taken.
A Dew Point of -40 C must be achieved in the exiting air. The testing of the exit air should be done at a number of exit points to confirm that each branch or section is adequately dry.
All flanges should be tightened and the pressure of the system should be elevated to prevent moisture ingression.
A primer coat of paint should now be applied to all "Dry Chlorine" piping. Details of painting procedures exist in other Aker Kvaerner Chemetics specifications.
Leak testing of the completed piping system and related equipment should be undertaken using air or nitrogen and soapy water applied externally as the fluid to detect the leak. In accordance with ANSI/ASME B31.3 para. 345.5.5, gradually elevate the pressure of the piping system to 1.7 Bar (g) or one-half the test pressure whichever is lower.  Use a wash bottle with a plastic tube to wet all flanges and threaded connections. The presence of bubbles will allow determination of leaking flanges or fittings. Also, perform a full visual inspection to B31.3 para 341.4.1 (a).
As confirmation that all leaks have been found and eliminated, a pneumatic test of the complete piping system should be conducted by pressurizing the piping to 110% of design pressure or 10 bar (g) whichever is lower, and locking in the compressed air for 30 minutes. A pressure reduction of no more than 5 % of the initial pressure should be used as a criteria for accepting the pneumatic test. After all leaks (as determined with padding air) have been corrected, the piping system can be insulated as required, except around all flanges. As further leaks will likely be detected  at some flange(s), when chlorine is in the piping, insulation of the flanges at this point would be unproductive.
The internal pressure of the piping system should then be reduced to 0.1-0.2 Bar(g) and a small volume of chlorine should be introduced. After checking by utilizing small purge that there is sufficient chlorine to make a test with an ammonia bottle, the pressure should be progressively increased to 1.7 Bar (g) or 110% design pressure, whichever is lower, by addition of dry inert gas. Each exposed and unexposed flange or threaded connection should be checked for the presence of chlorine (white cloud) with an ammonia bottle. Note that only ammonia vapour should be used to check for leaks, liquid ammonia does not need to be drained onto the connections. The use of masking tape to wrap the exposed flanges will improve the ability to detect leaks. The masking tape can be pierced once to provide a single point to check.
If possible valves should be stroked or modulated as part of the checking procedure.
Once all leaks have been corrected the exposed flanges and fittings can be insulated. An elevated pressure should be maintained in the system prior to going online.
Pre-spooled Piping Supply
Subsequent to shop fabrication and non destructive testing as required by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics design criteria, hydraulic testing to local code requirements must be performed on each pipe spool.
When hydraulic testing is completed the pipe spool can be prepared for "Dry Chlorine" service. The first step will be the use of a solvent such as trichloroethane to chemically clean both the exterior and interior of the pipe spool.
After chemical cleaning has occurred the pipespools should be cleaned with steam. Steam cleaning of the piping will remove oil, grease and loose scale. The cleaning of the pipe spools is to be carried out using low pressure steam. Condensate must be able to drain from each pipe section. Dead ended sections must be opened to permit flow of steam.
Steam cleaning of each pipe section should be continued for at least 24 hours or until a white target located at the outlet of the pipe section does not show signs of rust particles or millscale.
Dry, clean air (oil free) must be blown through the hot pipe immediately after steaming to remove any moisture. Heating of the dry air to 93o C prior to entry into the chlorine piping will aid considerably in the drying process. The dry air flow rate should be started at high volume rates to sweep moisture out of the system and then at reduced flowrates to allow equilibration between the exiting gas and the moisture remaining in the piping.
A primer coat of paint should be applied to the pipe spools prior to packaging. Care must be taken not to reintroduce moisture or contaminants into the pipe spools while applying the primer. Use of plugs or covers for the spool ends is advisable.
During crating, use of silica gel bags, shrink wrap coverings or other means of preventing moisture from entering the pipe spools is recommended. The material used for crating must be sufficient to comply with Aker Kvaerner Chemetics requirements for transport protection.
Shipments of pre spooled pipe arriving at the job site should be kept as intact as possible until installation is required. Removal of the protective packaging will only result in ingress of moisture and contaminants into the pipe spools.
All pipe spools should be kept in dry covered storage until needed.

APPENDIX 2: Leak Testing of PVC/FRP Piping - Dry Chlorine Service
The following minimum requirements shall apply for FRP wrapped PVC piping for dry chlorine service. ASME B31.3 and Chlorine Institute requirements for non-metallic piping shall also apply where appropriate.
Piping manufacture shall be in accordance with Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Engineering Standard/Specifications.
Piping for Dry Chlorine Service shall be received on site in an oil and grease free condition with open ends protected by flanges or covers from the ingress of rainwater, debris etc.
As a precaution, the installed piping shall be blown clean of any debris prior to pneumatic testing for leaks.
The design pressure for PVC/FRP piping shall not exceed 5 psig. Properly selected and calibrated low pressure gauges shall be installed during leak testing.
Leak testing of the completed piping system shall be undertaken using air or nitrogen and applying a soapy water solution externally to detect leaks at joint locations. This test shall be in accordance with ASME B31.3 paragraph 345.5.
A preliminary leak test shall be carried out at 3 psig.
The 3 psig pressure shall then be increased to 5.5 psig and held for 10 minutes. This pressure shall then be reduced to the design pressure of 5 psig and the inspection repeated.
Finally, the internal pressure of the piping system shall be reduced to 2 psig and a small volume of chlorine gas introduced. Sufficient quantities of chlorine shall be added to allow inspection of all joints etc externally with ammonia vapour. The presence of a chlorine leak will be evident by the appearance of a white cloud. The use of masking tape to wrap the exposed flanges will improve the ability to detect leaks. The masking tape can be pierced at one point to provide the check location.
All leaks shall be repaired and the system re-tested.
The piping system shall at no time be exposed to steam cleaning or purging.
Cleaning with solvents is not permitted unless specifically approved in writing by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.
