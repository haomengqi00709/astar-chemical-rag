# 5-SPC-0104 — Specification


[TABLE: 5-SPC-0104_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this specification is to define the requirements for the installation of field erected piping.

## Scope
This specification applies to the installation of all piping by the Piping Subcontractor.
Piping will satisfy the requirements of ASME B31.1 or B31.3 as applicable. Cases of conflict or doubt between the requirements of Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' documents and these codes will be referred to Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' representative for interpretation.
Piping is generally divided into three classes.
Power Piping as defined in ASME B31.1.
Plant services and utilities piping for fluids classed as ASME B31.3 Category D.
Process piping for fluids classed as ASME B31.3 Category N.
Where any welding or fabrication will be undertaken in the field as part of the pipe erection, the Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Standards in Section 6.5 will apply:

## Drawings
Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide the Piping Subcontractor with a detailed piping isometric for each line.  These isometrics show overall dimensions, bill of materials, and pipe support references.  Detailed piping isometrics may not be provided for some small diameter field run piping.
Dimensional information shown on the piping isometric drawings is nominal.   The Piping Subcontractor is responsible for all field adjustments necessary to accommodate equipment and field installation tolerances.
Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide piping general arrangements.   These arrangements are intended to show the general location of piping and supports only, and may or may not be updated to reflect the final isometrics.   Dimensional information shown on the piping isometrics will govern in cases of conflict.
Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide piping and instrumentation drawings (P&ID's) and a piping line list together with the appropriate references for pipe material specifications, pressure test methods and pressure, insulation, and painting requirements.

## Materials
All materials received at the construction site will be stored in a manner to prevent damage, loss or pilferage, and facilitate easy identification.
The Piping Subcontractor will have an effective system to identify different materials and components to prevent substitution.
The Piping Subcontractor will institute effective practices to control the issue of piping materials from storage and to recover components issued to the work site such as high alloy bolting, gaskets, spacers, fittings, etc. which are not utilised for the work in hand.
Carbon steel piping has been identified with a painted line extending the length of the pipe. Colours are identified in the pipe material specifications.
Fabricated titanium stub ends have been colour coded as indicated in the pipe specification.
Gaskets will be colour coded on the outside diameter surface to facilitate easy identification in the field as identified on the gasket specification.  Gaskets may be pre-marked by the Supplier.
Pipe materials have been supplied with sufficient quantities of construction and commissioning spares.  The Piping Subcontractor will be solely responsible for shortages of materials due to loss, theft and fabrication error.

## Schedules
The Piping Subcontractor will prepare a piping schedule in accordance with the overall construction schedule to the satisfaction of Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.   This schedule will be used to monitor the Piping Subcontractor’s performance.
The Piping Subcontractor will co-ordinate his work with other work being undertaken in the plant and will schedule his work to minimise disruption and maximise the use of common scaffolding, temporary access platforms, etc.
Piping should be scheduled for erection in sections and groups by area to minimise congestion and to maximise the use of common temporary scaffolds and access platforms.

## Installation
Piping will be assembled and erected so that a minimum of unintended cold springing and cold forcing occurs.  In cases where the isometric dimensions differ from the field dimensions to such an extent that even after using the field trim allowances and/or spacers cold springing or cold forcing is required, the Piping Subcontractor will make field modifications after receiving instructions and approval from Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.
Piping will be installed to permit free expansion and contraction except where the drawings specifically indicate an anchor or guide pipe support.
The standard tolerance for pipe installation is ± 13 mm (±  ½ inch), unless otherwise approved by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.
Where a slope is indicated but not dimensioned, it is intended that the line be generally horizontal, without visible sags or reverse slopes, thus aiding drainage in the direction indicated.
The Piping Subcontractor will ensure that no debris, weld rod stubs, paper and plastic products or any other material remains in any piping system.  Each line will be inspected and cleaned before any closing joint is assembled.  Temporary covers will be installed over open connections when the work is not being immediately continued.
Where socket weld fittings are used, the pipe will be so spaced in the socket that no stresses will be imparted to the weld due to "bottoming" of the pipe in the socket.
For screwed connections in steam service, joints will be made with a suitable anti-seize compound.   For screwed piping in other services, joints will be made using Teflon tape.  Wrapping will start approximately two threads from the end of the pipe and applied so that the tape will tighten when the joint is screwed together.  If the joint is broken, all old tape will be removed before re-assembly.
Pre-spooled piping should be installed before other connecting piping and before small equipment such as pumps are anchored.  This allows minor adjustment of equipment and connecting piping to fit the pre-spooled pipe. Where expansion bellows have been provided to allow for misalignment of pre-spooled piping, the installed bellows will be checked to ensure that the maximum allowable combined axial, parallel and angular displacement has not been exceeded.
Bellows will be installed with the face to face dimension as specified on the isometric and manufacturer’s drawing.  Compression or expansion of bellows may be required in order to meet the installation dimension requirements.
Where instruments or valves can not be installed in a pipe at time of pipe installation, the Piping Subcontractor will fabricate spool pieces of the appropriate face to face dimensions to allow assembly and testing of the pipe.
On screwed piping systems, the Piping Subcontractor should anticipate the need to install unions, in addition to those shown on the piping isometrics, to facilitate ease of fabricating and maintaining these systems.
Where specified by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative, Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics supplied start up strainers will be installed on pump suctions.  These will typically be removed after water batching but prior to plant start-up.
When installing wafer type valves and instruments, particularly butterfly valves, care must be taken to ensure that the item is centred on the flanges.  Butterfly valves will be opened to ensure that the valve it does not bind on the ID of the pipe.  Wafer check valves with a ‘loose’ flapper disc will be installed with the proper spacers or checked to make sure the pipe ID is with the manufacturer’s stated tolerance.
Flange guards are provided for lines containing strong acids and caustics as indicated on the piping line list.

### Bolting
Flanged connections will be made using bolting as specified in the pipe material specifications.
All bolting will be tightened with suitable wrenches or sockets only - adjustable "crescent" type wrenches will not be used.  Bolting will be tightened in the correct pattern and to the torque specified by the manufacturer or generally accepted industry standards.  In tightening joints, care will be taken to secure uniform pressure and avoid over-stressing bolts or dishing flanges. Gaskets will be carefully centred on the flange before tightening.
Bolt lengths will be approximately 6 mm (1/4 inch) past the nut after final tightening of the assembled joint.  In no case should the bolt projection be less than one exposed complete thread.
On projects where metric bolting is specified, tapped piping components including PTFE bellows and lug tapped valves may require imperial bolting.  Bolting materials will be of an equivalent specification to those listed in the applicable pipe specification.
Studs will be run into tapped components by using a second nut as a jamb nut and threading the stud in using standard wrenches.  Under no circumstances will studs be run into tapped components using pliers or "Vice-grips".
Particular attention will be given to bolting flat face plastic flanges to raised face or lined valves and equipment.  Bolting must be tightened in the appropriate sequence and torqued to the plastic flange manufacturer’s specifications in order to prevent over-stressing or cracking of the plastic flange.
If galvanised threaded rod is supplied and must be field cut to length, the cut end will be chamfered to allow free run of the nut.   The exposed ends of carbon steel fasteners will be coated with "Galvacon", or equivalent cold galvanising compound.

### Supports
The supporting of piping will be done in accordance with the piping isometrics, the general arrangement drawings, the Specification for Pipe Supports, 5-SPC-0005, and the Specification for Field Supporting of Pipe, 5-SPC-0006.
Pipe supports have been engineered for all lines of 80 mm (3") and larger.  For smaller lines, pipe supports have been indicated on the piping isometrics as a guide to the Piping Subcontractor.
Support steel, pipe supports, hangers, guides, etc. wherever possible should be installed before the piping is erected.  Temporary support will be used where required.
The Piping Subcontractor should anticipate and install additional pipe supports not shown on the piping isometrics where required and/or as identified or accepted by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative, particularly to accept loads during hydrostatic testing.

### Insulation and Painting
Piping will be insulated in accordance with the Piping Line List, 5-LST-0001, and the Specification for Insulation, 6-SPC-0001.
Carbon steel pipe and lined carbon steel pipe will be painted in accordance with the Piping Line List, 5-LST-0001, and the Specification for Protective Coating, 7-SPC-0001.
Plastic pipe located outdoors or in locations where direct sunlight can occur will be painted with an UV inhibiting latex paint in accordance with the Piping Line List, 5-LST-0001, and the Specification for Protective Coatings, 7-SPC-0001.
The Piping Subcontractor will be responsible for touch up of paint on equipment, piping and structural steel, where damage has occurred to the painted surfaces during the course of the piping installation.

## Inspection
All welded joints will be inspected in accordance with the appropriate fabrication specification.
Each line will be inspected by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' field representative for conformity to the drawings and specifications, with special attention to workmanship, gasket location, and bolt torque.
Removable sections of pipe at locations such as a pump inlet and discharge will be inspected to ensure no excessive cold spring or force is transmitted to the equipment.
Expansion joints will be inspected to ensure the cold position is within the allowable value.

## Testing and Flushing
Testing and flushing will be performed in the presence of the Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative and in accordance with specification 5-SPC-0102, Specification for Testing and Flushing of Process Plant Piping Systems or 5-SPC-0101 Pipe installation for Dry Chlorine Service.
Any defects, leaks, cleaning and support adjustments identified during testing will be rectified and subsequently re-tested if required by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative.
APPENDIX TC4
FIELD INSTALLATION OF SPOOLED TEFLON-LINED PIPE
Discussion
Teflon lined steel pipe is resistant to many chemical plant streams, and when used in appropriate services and installed with care can provide a long lasting piping system.
A Teflon lined steel piping system consists of individual sections flanged at each end and flanged fittings such as elbows, tees and reducers, all bolted together.  The quality of the completed line therefore depends on the care taken to align all components and carefully bolt up the flanges to create a leak tight system.
The mating faces of each flanged joint consist of the same relatively soft Teflon material as the lining.  Although not required by some manufacturers, Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics has specified that gaskets be installed between the teflon faced flanges.  The presence of soft facing surfaces requires very special attention to the sequence and torque applied to the bolt tightening operation.
This installation instruction includes guidance to the Subcontractor to enable him to assemble a safe and satisfactory system.
Pre-Assembly Inspection
All Teflon lined pipe and piping components are received at the job site with flange face protectors attached.  These are sometimes made of wood, but are most commonly special plastic covers. These covers must be left on the pipe and fittings at all times except for inspection, after which the covers are reinstalled and removed just before assembly.  Each component must be individually inspected to ensure the inside is clean and, most importantly, that the flange face is flat and not scratched, gouged or otherwise damaged, and that no cracks appear in the lining, particularly at the radius between the flange face and the bore.
Some surface imperfections on the face of the flange face as designated by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics site representative may be polished out by hand using fine abrasive cloth or paper.  Cracking at the radius, or in any other location, is cause for rejection and any such part or component must not be used.  In addition to the normal routine receiving inspection of the pipe and fittings, each Pipefitter should visually inspect each component at the time of erection and reject damaged components for subsequent repair or replacement.
Gaskets used are Teflon.  Each gasket will be examined before installation for surface imperfections, scratches or gouges.  Damaged gaskets will not be used.
Welding is not permitted on any lined pipe or fitting.
Assembly
The procedure for line assembly starts with the in place pre-assembly of the total line including all components and branches, with all bolts inserted and tightened "finger tight" using no tools.  Bolts should be coated with a suitable "anti-seize" lubricating compound.
To accomplish this pre-assembly, all of the specified pipe supports, hangers, guides etc. must be in place, and additionally, temporary supports made of wire, wood, etc. are usually required for each horizontal pipe spool length.
Following this pre-assembly of the total line, the line will be reviewed for dimensional discrepancies.  An additional gasket can be inserted between flanges to increase length in increments of 1/16 inch (2 mm) per gasket joint.  Where this is insufficient, Teflon spacers can be used.  At any location there will never be more than one spacer and/or two gaskets.
In some lines, Teflon bellows expansion joints are included as part of the design.  Each expansion joint has a very narrow range of acceptable dimensions in the "cold" position.  The piping must be adjusted to allow the joint to be installed within this small range.
After a suitable adjustment of the piping components in the line has been reached, the bolting at each joint must be tightened to achieve a leak free system.  The tools required are a standard open end wrench and a torque wrench with a socket.  Do not use adjustable "crescent" type wrenches.
Starting at a fixed anchor point on the pipe line, usually a flanged connection to a piece of equipment or tank, each joint is made progressively, torquing up to one half of the torque value recommended by the manufacturer.  The bolting sequence is important, as the soft flange facing can be easily distorted by uneven tightening.  Uneven tightening often will show in the assembled line as slight "bends" at the joint.
Following completion of the first connection to a torque one half of the recommended value, the next flange is tightened and work proceeds progressively down the line.  After completion of this tightening sequence, the line is again reviewed to ensure the expected dimensions will be maintained.
Where "bends" or other slight misalignment is evident, nuts on the "tight" side should be released by one half turn and nuts on the "loose" side tightened by one half turn, repeating until alignment is achieved.
Spacers may be required to be added or removed to adjust dimensions.  To correct for major angular misalignment, a standard 1/2 inch (13 mm) thick Teflon spacer can be machined on the face to create a wedge sloped spacer which is approximately 1/2 inch (13 mm) thick on the wide side and not less than 3/8 inch (9 mm) on the narrow side.  The use of such tapered spacers must be reviewed and approved by Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' site representative before installation.
After achieving satisfactory alignment of the total pipe line, each joint will be re-torqued to the value recommended by the manufacturer, following the same sequence of flanges and the same balanced sequence of bolt tightening.
The temporary supports should then be removed and the permanent supports adjusted where necessary to correctly guide and support the finished line.
During leak test with water, every connection must be inspected to ensure a tight joint has been achieved.  If a leak occurs, do not tighten bolts further or permanent damage to the flange faces may occur.  Instead, the bolts on the opposite side should be loosened by one half turn, and the bolts on the leaking side tightened by the same amount.
If leaking still persists, the bolts should be removed and the faces of the flanges inspected for damage.  Any scratches or dents which do not exceed 20% of the liner face thickness can be repaired by hand polishing with fine abrasive cloth or paper.  If these measures fail to produce a leak tight joint, one or more of the components must be replaced.
For safety, do not tighten or loosen any nut or bolt while a Teflon lined piping system is under pressure or more than 30°F above ambient temperature.
After the first 24 hours of "hot" operation, the system should be shut down, alignment of expansion bellows rechecked and adjusted and all bolts checked for proper toque.

APPENDIX CF1 & VF6
FIELD INSTALLATION OF FRP WRAPPED PVC AND CPVC PIPE
Intent
The purpose of the reinforcement described is to increase allowable pipe support spacing and to provide additional mechanical strength to resist breakage or damage from external loadings.  The reinforcement is not considered to increase the pressure or temperature rating of the pipe system.
CF1 and VF6 are supplied as CP3 and PV6 loose fittings, respectively, and bulk FRP wrap material.
Materials
Materials will be as specified in the pipe specification.
Piping
The outer surface of the PVC or CPVC piping will be roughened with a grinder and all grinding dust removed with dry compressed air where required.  Coat the roughened surface with a catalyzed bonding resin as per the manufacturer’s installation instructions.
Wrapping will be done using rolls of fibreglass reinforcement, wound spirally with a slight overlap at the edges.  Each layer of mat will be completely covered in resin and rolled to remove all entrained air pockets.  Reinforcement will be applied to the piping to the thickness specified in the pipe specification.  Reinforcement will be applied in several sessions with no more than six layers of mat applied per session to avoid an excessive exotherm.  The surface finish of the reinforcement will be smooth, free of sags, drips or wrinkles and will contain no exposed glass fibres.
The final two layers of mat will be applied with a fire retardant resin.  When the final wrap has hardened completely, a final pigmented hot coat containing ultraviolet inhibitors will be applied.
Wrapping of straight pipe will be done under clean, controlled conditions, preferably with the pipe on a mandrel.
Piping can either be:
"dry fit" in the field to required length and then wrapped up to 2" (50mm) of intended socket depth at joints; or
cut to the theoretical cut length plus 2" (50 mm) field trim each end.  Wrapping will be extended to within 3" (80 mm) of theoretical cut length each end.
The unwrapped ends of each pipe will be protected during the wrapping process to keep them clean and free from any resin, dirt or other contaminates that might interfere with making good solvent cemented joints.
Jointing
The pre-wrapped pipe and unwrapped fittings will be installed and all joints solvent cemented using the manufacturers recommended materials and procedures.
FRP wrapping of the joints is to be done only after the pressure testing of the completed spool.
Bolts of flanged joints will be tightened in accordance with the manufacturers recommended sequence and bolt torques for plain PVC or CPVC flanges.
Fittings & Flanges
Following pressure testing and inspection of the piping system, a double layer of chopped strand mat and resin will be overlaid over all pipe fittings, the hubs only of all flanges, and the adjacent portions of pipe reinforcement to a length equal to the OD of the reinforced pipe (minimum one inch).  Prior to application of the overlay, the affected surfaces of pre-applied pipe reinforcement will be ground to remove the surface finish, and the fittings, bare pipe and flange hubs will be treated with bonding resin.  The reinforcement of fittings and flanges will again be applied in a neat, workmanlike manner, so as to present an outer surface of smooth contour, free from sags, drips or wrinkles, and will contain no exposed glass fibres.  When the final wrap has hardened completely, a final pigmented hot coat containing ultraviolet inhibitors will be applied.
APPENDIX PF1
FIELD INSTALLATION OF
FRP WRAPPED POLYPROPYLENE PIPE
Intent
The purpose of the reinforcement described is to increase allowable working pressure at a given temperature. The reinforcement also allows greater pipe support spacing and provides additional mechanical strength to resist breakage or damage from external loadings.
PF1 is provided as factory fabricated spools according to Kvaerner Aker Kvaerner Chemetics isometrics.   Field joints are allowed and involved butt welding of the liner and FRP lining over the joint.
Materials
Polypropylene pipe will be a factory spooled FRP wrapped piping system as specified in the pipe specification.
Pre-cut field joint kits will be provided.
Piping Assembly
Piping will be assembled using the standard tools and procedures for butt fusion of polypropylene pipe (see Appendix PP6).  Care will be taken in performing butt welds to ensure that the exterior fibreglass does not get embedded in the face of the weld.  The exterior bead formed by the fusion process will be ground flush prior to wrapping.
FRP wrapping of the joints is to be done only after the pressure testing of the completed spool.
