1. 
Purpose 
The purpose of the Process Flow Diagram (PFD) is to record the “steady state” balanced flows, 
temperatures and pressures of the defined system.  The PFD is used by the process engineer 
as a starting point for producing the PDS, PDL and Line Lists.   
 
This procedure defines the steps required to achieve a published PFD and assigns 
responsibilities for document preparation, checking and approval.

COMMENTARY 
 
To Section 1 
The Process Flow Diagram will show the steady state mass balance at the design capacity of 
the plant.  Flows, pressures and temperatures for all significant streams, including utilities, 
must also be shown.  Pump kick-back lines, drain lines, etc. are not show on the PFD. 
Normally the only physical properties that needs to be shown on the PFD is density.  Other 
physical properties are show on the relevant PDS , PDL, Line List, etc.  
 
There may be a number of cases, e.g. strong acid plants, where additional Process Flow 
Diagrams are required to fully define the plant capacity and/or operation. 
 
The term “steady state” is understood to apply for “instantaneous design conditions” such as 
maximum operation, minimum turndown, autothermal condition etc.  These are not true steady 
state conditions, but represent conditions under which it is necessary to establish mass and 
energy balances. 
 
To Section 3.3 and 3.4 
PFD Sketch: 
Process prepares a PFD sketch from a source, which can be from one or more of the following: 
• 
Standard template 
• 
Proposal Flow Diagram, which is an Attachment to the Contract 
• 
Previous PFD used in another project 
• 
Hand sketch 
 
Clarity of the presentation is important.  All main process and utility lines must be shown.  
Grouping and crowding equipment at the expense of clarity should be avoided. 
 
Drawings: 
• 
The equipment shall be drawn approximately in proportion to the actual sizes, in a 
sequence of flow from left to right.  
• 
Each equipment item including those with duplicate sets is to be shown. 
• 
Connectivity lines will be: 
• 
Solid thick line for major process lines 
• 
Solid thin line for minor process lines and utility lines 
• 
Dashed lines for existing equipment 
• 
The first page of the set of drawings will show the Project Design Capacity. 
 
Drawing Borders and Size: 
• 
Standard border showing the vertical block 
• 
Non-standard borders may be requested by the Client 
• 
Standard size drawing may be metric “A1” or Imperial “D” size 
 
Numbering: 
• 
PFD drawing number : Refer to 0-PRC-0002: Document Numbering
