# 0-PRC-8503 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8503_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this procedure is to eliminate the potential for injury to personnel and damage to equipment due to fire or explosion resulting from cutting, welding and similar hot work performed during commissioning of a system or of an entire chemical plant.
This regulation will be followed by the Commissioning Team (Aker Kvaerner Chemetics personnel, Contractors and Vendors rep) after a circuit, any system or equipment is handed over mechanically completed for commissioning by the Construction Manager to the Commissioning Manager.
When available, a Client’s regulation may be followed if the requirements and guidelines spelled out on Aker Kvaerner Chemetics’ procedures are fully covered by the Client’s regulation.
Note: This procedure is based on Aker Kvaerner’s Open Flame / Hot Work procedure issued in January of 2003 and found in the AK’s Safety Policy Manual section 7, item 7.03.

## Scope
This safety regulation will apply to any work wherein sufficient heat or sparks may be generated to serve as a source of ignition in any work area.

## Responsibilities
The hazard assessment created for the task must be understood by all members of the team involved with the hot work activity.
The Commissioning HSE Manager is responsible for the administration of the Hot Work Permit system.
In case the Commissioning HSE Manager is temporarily away from site (i.e., R&R, site leave, sick leave, etc.),  the Commissioning Manager will take over the administration of the Open Flame and Hot Work Permit system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will be responsible for issuing permits or, when applicable, for obtaining permits from the client issuing authority.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will establish and maintain a program that adequately informs Chemetic’s personnel of the existence, purpose and procedures of the Hot Work system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager and the Work Supervisor must sign a valid permit for this program.

## Training
Each employee who is authorized to perform open flame/ hot work shall be thoroughly trained in all aspects of these guidelines. Everyone must go through Open Flame/ Hot Work Training provided by the Commissioning HSE Manager, either on Site or in Aker Kvaerner Chemetics office.
Training shall be documented and entered into the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics log book that the Commissioning HSE Manager keeps.
All Open Flame/ Hot Work incidents and near misses shall be thoroughly investigated and documented.

## Exception
There is no exception to this regulation for all process, utilities and chemical storage areas.
The following areas and situations may obtain an exemption to this regulation.
Hot work to be conducted in a laydown yard or another such isolated area where no (zero) risk of exposure to or the ignition of combustible/flammable materials.
New construction areas with no (zero) risk of exposure to or the ignition of combustible/flammable vapour or solid materials may obtain exemption from hot work permit.
Areas approved as fabrication shops by the company or client.  The Commissioning HSE Manager signs approval.

## General Requirements
A "Hot Work Permit" will be required when using any equipment, tools or apparatus capable of generating heat, sparks or flames in any restricted areas.  Examples include but not limited to, welding, burning, cutting, sawing, grinding, drilling, open flame operations, non-explosion proof electrical equipment, sandblasting operation of internal combustion engines and hot tap operations.  See Form DP-802.702 for a sample Hot Work Permit form.
A job-specific written procedure must be prepared and approved before any work is started that requires welding together of connection or additions to piping, vessels and tanks containing flammable liquids, gas or combustible materials under pressure.
This regulation is NOT applicable for execution of hot tap activities. A Hot Work permit is not a substitute for an approved hot tap procedure.  However, all hot taps require a Hot Work permit. Refer to API publications 2201: Procedures for Welding or Hot Tapping on Equipment Containing Flammables for a more detailed discussion of hot taps.

## Procedures

### Task Safety Awareness (TSA)
No hot work permit will be issued until the Commissioning HSE Manager completes a Task Safety Awareness (TSA) review and has it posted at the location of the work. See Form DP-802.306 (Task Safety Awareness form).

### Completion and Issue of Permits
Every question on the form must be answered in ink.  Where any item is not applicable, it will be marked thus and not left blank.
The person executing the work will sign the permit, thereby signifying that he and his crew fully understand the conditions spelled out on the permit and will honour those conditions.
The form will be completed in duplicate in the English language and if necessary in the local language.  The hard copy is to be displayed at the work site. The soft copy is to be retained in the commissioning office until the work has been completed.
On completion of the work, the bottom portion of the permit shall be completed by the person executing the work and returned to the point of issue.
NOTE:  	This permit becomes invalid if work does not start within one hour of issue or if there is a break in the work duration.

### Duration and Validity of Hot Work Permit
A permit is not valid for more than one shift, unless authorized by the Commissioning HSE Manager. In any case, the duration of the Hot Work Permit shall not exceed 12 hours. A new permit is to be issued if the job / task duration exceeds 12 hours.

### Cancellation of Permit
Any competent authority may stop the work covered by this permit at any time if they consider the prevailing conditions or work methods to be unsafe.  Anyone stopping work in this manner will remove the site copy of the permit and return it to the Commissioning HSE Manager, giving his reasons for his action.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will visit the work site and decide whether or not the permit should be re-validated.  It must be clearly understood that, with just reason, any competent authority may stop the work, but only the Commissioning HSE Manager can permit a restart.
NOTE:   This permit is automatically cancelled in the event of a flammable release in the vicinity or the sounding of a fire or gas alarm.

### Hot Work Permit Guidelines
All lines leading to and from the equipment or line to be worked on shall be isolated, blinded off or disconnected. Refer to 0-PRC-8505-A for Line and Flange Break procedure.
The Commissioning HSE Manager is responsible for testing and certifying in writing that no flammable gases, liquids or solids, oxygen deficiency or toxic materials are present in the location.  Gas testing shall be in accordance with approved methods and equipment.  Tests should indicate, and the Hot Work Permit certifies, that there are no flammable gases present.
Contractors should be encouraged to own, maintain, and operate their own oxygen and gas detection devices.  The Commissioning HSE Manager  is the final authority when conflicting test results are encountered.
Adequate ventilation shall be provided when working in a closed container or enclosed space (i.e., a minimum of 12 changes of air per hour at the point or points where work is being performed).  If adequate ventilation is not possible, suitable respiratory protection shall be used.  Refer to 0-PRC-8502-A for Confined Space Entry procedure.
Before burning into or heating any hollow vessel or equipment, which has been in service of any kind, the vessel or equipment shall be properly vented (unless the operation is performed under controlled conditions so that any over pressuring will be avoided).
A competent fire watch will be assigned to the job when indicated on the Hot Work Permit. The fire watch will have no other duties when carrying out his assignment.
A fully charged fire extinguisher of a type and size designated as suitable shall be provided where Hot Work is to be done.
Gas-burning and welding torches and hose shall be removed from all vessels or closed containers, or hose shall be disconnected at the regulators at the end of any shift and at lunch time or at any other time when work is discontinued.
Cylinders of any flammable material shall never be placed in a container where Hot Work is to be done.
When Hot Work is done in overhead locations, a method for catching hot metal, electrode stubs, etc., must be provided to prevent a fire and personnel hazard.
The Commissioning HSE Manager shall not issue a Work Permit covering Hot Work while a lock-out, tag-out procedure allowing opening of lines and vessels is in existence in the area that could be made hazardous by any release.
All openings from which releases occur shall be considered hazardous.  The area of concern shall cover the limits where there is a remote possibility that sparks from Hot Work could fall into any vapours which could be released from the opening or could flow to a point where sparks might ignite them.  If a lockout, tag-out procedure for opening is in existence, it is the responsibility of the Commissioning HSE Manager to see that a Work Permit for Hot Work is not issued or the lockout, tag-out procedure for opening is terminated.
If the Commissioning HSE Manager decides sufficient liquid or vapour has been released to create a hazard, a Work Permit covering Hot Work shall not be issued until the hazard has been eliminated.
When electric welding is to be performed, the ground connection shall be attached to the work within the area covered by the permit, and the welding machine shall be in the area.  The ground and the stringer should be as close together as possible and on the same equipment.
Ground connections, except under approved "Hot Tap" conditions, must not be attached to "live" process lines or vessels.
Flammable gas and oxygen deficiency testing will only be undertaken using an approved explosive meter or oxygen meter.

### Fire Watch Requirements
The fire watch person shall be trained by the Commissioning HSE Manager on the proper execution of the fire watch responsibilities.  Proper use of fire extinguisher and explosion meters shall be included in this training.
The fire watch shall be familiar with the location and nature of the hot work.
The fire watch shall have no other duties.  If the fire watch leaves, the hot work will be disconnected, the welder turned off and electric supply turned off until he/she returns.  The fire watch shall remain in constant communication range with person(s) doing work.
The fire watch shall be provided with the proper fire extinguisher(s), which shall be located in the immediate area for quick availability.  These extinguishers should not be taken from safety stations.  They are to be used for emergencies only.
The fire watch shall be provided with an explosion meter on jobs that could potentially involve flammable gases or vapours.  The area shall be periodically tested with the meter.  If the explosion meter reading indicates a percent concentration at the lower explosive limit, the hot work shall be stopped and the potential problem corrected before the hot work can be restarted.
The fire watch shall continually survey the work area for combustible materials.
More than one fire watch may be required depending on potential impact on other areas, such as floors below the hot work activity.
If a potential for a spill in an adjacent area could create a hazard, a roving monitor with an explosion meter shall be assigned to patrol the area with instructions to immediately stop the hot work if flammable conditions are detected.
If there is a fire that the fire watch cannot extinguish quickly and safely, the emergency evacuation alarm shall be activated.  The fire watch shall always know the location of the nearest alarm box.
