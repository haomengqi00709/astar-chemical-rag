# 0-PRC-8007 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8007_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure outlines the process to be used for a systemic analysis of hazards that are present during the execution of a job or task, with the goal of creating procedures or specifying the means to eliminate or minimize all hazards.

## Definitions
Hazard – Is an existing condition, or a condition created during the execution of an activity that, if left uncontrolled, can result in an injury, illness, or loss.
Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) – The process of systematically analyzing job tasks to identify hazards before they occur. It focuses on the relationship between the worker, the task, the tools, and the environment. Its purpose is to seek the best methods, best tools, and the best safety equipment to remove risk of injury, or risk of environmental damage.

## Scope
Job Hazard Analysis will be applied in all medium to extreme risk tasks in all areas of operations.  See step 1 of section 6.2 for task selection guidelines.

## Responsibilities

### Managers/Supervisors
The Site Manager, Site Safety Manager and his Supervisors will cooperate to identify tasks that require a JHA.
All Managers and Supervisors will demonstrate leadership in the application of JHA.

### Health and Safety Manager
The site Safety Manager, or other qualified personnel will ensure the JHA is completed. See below for application procedure.
The site Safety Manager will approve all JHA’s performed on his job site.
The Safety Manager will make the JHA’s available to the supervisory employees.

## Job Pre-Start Orientation of Supervisory Personnel
Each new supervisor upon assignment to the project will receive an orientation that fully explains the principles and steps of Job Hazard Analysis (JHA).

## Application Procedure
JHA is to be applied at the worksite during the job planning phase. Jobs with similar characteristics and same task steps may be grouped together for the purpose of performing the JHA.

### Qualification Requirements
Hazard assessments will be prepared by a qualified person who has adequate training and experience in the recognition, evaluation and control of hazards, and in consultation with the person assigned overall responsibility for administration of the Health and safety on the work site.
Hazard assessments may be done with the employees prior commencing a task, provided that the hazard assessment is led by a qualified person.
Confined space entry hazard assessments will have special requirements, refer to:
Standard-0-PRC-8502 Confined Space Entry

### Performing a JHA – 7 Basic steps
A record of the JHA is to be kept using form:
Standard-0-REP-8007a Job Hazard Analysis Worksheet
For confined space entry the JHA record is to be kept using form:
Standard-0-REP-8007b Confined Space Hazard Assessment
A job hazard analysis is broken down into 7 major steps.
Select the job to be analyzed
A JHA is required:
All confined space entry, or confined space work
If the Work Permit or Work Order states it is a requirement
Other high risk jobs, such as:
Jobs with a history of, or potential for injury, illness, or environment impact;
High risk jobs in which one simple human error could lead to a severe accident or injury;
Jobs that have not been assessed for risk;
Jobs that have undergone changes in processes and procedures; and
Jobs when new employees are performing the task.
Begin by filling in the job description at the top of the form. This is a short explanation about the job and should not be too complex.  Enough detail should be provided to properly identify the job and differentiate it from other jobs.
List the steps necessary to the job
Break the job into a sequence of steps, each describing what is being done.  This procedure requires the assistance of a safe, experienced worker.  Care must be taken not to be too detailed.  Each step should tell what is being done - not how.  When recording the step, begin with action word like "remove", "carry", or "open", and end by identifying to what the action was applied.  For example, "remove the die", "carry to bench", etc.  After observing and recording the steps, the observed employee should verify all steps and explain any deviations that may occur.  The deviations that occur irregularly could lead to an accident.
Identify the potential hazards for each step
Identify the hazards and determine what can go wrong and result in an accident.  Question each step.  Can the employee be struck by a moving object, strike against a stationary object, get caught in moving equipment, slip or fall, strain himself, or be exposed to a toxic substance or excessive noise performing the task?
Develop hazard controls and record (Precautions)
Develop a recommended safe job procedure that eliminates or controls hazardous conditions or operations.
Apply the following “Hierarchy of Risk Control” (in most effective to least effective order):
Elimination or substitution of the process/substance by finding a new way to do the job
Changing the physical condition that creates the hazard
Engineering controls, (e.g. guards, mechanical aids)
Administration controls (e.g. reducing exposure , rotating employees, procedures, signage)
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
Providing additional training to the employee.
In doing the above seek the help of the experienced employee (the domain expert). His experience with "near misses" and time spent on the job will enable him to make practical suggestions.  The employee's involvement will also improve employee acceptance when the new procedures are implemented.
Review the JHA Record
Complete the JHA record. Ensure that you have listed: Job Step/Activity, Potential Hazards, and Control measures.
Review the recorded JHA; assign a risk ranking to each task activity and assign a responsible person for critical activities.
Integrate into worker-training program
Integrate the risk control measures with the job steps together to form the job’s standard operation procedures, by using the JHA as the guide to brief employees during a their pre-job Task Safety Awareness.
Re-evaluate
A review of and existing JHA will be done when:
The first task using the JHA is completed
Improvements are requested from employees doing the task
New hazards identified
The method of performing the task changes
When accidents or near-misses occur on jobs that have been analyzed, the causes of the accidents should be checked against the JHA to make sure that controls have been established to handle the causes.

## JHA Records
Each project will maintain a record of all JHA reports.
Projects shall continuously stress the concepts, principles of application, and importance of JHA in Safety topics and meetings.
