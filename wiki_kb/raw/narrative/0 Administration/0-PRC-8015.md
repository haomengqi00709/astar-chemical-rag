# 0-PRC-8015 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8015_table_1]


## Purpose
This specification describes the minimum requirements for personal protection equipment to be used in construction or fabrication activities.

## Personal Protective Equipment
Personal protective equipment is proven to reduce risk of injury when factors that can lead to an accidental exposure cannot be fully controlled.  Aker Kvaerner Chemetics employees will wear the required (or recommended) protective equipment necessary for entry into a work area or for the performance of a specific task.
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide all protective equipment at no cost to the employee, with some limitations on frequency.
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will provide protective equipment for visitors where and as necessary.

## Clothing
Appropriate clothing for employees working in a non-office environment will be long sleeved shirt and long sleeved pants of proper fit. Shirts must be tucked in at all times. Excessively loose fitting clothing is a hazard.
Employees working with power tools or in close proximity to rotating machines will take care that loose clothing, long hair, ties, jewellery, accessories, etc. do not pose a danger.  Long hair must be tired up; jewellery must be removed or covered; ties must not be worn; and clothing must be of proper fit.
In situations where the skin or body of an employee may be in danger, special clothing must be worn.  This includes rainwear, chemical suits, flash suits, etc.
Company provides appropriate work wear to employees.
Company approved work wear with weekly laundry service will be provided for all shop employees.

## Protective Footwear
Safety boots are required for all employees and visitors in the following areas of operations:
Construction sites
Workshops
Company approved footwear is limited to CSA Grade 1 boots, which meet the CSA standard Z195 (or equivalent ANSI standard).  A green CSA patch in the shape of either a rectangle or triangle indicates CSA approval. The triangular patch designates sole puncture protection, in addition to impact protection.  All CSA approved boots provide protection against falling and sharp objects in a heavy industrial work environment.
Special Purpose Footwear
Whenever required, special purpose footwear shall be selected to provide maximum protection for the employee.  Such footwear will at minimum incorporate a steel shank and steel toe.

## Head Protection
Approved hard hats are required to be worn by all employees working in constructions sites, and in other work environments if the employee is in danger of sustaining a head injury due to ongoing work above him/her or other falling hazards.
Protective headgear must meet the requirements of CSA standard Z94.1 (or ANSI equivalent).
Employees will be instructed in the proper use of the hard hat at the site-specific health and safety orientation.

### Proper Usage Notes
Metal hard hats or bump caps are not acceptable head protection.
It is unacceptable to drill holes in headwear for ventilation.
Cracked hard hats will be replaced with a new one immediately.
Hard hats will not be worn on top of everyday caps or hats.
Head band will be adjusted for proper snugness, and the harness will be adjusted so that there is at least 1-1/4 inches between the head and the inside shell of the hat.

## Protective Eyewear
Protective eyewear is required on all construction sites and fabrications facilities.  In other locations employees will be notified of the requirement at the site-specific health and safety orientation.
Standard eye protection for Aker Kvaerner Chemetics is ANSI or CSA approved industrial safety glasses with side shields.  All protective eyewear worn must meet CSA Z94.3 standard (or the equivalent ANSI Z87 standard).
It is imperative to maintain all protective eyewear with frequent cleaning, as well as to replace any scratched or damaged safety glasses/goggles.

### Prescription Eye Protection
Contact lenses are not to be worn in the shop, laboratory setting, construction or maintenance sites, even when covered with approved goggles.
Employees who wear prescription glasses must have eye protection that meets the above standards and includes side shields. The following guidelines are appropriate for non-standard eyewear:
Tinted glasses are not to be worn inside buildings or at night.
Employee may add side shields to present glasses if the glasses with side shields are ANSI or CSA approved. Side shields will be of the type approved for the frames, or of universal fit, and must be securely fastened to the frame.
Employee may choose to wear ANSI or CSA approved nono-goggles that can be worn over non-approved prescription eyewear without disturbing the adjustment of the prescription eyewear.

### Chemical (slash-Proof) goggles
Approved chemical mono-goggles will be provided to ensure protection from hazards associated with handling or dispensing liquid chemicals.  Full face shield may be worn with goggles and may be required for certain tasks.

### Face Protection
A CSA or ANSI approved full face shield will be worn to provide face protection to the employee from flying particles, splashes or mist.  A face shield provides only limited protection to the face and eyes from direct impact objects. Additional eye protection must be worn in conjunction with a face shield (i.e. mono goggles).
Tasks that dictate the use of a face shield include:
Blowing
Chipping
Grinding
Hot tar work
Liquid metal work
Pneumatic tool use
Pouring or handling of irritants
Sawing metals or masonry
Scraping
Goggles must be worn under the face shield when:
Grinding or chipping overhead or in an enclosed or confined space.
Grinding with abrasive stones or wheels.
Using power wire brushes.
Handling wet cell batteries.
Handling caustics, acids, and other chemicals where spill or splash hazards exist (chemical goggles).
Operating masonry saws.
Operating abrasive cut-off saws (gasoline or electric).
Using powder actuated tools.
Operating pneumatic impact wrenches.
Any operation which subjects the eyes or head to flying particles.
This list is not inclusive or exclusive, contact the site HSE manager for assistance in eye protection selection.

### Burning Goggles
Burning goggles with at least a number 6 shade of lens will be worn while performing oxy-acetylene, cutting, and welding.

### Welding Hood
A CSA or ANSI approved welding hood with the proper filter lens and an approved hard hat will be worn while performing arc-welding. A welding hood with a filtered lens of number 10 shade or darker will be used to provide protection from ultra-violet rays produced during electric arc welding.  Approved safety glasses with side shields shall be worn in conjunction with the welding hood to ensure protection from popping hot slag when the hood is raised.
A welding hood with a "flip-up" window and secondary clear lens is recommended as it is considered to provide greater protection to the welder.

### Exceptions to Eye Protection Requirement
Eye wear is not required:
While working in offices, office trailer, washrooms, or change rooms.
While inside a motor vehicle, or enclosed equipment cab.
When sighting through transits, levels or similar instruments, employees may remove safety glasses temporarily for the duration of the sighting.

## Hearing Protection
Employees who are regularly exposed to hazardous noise levels (8-hour at greater than Time-Weighted Average (TWA) of 85 dbA, or 12-hour at greater than TWA 82 dbA) will wear hearing protection.  It is recommended that hearing protection be worn during any exposure to persistent noise levels above TWA of 85 dbA.
Hearing protection is required in workshop areas, and in construction areas where the employee is exposed to noise levels above the previous guidelines.
Hearing protection may consist of foam ear inserts, moulded ear inserts, and muff-type hearing protectors depending on noise level.  All hearing protection worn must meet the requirements set out by CSA Standard Z94.2-02 "Hearing Protection Devices" (or equivalent ANSI standard). Consult standard for the class of hearing protection device (Class A is the best).
The Company will monitor the workplace for hazardous noise levels. Areas with hazardous noise levels will be identified with signs posted in conspicuous places to caution personnel of the potentially hazardous exposure.
Reference
CSA Z107.56-94 Procedures for the Measurement of Occupational Noise Exposure
CSA Z94.2-02 Hearing Protection Devices - Performance, Selection, Care, and Use

## Respiratory Protection
When employees are working with inorganic fibre insulation material, or any other material that may release an airborne hazardous substance, or are exposed to welding and cutting fumes, respiratory masks are required to be worn at all times.
Facial hair, piercings, or any other facial feature that may compromise the seal between the face and respirator are prohibited.
Respirators must not be modified in any way and damaged respirators are to be replaced immediately.
The 3M N95 Particulate Respirators are provided by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for all employees and visitors.   Standards for their use are set out in CSA standard Z94.4.  These masks are not for use when dealing with oxygen levels below 19.5% or contaminants in excess of 10 times the permissible exposure limit. In case where the particulate respirators are not adequate, oxygen-providing respirators must be used:  3M models 8212 and 8214 are particularly recommended for protection against welding fumes.
Selection or respiratory protection masks is beyond the scope of this document. For selection of masks to protect against specific fumes consult with the HSE Manager.
A complete list of CSA approved respiratory devices can be found on the NIOSH Certified Equipment List.

## Hand Protection
Standard hand protection gloves are recommended when working with rough or unfinished materials.
Special hand protection is required when handling solvents and chemicals. Consult Material Safety Datasheets for appropriate glove materials.
Shop employees are required to wear leather palmed gloves to protect against serious cuts.
Employees will be provided with and trained in the use of any specific hand protection requirements at the site specific Health and Safety orientation.

## Fall Prevention
Any employee engaging in work more than 2.4 metres above safe ground must use a fall protection device that meets all requirements outlined in CSA standard Z259 (or ANSI equivalent).  These devices include, but are not limited to safety belts, lanyards, personnel lowering devices, lifelines, body belts, and safety straps.
Fall arresting devices must prevent the protected employee from enduring a fall force of more than 8 kN, or free-falling more than 1.2 metres.  The anchor of a fall protection system must be able to withstand a force of 17.8 kN.
All fall prevention equipment must be inspected before and after each use for possible damage.
When ladders are used, they must be tied off or held by a spotter.
