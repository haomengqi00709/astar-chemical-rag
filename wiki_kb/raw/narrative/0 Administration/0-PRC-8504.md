# 0-PRC-8504 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8504_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this lock and tag out procedure is to ensure the safety and health of personnel by requiring that certain precautions be taken before entering an enclosed space, or prior to servicing or repairing power driven equipment.
This regulation will be followed by the Commissioning Team (Aker Kvaerner Chemetics personnel, Contractors and Vendors rep) after a circuit, any system or equipment is handed over mechanically completed for commissioning by the Construction Manager to the Commissioning Manager.
When available, a Client’s regulation may be followed if the requirements and guidelines spelled out on Aker Kvaerner Chemetics’ procedures are fully covered by the Client’s regulation.
Note: This procedure is based on Aker Kvaerner’s Energy Isolation (Lock. Tag, Test and Try) procedure issued in January of 2003 and found in the AK’s Safety Policy Manual section 7, item 7.10.

## Scope
This procedure defines the minimum requirements for safety Lock and Tag out to assure the safety of personnel and of equipment when performing work on or around equipment capable of having an energy source applied.

## Responsibilities
The hazard assessment created for the task must be understood by all members of the team involved with Lock and Tag out activity.
The Commissioning HSE Manager is responsible for the administration of the Lockout and Tagout Permit system.
In case the Commissioning HSE Manager is temporarily away from site (i.e., R&R, site leave, sick leave, etc.),  the Commissioning Manager will take over the administration of the Lockout and Tagout Permit system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will be responsible for obtaining permits from the client issuing authority.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will establish and maintain a program that adequately informs Aker Kvaerner Chemetics’ personnel of the existence, purpose and procedures of the Lockout and Tagout system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager and the Work Supervisor must sign a valid Safety Tagout Order for this program.

## Training
Each employee who is authorized to perform lockout and tagout shall be thoroughly trained in all aspects of these guidelines. Everyone must go through Lockout and Tagout Training provided by the Commissioning HSE Manager, either on Site or in Aker Kvaerner Chemetics office.
Training shall be documented and entered into the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics log book that the Commissioning HSE Manager keeps.
All Lockout and Tagout incidents and near misses shall be thoroughly investigated and documented.

## Definitions
“Safety Tagout”- The placement of a tag accompanied by a lock as required on the energy isolating device in accordance with an established procedure indicating that the energy isolating device will not be operated until removal of the tag in accordance with an established procedure.
“Danger Tag”- A prominent warning device that is capable of being securely attached and that, for the purpose of protecting personnel, forbids the operation of an energy isolating device and identifies the authorized individual or authority who has control of the clearance. A danger tag conforming to ANSI Z35.1, Section 5.3.1 Class 1, is used for this purpose.
“Extension Control Tag”- A small RED TAG or label placed on remote operating devices where applicable. It’s only function is to inform employee’s that the equipment has been tagged out back at the source and to reference the applicable safety tagout order number.
“Lockout Device”- A device that utilizes a lock and key to hold an energy isolating device in the safe position for the purpose of protecting personnel and equipment.
“Energy Source”- Any electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, nuclear, thermal, or other energy source that could cause injury to personnel or damage to equipment.
“ Energy Isolating Device”- a physical device that prevents the transmission or release of energy, including, but not limited to, the following: a manually operated electrical circuit breaker; a disconnect switch; a manually operated switch; a slide gate; slip blind; a line valve; blocks; and similar devices with a visible indication of the position of the device. (Push buttons, selector switches, and other control/circuit type devices are not energy isolating devices.)
“Knowledgeable Individual”- One who knows the effect of operating the controls or equipment.
“Authorized Individual”- A knowledgeable individual to whom the authority and responsibility to perform a specific assignment has been given by the employer.
“Commissioning HSE Manager”- The authorized individual(s) responsible to administer the safety tag out program.
“Affected Employee”- A person whose job includes activities such as erecting, installing, constructing, repairing, adjusting, inspecting, testing, operating, or maintaining the equipment/process.
“Safety Tagout Order”- A sequentially numbered form containing all the necessary information to control, document, audit, and status the safety tagout program at any time (Form FO1).
“Clearance”- The term used to identify acceptance of administrative control by an authorized individual for an equipment/process which has been safety tagged to allow performance of specific work tasks.
“Group Safety Tagout”- The term is used to identify the method by which safety tagout is accomplished in a safe and effective manner so as to permit more than one authorized individual, independent of others, to accept a clearance to allow performance of specific work on an equipment/process under one safety tagout order.
“Isolated”- Equipment/process is considered  isolated when every energy source has been blocked and/or vented by an energy isolating device to the extent necessary for the work to be performed and such that the equipment cannot change status and is administratively controlled.
“Verify”- The manipulation and placement of tags on energy isolating and other devices are correct as called for by a tagout order, and as required for personnel/equipment safety for specific work to be performed or instructions followed. Ensuring that all physical blocks/grounds/metering have been accomplished, in order to assure zero energy and zero mechanical state.
“Accept”- To take or assume the duties and responsibilities of administrative control for an equipment/process by an authorized individual.
“Release”- To give up the duties and responsibilities of administrative control for an equipment/process by an authorized individual.
“Restore”- A piece of equipment is considered to be restored when all safety tags have been removed and all required energy sources have been established such that the equipment can perform its intended function and all administrative controls have been released.
Slash(/)- A slash (/) denotes”and/or” and indicates that the two words or expressions, such as tagout/lockout or equipment/process, are to be taken together or individually.
“Will”- The word “will” denotes a mandatory requirement.
“Should”- The word “should” denotes an advisory recommendation.

## Application
The requirements of this procedure apply to all situations where the unexpected application of power, start-up, or release of stored energy of the equipment/process would be likely to endanger personnel or damage equipment.
This procedure acknowledges that there are conditions and standards, which may be site-specific or particular to specific applications of work, which may require consideration. Any safety lock & tag out procedure prepared for a site-specific or task-specific condition will conform to the minimum standards for personnel or equipment protection as set forth within this procedure.
When and where work is being performed on an existing facility, the procedure should be guided by the client’s active “in plant” procedure. However, the procedure in use will conform to the minimum safety standards for personnel and equipment protection as set forth within this procedure.

## Guiding Principles
A work permit is required to perform work on equipment in a plant under commissioning or operation.
After obtaining a permit work may only begin after:
Shutting off and locking out all electrical power sources to the equipment.
Shutting off and locking out any other sources of energy that can provide motive power to the equipment.
Shutting off and locking out valves controlling flows to the equipment, and if possible blinding line between equipment and controlling valve.
On pressurized systems, pressure between the equipment and the locked out controlling valve   must be released.
A DANGER TAG will be placed at the power sources of all equipment being serviced, and at all valves controlling flows into the equipment.

## Issuing Tagout Orders and Recordkeeping
The Commissioning HSE Manager is responsible to administer the safety tagout program.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will keep and maintain a complete set of records pertaining to the safety tagout program. At a minimum, these records will consist of the following:
Sequentially numbered records of all safety tagout orders.
A list of knowledgeable appointed individuals to perform the required actions of the safety tagout program.
The safety tagout order is utilized to control, document, audit, and status the safety tagout of equipment/processes. All information pertaining to safety tagout necessary to satisfy these objectives will be recorded on this form including the means to accept and release administrative control for an equipment/process. At a minimum, the three parts of the form will include the following information:
An index of all safety tagout orders.
An individual sequential order number.
The field site.
An accurate description of the equipment/process.
An accurate description of the work to be performed.
A complete listing of all energy isolating devices, sequential order of manipulation, device position, and locks/tags will be identified.
Any special instructions including the placement of protective grounds.
The signature, date, and time of the authorized individual (Commissioning HSE Manager) reviewing and approving the safety tagout order.
The signature, date, and time of the authorized individual (Commissioning HSE Manager) performing the safety tagout on the equipment/process.
The name and signature, date, and time of the authorized individual accepting or releasing the equipment/process.
The signature, date, and time of the authorized individual (Commissioning HSE Manager) ordering the equipment/process restored to service.
The signature, date, and time of the authorized individual removing the tags/locks and restoring the equipment/process to service.
An Authorized list (Form FO2) will be prepared and maintained by the Commissioning HSE Manager for use on the site. The list will include only those knowledgeable appointed individuals to perform the required actions necessary to maintain an effective program.
All information required on the tags will be filled in before the tags are attached. At a minimum, this will include the following:
An order number designating the year and the sequential number of the respective order- (example 98-001).
The date.
The name of the authorized individual for whom the tagout is performed.
An accurate identification of the equipment/process.
The work to be performed.
When more than one authorized individual requests safety tagout on the same equipment/process, a separate safety tagout order will be prepared and separate lock & tags will be applied for each person except when “Group Safety Tagout is Approved.
Group Safety Tagout is acceptable when the requirements to perform work by more than one authorized individual on a given equipment/process can safely and effectively be accomplished by a single safety tagout order. In this instance the following actions will be accomplished:
Commissioning HSE Manager will Lock Out Specific Energy isolating devices, then place his keys into a (Lock Box with a clear lid).
Each affected employee will then place a lock on the box and keep their key.
A list will be kept of each individual participating in “Group Lock Out”.
Restoration of equipment/process will be as outlined previously in this procedure.
Danger tags block and prohibit the operation of equipment. A danger tag will be placed on each properly positioned energy-isolating device to warn employees that the device will not be operated. Lockout devices will be used in conjunction with Danger tag in all cases where lock out is possible. (Exceptions to this requirement can only be waived by the Commissioning HSE Manager) and then only if it can be shown that equivalent measures and procedures have been initiated to provide for the safety of all affected personnel.
A danger tag may be used without a lock but a lock will never be used without a danger tag.
A danger tag/lockout will not be placed or maintained on a piece of restored equipment.
A piece of equipment will not be restored until all appropriate danger tags/lockout devices have been properly released.
No employee will perform any work on any equipment/process under safety tagout except that such work is specifically covered by a safety tagout clearance and as specifically directed by the authorized individual identified on the safety tagout order.
Extension control tags will be used in conjunction with danger tags as required and are placed on remote operating devices to advise affected employees of the presence of a safety tagout at the source. They are used for informational purposes only and will contain the safety tagout order number and the name of the authorized individual as it appears on the safety tagout order.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will conduct a monthly review of the tagging system logbook. A more frequent review will be performed if deemed necessary. This review will consist of:
Checking the active section of the logbook against the index to ensure accountability of all active tagging orders.
A spot check of active tagging orders to verify compliance and trends of noncompliance with the procedure.
A spot check of tags to ensure that the tags are still attached, that they are on the correct equipment, and that the equipment status is per the tagging order.
M.      Lockout/tagout devices will be durable, and standardized for each site, this can be by marking, color code, dyed and used exclusively for safety tagout.

## Procedure Instructions
Prior to the beginning of any work, a survey will be performed by an authorized individual to identify any/all energy sources and related exposures to determine if the need for safety tagging is required.
At any time that work progresses to the point where the potential exists for an energy source to be applied to the work (such as with new construction or modifications), the safety tagout program will be initiated.
To initiate a tagout on equipment/process, the authorized individual responsible for the work will prepare the appropriate order, identify the equipment/process, detailing the work to be performed, and listing any special instructions and/or considerations. The authorized individual will also clearly identify the order of each manipulation, the resultant position of each energy isolating device, and the necessity for locks and /or grounds, which may be required.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will review the order to ensure that adequate safety is being provided for all affected employees and/or equipment involved specific to the work to be performed or the instructions to be followed and will make and discuss any corrections as will be required with the authorized individual. The Commissioning HSE Manager will approve the order and assign the sequential order number.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will arrange and be responsible for isolating the equipment/process and placing the appropriate tags & locks on each energy isolating device, and will verify and assure the proper position of each energy isolating device and that all tags/locks are placed at the correct points per the order.
Upon notification that tagout is completed, the authorized individual responsible for the work will perform an inspection to verify that all energy isolating devices are properly set and that all danger tags or caution tags are correctly placed to meet safety requirements, also that any physical blocks and protective grounds have been placed per the tagout order. Checking to ensure that the equipment/process is in a zero energy state.
After ensuring that safety requirements are met, the authorized individual responsible for the work will then sign the safety tagout or caution order accepting a clearance.
Any safety tagout order clearance issued to an authorized individual will remain in effect until it is properly released per this procedure. Danger tags will not be removed nor the equipment restored to service until the equipment/process has been released by the authorized individual named on the appropriate order.
Danger tags will be applied or removed only in an authorized manner by authorized individuals. Prior to releasing safety tagging, the authorized individual responsible for the work will inspect the work to assure all work is complete and all affected individuals are clear.
The authorized individual will notify the Commissioning HSE Manager that the work is complete and will sign under “Release Authorization” on the appropriate order.
Should any authorized individual accepting a clearance become unavailable for any reasons prior to release of the clearance, that person’s supervisor and the Commissioning HSE Manager will have the responsibility for satisfying the requirements of items J and K, and jointly ordering the equipment/process restored to service.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will order the equipment/process restored only after the completed work inspection and release authorization has been signed:
The Commissioning HSE Manager will inspect the work and confirm the work is complete before ordering the equipment restored.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will indicate concurrence by signing the appropriate order and ordering the equipment/process restored to service.
Restoring the equipment to service will be the responsibility of the Commissioning HSE Manager.

## Shop Equipment
The qualified operator of shop equipment must lock out his equipment to change tools, chucks, blades, and perform similar tasks.  A power disconnect switch must be provided for this purpose at or near the equipment unless the equipment can be unplugged.
Push-button or butterfly controls will not be used for purposes of lockout.  A lock without a tag may be used for this purpose.  (The above does not apply to any maintenance or repair work that is done by other than the authorized operator.)

## Locks
A master series of locks to be used specifically for lockout may be provided to each craft that requires them. Only individual keyed locks will be used.  The key will remain in the possession of the person placing the lock.

## Warnings
Due to the serious consequences of not following proper lockout procedures, disciplinary action for failure to follow procedures will be severe.
Any person who operates a switch or device to which "DANGER" tags and/or locks are attached or removes a tag or lock without authorization will be subject to immediate termination.
The same will apply to any person who works on power driven equipment without following this procedure.
Prior written approval from the Site Safety Manager will be the only acceptable way to deviate from this procedure.
