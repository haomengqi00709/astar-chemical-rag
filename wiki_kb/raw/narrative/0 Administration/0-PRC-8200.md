# 0-PRC-8200 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8200_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure describes the process for an orderly response to an emergency at the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Technology Centre located at 8515 Eastlake Drive, Burnaby, B.C., V5A 4T7
The following sections cover the anticipated emergency situations that could be encountered in the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Technology Centre (AKCTC). They outline typical and recommended courses of action. It is recognized that the Emergency Co-ordinator will be in charge during emergency situations and, as warranted by circumstances, may take actions that could deviate from the actual procedure.

## Responsibilities
The Emergency Co-ordinator (ECO) will normally be (in order of priority):
AKCTC Supervisor
Core Members of the Safety Sub-Committee
ECO shall be responsible for contacting, if necessary, external emergency services and relevant government agencies. He/she will also handle any inquiries from press or other mass media.
It is mandatory that all AKCTC personnel become familiar with the location of all emergency equipment and facilities. This will include Fire Extinguishers, Safety Showers, Eye-Wash Stations, Respirators, as well as First Aid Cabinets, First Aid Personnel, Emergency Exits and how to dial for outside help (dial 9-1-1 directly for Fire Dept., Ambulance and Police). The location of the above items can be found on the AKCTC Floor Plan, posted at the entrance to Fax/Xerox nook.

## First Aid
All injuries, no matter how small, must be reported to a certified first aider as soon as possible. Only personnel with a valid First Aid (level 1) Certificate shall be administering first aid and are permitted to remove items from the first aid cabinet located in the AKCTC lunchroom.   A list of personnel holding a valid First Aid certificate shall be posted on the first aid cabinet.
If outside help is required follow the procedure outlined in Medical Emergency (4).

### Accident and Injury Reporting and Recording Procedure
All injuries shall be reported to the HSE Management Committee.
Appendix G in the Lab Safety Manual contains an overview of the OSHA criteria for recording work related injuries and illnesses.  These criteria could be used as a guide by the first aider to assess any accidents and/or injuries that are reported to him/her.   Although the OSHA assessment and reporting guidelines are comprehensive, in B.C. the WCB regulations shall prevail and the WCB-issued report forms shall be used when reporting and recording any accidents and/or injuries.  Forms 6A (Worker’s Report of Injury or Occupational Disease to Employer), 7 (Employer’s Report of Injury or Occupational Disease), and 7A (First Aid Report) shall be filled out and submitted according to WCB regulations.  Copies of these forms are attached in Appendix H of the AKCTC Safety Manual.

## Medical Emergency
The initial responsibility for aid rests with the first person at the accident scene. This person shall act quickly but in a calm and reassuring manner. First Aid and/or outside medical help shall be summoned immediately. For outside help call 9-1-1 and ask for an ambulance. Arrange for someone to meet and direct the outside help. An injured person should not be moved, except when necessary, to prevent further injury.
The emergencies that can be anticipated in the laboratory include:
Thermal and chemical burns.
Cuts and puncture wounds from glass or metal, including possible chemical contamination.
Skin irritation by chemicals.
Poisoning by ingestion, inhalation or skin absorption.
Asphyxiation (chemical or electrical).
Injuries to eyes from splashed chemicals.
In the case of chemical exposure, procedures for treatment of specific medical emergencies can be found under the Emergency and First Aid section in the MSDS of the chemical in question. If not enough information is available, call the Emergency telephone number located on the MSDS.
Other medical emergencies requiring prompt First Aid assistance are:
Treatment for Electrical Shock,
Cardiac Arrest,
Stoppage of Breathing,
Heavy Bleeding, and
Shock (which often accompanies severe injury).
In all cases of injury or illness, the attending person(s) should be aware of, or look for emergency medical identification around the victim’s neck, wrist, or in his/her wallet.

## Fire Response
In Case of Fire Alarm (smoke detector, fire siren, or verbally announced)
If it is safe to do so:
Turn off heat to any on-going experiments and if necessary quench with water
Turn off water supplies if not necessary
Lower sashes of fume hoods
Leave via nearest exit and report to the Emergency Assembly Point. Remain in the area until “all-clear” signal is given, or unless otherwise instructed.
If you have an outside visitor, you are responsible for their safety. Visitors shall accompany their host to the Emergency Assembly Point.
Specific Experimental Units
Each specific experimental unit (e.g. ClO2 generator) shall have a documented operating procedure. This procedure shall include “Emergency Shut Down” instructions.
Minor Fires
In the event of a minor fire, AKCTC personnel shall:
Alert other personnel in the area.
If you are alone do not attempt to put out the fire.
Shut off electrical equipment in use as quickly as possible, leaving all work in progress in a safe condition.
With the help of others attack the fire as soon as possible. Get the nearest fire extinguisher (or hose) and attempt to put it out.
Keep in mind that chlorate fires should be extinguished with water only.
Currently, the dry chemical extinguishers we have are deemed suitable for all other types of laboratory fires.
Major Fires
In the event of major fire, AKCTC personnel shall:
Activate the fire siren and/or announce the fire through the intercom.
Dial 9-1-1.
Immediately turn off power to the lab from the outside electrical room.
If possible, assist injured personnel.
Close all doors to confine/contain the fire before leaving the building to report to the Emergency Assembly Point
Remain in the area until “all clear” signal is given, or unless otherwise instructed.

### Fire Drills
Semi-annual Fire Drills will be conducted to test the personnel’s readiness for a real emergency situation. The designated Fire Warden (or his deputy) will organize the drill. He/she will also have the responsibility for conducting the head count and calling off the drill. Personnel and immediate neighbours will be advised about the date (but not the time) of the drill. An audible means (e.g. fire horn) will be used to initiate the drill. The date of the drill and results shall be recorded in the minutes of the following AKCTC Safety Meeting.

### Fire Extinguishers
Multipurpose dry chemical fire extinguishers are available throughout the building and their location is properly marked. These extinguishers are inspected and serviced yearly by an outside company, namely Vancouver Fire and Security. A service tag is placed on each extinguisher to mark the next service date. Impromptus Burnaby Fire Hall inspections also help ensure that a preventative fire safety attitude is being maintained year-round. Staff is responsible for familiarizing themselves with the location of the fire extinguishers in order to be able to access the closest one in the event of a fire.

## Explosions
In the event of an explosion, AKCTC personnel shall:
Alert other personnel in the area
Assist injured personnel or summon first aid help (including Ambulance Service, if warranted).
If necessary, announce a fire alarm and follow the Major Fire procedure.

## Evacuation Procedure
In case of a major emergency, it may be necessary to evacuate the premises. Normally, evacuation will be ordered by the ECO and broadcasted through the Technology Centre by means of the intercom. All personnel must evacuate and proceed to the Emergency Assembly Point for headcount and further instructions.
The Emergency Assembly Point is the postal outlet of the Lake City Industrial Park (corner of a unit directly opposing our west back entrance and west from ADT).
The ECO will immediately conduct a headcount. If not all lab personnel (including visitors) are accounted for, the ECO will co-ordinate the re-entry and rescue effort with external emergency services (e.g. Fire Dept., HAZMAT team) to assist with the evacuation of those who were unable to leave on their own. Notwithstanding the above, every individual shall evacuate on his/her own, if faced with an uncontrollable situation. He/she should then contact the ECO (e.g. by re-entering the Centre from the office side) and report on the nature of emergency.

## Earthquake
All personnel must be familiar with the exit paths and the safe areas in the building. Familiarity with the evacuation and emergency shutdown procedures is mandatory.
All top-heavy shelving, cabinets, and computers shall be properly secured during initial installation. Regular inspections will reveal any deficiencies.
During the earthquake, it is recommended that all personnel move away from windows and tall shelves and/or structures. Take shelter under a strong desk or stand in a corner or doorway and watch for falling fixtures.
After the shaking stops, first check for injuries, but do not move seriously injured persons unless they are in immediate danger. Apply first aid if requires, then report to the emergency assembly point for roll call and directions.

## Spill Management

## Chemical Spills
If an accidental release of a chemical occurs, AKCTC personnel shall:
Alert other personnel of potential hazard
Turn off any heat sources and ventilate the area as necessary.
Use proper protective equipment (e.g. gloves and personal respirators, face shields, etc.).
Attempt to contain the release in order to counteract, neutralize or absorb the chemical. See MSDS for cleanup methods.
Depending on the severity of the spill, call 9-1-1 (Fire Department) and follow Major Fire procedure.
Refer to Table 1 “Determination of Materials and Agencies Requiring Notification” (Appendix O of the AKCTC safety Manual) to determine if a spill is reportable and which agency(ies) should be notified. This is an official guideline from the B.C. Waste Management Act (B.C. Reg. 166/93) on “Spill Reporting Regulation” and must be complied with in the province of British Columbia.
If the release is to the environment, i.e. sewer or outside the building, and if it is substantial, cordon-off the spill area and call City of Burnaby Chemical Spill/Sewer Emergency 24 hours line at (604) 294-7200. The AKCTC Supervisor should preferably do the latter.
If you call, be prepared to provide the dispatcher the following information:
Your name and contact phone number;
Name and telephone number of the person who caused the spill;
Location and time of the spill;
Type and quantity of the substance spilled;
Cause and effect of the spill;
Details of action taken or proposed;
Description of the spill location and surrounding area;
Names of agencies on scene, and;
Names of other persons or agencies advised concerning the spill.
If you are unsure of the answer to any of these questions, respond by saying that you do not know at this time.
If outside agencies were notified, contact the Company’s president. If unavailable, contact the HSE Manager, Legal Counsel, or the VP-Finance, in that order.
Gas Release
If a gas release occurs, depending on the severity (i.e. type of gas and quantity), AKCTC personnel shall:
Alert others and evacuate the area if necessary.
Using appropriate protective equipment, remove or turn off the source of release. If possible, isolate, neutralize or absorb the release.
If the release is life threatening (e.g. HCl, Cl2, etc.), evacuate the building (follow Major Fire procedure)
Alert neighbouring businesses and, if necessary, evacuate.
If the release is to the environment and is substantial, report the accidental gas emission to Burnaby’s City Hall (Environmental Emergencies Department at 604-294-7200). The AKCTC Supervisor should preferably do the latter.
Refer to Appendix O, Table 1 “Determination of Materials and Agencies Requiring Notification” (in the AKCTC Safety Manual) to determine which other agency(ies) should be notified. This is an official guideline from the B.C. Waste Management Act (B.C. Reg. 166/93) on “Spill Reporting Regulation” and must be complied with in the province of British Columbia.
