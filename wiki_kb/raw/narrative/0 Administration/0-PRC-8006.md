# 0-PRC-8006 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8006_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure outlines the process to be used to involve the employee in the identification and elimination of potentially hazardous conditions.

## Definitions
Task Safety Awareness (TSA) – Pre-task review of the tools, safety equipment, and the best methods to remove risk of injury, or risk of environmental damage. This process is carried out by the employees charged with doing a task with the help of their supervisor and/or the HSE manager.
TSA is the basis of an awareness campaign intended to re-enforce and ensure employees and supervisors execute their joint responsibility to analyse each work assignment for hazards, and to agree on sound safety practices before beginning a work assignment.

## Scope
Task Safety Awareness (TSA) will be applied in all areas of operations before commencing a new task.

## Responsibilities

### Supervisors
A supervisor assigning a task to any person or group shall identify necessary instructions and precautions (from a prior Job Hazard Analysis) that will provide the maximum level of safety for each employee in the performance of the assigned task.
The site HSE manager/supervisor will review existing Job Hazard Analysis with the employees prior to the start of a task.
It is the responsibility of the Supervisors to TEACH the employee how to perform a task safely. In performing a TSA the supervisor is demonstrating to each employee the appropriate safety protocols that pertain to the task they are assigned to perform.
The supervisor will ensure that each employee thoroughly understands each safety instruction provided on tasks they are to perform.

### Employees
Participate in the Task Safety Awareness before beginning a new task.
The employee will ask for TSA review and instruction from their supervisor if such instruction was not given.
The employee will follow recommended safe practices and perform the work with due care and attention.
The employee will wear appropriate personal protection equipment at all times.

## Job Pre-Start Orientation

### Orientation of Supervisors
Each new supervisor upon assignment to the project will receive an orientation that fully explains the principles and steps of Task Safety Awareness.
HSE Managers, Craft Superintendents and Department Heads will provide TSA orientation for supervisors.
Each supervisor upon completion of TSA orientation will be required to sign and date a pledge poster for his/her craft or department.  The pledge poster will be kept in the Craft/Department office.

### Orientation of Employees
TSA concept and steps of application will be included in each project's New Employee HSE Orientation.  Each employee will be instructed not to start any work task without first discussing its potential safety hazards and precautions with their supervisor.

## Application Procedure
No job is to start until the employees involved in the task complete a Task Safety Awareness review and the TSA is posted at the location of the work.
In new or complicated tasks the HSE Manager will determine if a full Job Hazard Analysis is required, and where one is required, employees will be led by the site HSE Manager (or other qualified personnel) in performing a Job Hazard Analysis as described in:
Standard-0-PRC-8007 Job Hazard Analysis
If a Job Hazard Analysis has been done on the task previously, the task supervisor will review that JHA with the employees as part of the pre-task TSA and ensure that the employee have understood the safe working plan.
Otherwise, employees will use the TSA method to analyse common tasks performed repeatedly, and give more consideration to the TSA of tasks performed less frequently.
Note: Standard-0-REP-8006a “Task Safety Awareness Form” will be used as a check list during the TSA and will be used as a record for the TSA.

## TSA Records
Each project will develop a means for monitoring the TSA program to ensure it is being fully implemented.
Projects shall continuously stress the concepts, principles of application, and importance of TSA in Safety topics and meetings.
TSA records will be reviewed periodically during a project. The results of follow up will be used as a means of providing rewards in recognition of compliance or used to take proactive action in cases of non-compliance, and as a means of improving the TSA for subsequent tasks.

## TSA Promotion
Working safely requires a safety mindset.  That mindset will be reinforced at the site on a continual basis.  The following materials or sources for promotion of TSA awareness should be used at the work site:
Posters (steps of application)
Pledge Posters in each craft office
Banners (outdoor 10' x 42")
TSA Awareness Cards given to each supervisor
TSA Hard Hat Stickers
Forms

TSA Application Guidelines
Employees will analyse the task to be performed for hazards using the TSA method.
A TSA is not generally required for jobs where a Job Hazard Analysis (JHA) has been prepared immediately prior to the job in conjunction with the work team.
If a generic JHA is available, a TSA is necessary to ensure all hazards likely to be present during the task are considered.
A TSA will be necessary if conditions change or new workers get involved in the task.
No job is to start until the employee completes a Task Safety Awareness (TSA) review and has the TSA posted at the location of the work.
