# 0-PRC-8502 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8502_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this regulation is to ensure the safety and health of employees who enter or work in any type of enclosed space such as tanks, pipes, pits, vaults or similar confined spaces where an oxygen deficient or gaseous atmosphere or chemicals may exist or where flooding may occur or in such a space where emergency egress would be restricted.
This regulation will be followed by the Commissioning Team (Chemetics personnel, Contractors and Vendors rep) after a circuit, any system or equipment is handed over mechanically completed for commissioning by the Construction Manager to the Commissioning Manager.
When available, a Client’s regulation may be followed if the requirements and guidelines spelled out on Chemetics’ procedures are fully covered by the Client’s regulation.
Note: This procedure is based on Aker Kvaerner’s Confined Space Entry procedure issued in January of 2003 and found in the AK’s Safety Policy Manual section 7, item 7.05.

## Scope
This procedure applies to all personnel who may be required to enter tanks and other enclosed spaces as defined below.

## Responsibilities
The hazard assessment created for the task must be understood by all members of the confined space entry team.

### Commissioning HSE Manager
The Commissioning HSE Manager is responsible for the administration of the Confined Space Entry Permit system.
In case the Commissioning HSE Manager is temporarily away from site (i.e., R&R, site leave, sick leave, etc.), the Commissioning Manager will take over the administration of the Confined Space Entry system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will be responsible for issuing permits or, when applicable, for obtaining permits from the client issuing authority.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will establish and maintain a program that adequately informs Chemetics’ personnel of the existence, purpose and procedures of the Confined Space Entry system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager and the Entry Supervisor must sign a valid permit for this program.

### Entry Supervisor
The supervisor who is directly responsible for employees who work inside a confined space has the following responsibilities:
Knows hazard/possible hazardous condition(s) and possible behavioural effects of exposure. ( Reviews MSDS of chemicals found in the process)
Requests and obtains an entry permit from the Commissioning HSE Manager.
Completes a pre-entry check prior to the commencement of entry work.
Ensures that atmosphere has been tested to determine the oxygen level and concentration of flammable vapours, gases, and toxic contaminants. All atmospheric testing equipment will be calibrated according to the manufacturer's recommendations, tested and certified before each use with calibration gas.
Ensures that isolation/lock, tag, try procedures have been accomplished.
Restricts entry into a confined space where concentration limits are in excess of the following:
Flammable vapour or gas greater than 10 percent of the Lower Explosive Level (LEL).
Toxic values above the threshold value in accordance with the OSHA 29 CFR Part 1910; Subpart Z - Toxic and Hazardous Substances, including supplements of General Safety and Health Regulations.
An atmospheric oxygen concentration below 19.5 percent or greater than 23.5 percent.
Terminates entry upon completion of work, expiration of permit, or if a condition arises in or near the permit space that places occupants at risk. Ensures permits are reissued or updated before authorizing re-entry into the confined space.
Authorizes entrant to enter space with respect to work scope and assists in prevention of entrance by unauthorized persons into confined space.
Verifies all authorized entrants and standby attendants understand company policy, procedures, and the nature of the hazards of entry into the confined space. The supervisor must also ensure that all personnel are confined space trained and trained in the use of retrieval equipment and evacuation and rescue procedures.
Assures signage and/or tape-off of openings when not in use.
If the client or a third party is used for rescue purposes, the Commissioning HSE Manager must evaluate and ensure that the service being used has the capability to respond in a timely manner and has the capability to perform the rescue for the type of confined space work being performed.

### Authorized Entrants
The employee(s) required to enter a confined space has the following responsibilities:
All authorized entrants shall understand company policy, procedures, and the nature of the hazards of entry into confined spaces.
All personnel are confined space trained and trained in the use of retrieval equipment and the evacuation and rescue procedures prior to entry.
Obtains authorization from the Entry Supervisor to assure that he/she is an Authorized Entrant and to obtain authorization to enter a confined space.
Knows the hazards that may be encountered during entry and knows the mode, signs or symptoms, and consequences of any potential exposure.
Ensures that an entry permit has been issued for the confined space and that all requirements of the permit have been met.
Knows how to use provided retrieval equipment.
Knows procedures for communication with the attendants.

### Standby Attendant (Hole Watch)
All authorized attendants shall understand company policy, procedures, and the nature of the hazards of entry into confined spaces.
All attendants are confined space trained and trained in the use of retrieval equipment and the evacuation and rescue procedures prior to entry.
Knows the hazards that may be encountered during entry including information on the mode, signs or symptoms, and consequences of any potential exposure.
Maintains continuous count of authorized entrants in the confined space through the use of sign in/out rosters or tracking systems. Collects ID Cards of personnel in Confined Space until their exit.
Tends lifelines, keeps tools and materials immediately available and passes the materials to entrants in the confined space.
Remains outside the confined space to monitor the entrants (through voice or other means of communication), the confined space, and surrounding area for possible change in conditions that may have the potential for increased hazard.
Monitors ventilation equipment and atmospheric monitoring equipment, if necessary, to ensure that contaminated air does not enter the confined space.
The standby attendant will never enter the confined space to perform rescue. Attendant is to summon help (an air horn blast or other alarm) and proceed with rescue efforts from outside the confined space until help arrives. No entry for rescue will be done until trained rescue personnel arrive, and person or persons effecting rescue have donned appropriate safety equipment for confined space rescue.

## Training
Each employee who is authorized to perform confined space entry shall be thoroughly trained in all aspects of these guidelines. Everyone must go through Confined Space Entry Training provided by the Commissioning HSE Manager, either on Site or in Chemetics office.
Training shall be documented and entered into the Chemetics log book that the Commissioning HSE Manager keeps.
All Confined Space Entry incidents and near misses shall be thoroughly investigated and documented.

## Definition of a Confined Space
Confined spaces have limited or restricted openings for entry and exit, such as vessels, vats, ship holds, sewers, tunnels, large pipelines, and other such spaces that workers could enter to perform work.
The Occupational Health and Safety Regulation defines a "confined space" as an area, other than an underground working, that
is enclosed or partially enclosed,
is not designed or intended for continuous human occupancy,
has limited or restricted means for entry or exit that may complicate the provision of first aid, evacuation, rescue or other emergency response service, and
is large enough and so configured that a worker could enter to perform assigned work.

## Preparing to Enter a Confined Space
Before entering a confined space a written Hazard Assessment must be prepared by the Commissioning HSE Manager for the space of interest (see Confined Space Hazard Assessment form).
It is the responsibility of the designated “Competent Person” to perform the task safety analysis.  If there is an existing confined space entry protocol available that is applicable (because the space has been entered before) that plan must be reviewed by the “Competent Person” (defined below) on the confined space entry team.
The entry protocol shall include, but not be limited to:
Assignment of responsibilities
Written safe work procedures for entry into and work in the confined space, that address, where applicable:
identification and entry permits
lockout and isolation
verification and testing prior to entry
cleaning, purging, venting or inerting  and ventilation
standby persons
rescue
lifelines, harnesses and lifting equipment,
personal protective equipment and other precautions
coordination of work activities
personal protective equipment and lifeline/harness system.

## Health and Safety Considerations

### Hazards
Toxic vapours in unhealthy or fatal concentrations may result from residue of last material in tank by gradual release from sludge or scale, they may be introduced by leakage from interconnected systems, or introduced by use of cleaning solvents, or by welding, cutting, etc.  Flammable gases or dust have the potential of fire or explosion.
Other potential hazards include, but are not limited to:
Toxic vapours replace oxygen in confined space, resulting in an oxygen-deficient atmosphere.
Inert gas used to exclude oxygen from a specific area of work, such as when nitrogen is used to purge air (oxygen) from piping and vessels prior to start-up.
Air in clean tanks closed for an extended period may become deficient in oxygen because of oxidation of the tank’s metal.
Improper or inadequate ventilation during tank work may also result in a lack of oxygen.
Electric shock may result from portable lights, tools, or other improperly grounded electrical equipment.
Injury may result from mechanical equipment that may be defective or incorrectly locked out.
Difficulty may be encountered in removing workers who are seriously injured or lose consciousness.
Injury may result from direct contact with corrosive chemicals or dermatitis-producing chemicals.
Physical hazards such as slipping, falling, or falling objects may exist.

### Personal Protective Equipment
Supervisors and all personnel who may be required to enter vessels and other confined spaces shall review this instruction to ensure compliance with all applicable paragraphs.
Personnel with respiratory problems shall not be permitted in tanks.
All personal protective apparel and respiratory equipment shall be worn when entering any hazardous enclosure as required.
All personal protective apparel and respiratory equipment will be maintained in good condition, preferably stored in ventilated cabinets and cleaned or sanitized according to good housekeeping and hygiene standards.

### Confined Space Entry Guidelines
The hazards inherent in vessel entry can be avoided or overcome if the following principles are applied properly each and every time a vessel is entered.
Preparation of Space - The space should be thoroughly prepared prior to the entry of personnel.
Supervisors over the area in which vessels to be entered are located and supervisors responsible for other work conducted in such areas shall coordinate and regulate operations of their organizations in a manner which minimizes hazards to personnel entering such vessels.
Supervisors contemplating entry of personnel into vessels shall consult the Commissioning HSE Manager.  No deviations from the safety requirements specified in these instructions will be permitted without prior approval from the Commissioning HSE Manager.
Cleaning - According to the nature of the contents, the vessel should be emptied and made clean and void of residue by hot or cold water flush, by steaming, by chemical neutralization or by air purge, with harmful vapours vented safely to the outdoors where practicable.
Ventilation - The vessel should be thoroughly ventilated preferably by some positive method of mechanical exhaust to avoid recirculating contaminated air.  All ventilation or air moving equipment shall be grounded to the vessel to avoid static sparks.  After the vessel is cleaned and ventilated, the mechanical exhaust system should be kept operating.  Should decontamination involve flammable liquids, vapours, gases or dusts, all sources of ignition shall be eliminated.
Isolation - The vessel should be completely isolated from all other systems and equipment. Positive and adequate measures must be taken to prevent harmful material (solids, liquid or vapour) from entering the vessel while workers are inside. In all cases where lines have contained hazardous fluids or inert gas, or where they contain fluids at high pressures or temperatures, the lines should be physically disconnected and isolated by placing a blank in the lines as close to the tank as practical.
Drains or overflow lines should be disconnected or blanked if they extend into a sewer from which steam or fumes could be conducted back into the vessel or if they connect to other equipment or extend to a remote location.
All lines to be disconnected or blanked or capped to prevent flow from disconnected ends entering a vessel opening or draining on to assistants on the outside.  All blanks shall be sufficient thickness and tensile strength to withstand maximum pressures that may be imposed and of material which will withstand corrosion of chemicals to which they may be exposed.
Lock Out - Motor control centre disconnect switches supplying power to any mechanical mixers, agitators, pumps and cover or door actuators should be tagged and locked in the "OFF" position.  It is not adequate to lock a local push-button station as it still may be possible to energize the circuit.  The man in the vessel should keep the key, and he alone shall be authorized to unlock the switch and remove the tag on completion of the job.  Where more than one man is exposed, each should place his own lock on the switch.  In certain cases, it may be advisable to have an electrician pull the line fuses in the electrical circuit in addition to lock and tag requirements.  Refer to procedure 0-PRC-8504-RA Lockout and Tagging.
Testing Oxygen and Gas - Before entry is made into any vessel, a test for oxygen and both flammable and toxic gases shall be made and a vessel Entry Permit (form DP-802.705-1) issued.  The last substance contained in the vessel must be considered for complete gas testing. Residue from the substance can leave or generate toxic fumes that normal O2/combustible gas check will not detect.  Under no condition will entry be made until this procedure is carried out and all potential gases and contaminants are checked for.
Gas testing will be done prior to any entry, and at intervals of one hour during the work/inspection of the confined space.
Issuance of Entry Permits - The Entry Permit (form DP-802.705-1) certifies and records approval for vessel (enclosed or confined space) entry.  It serves as a method of formalizing agreed upon procedures and also as a checklist to ensure that all existing hazards are considered, evaluated and correct protective measures taken.  No employee shall enter any vessel without reporting to and securing written approval from the immediate supervisor and the Commissioning HSE Manager.  The supervisor shall discuss the project in detail with those entering the vessel.  He should review the hazards of the product or other materials to which they may be exposed and document this training.  The supervisors of all men involved shall then review and complete the authorization form jointly.  An Entry Permit is not a work permit and is valid only for entry and inspection of a vessel or confined space, etc.  Should it be
necessary to perform Hot Work inside a vessel, both an entry and Hot Work Permit will be required prior to commencing work.  Appropriate supervisors must ascertain by personal investigation immediately before vessel entry and before signing the authorization that the entry and incidental work will be safe.  To ensure continuation of safe conditions if a break of appreciable duration occurs during the job, the authorizing supervisors must reinvestigate the site before the job can be allowed to resume.  Commencement of a new shift will always require reinvestigation and endorsement of a new authorization by new shift supervision.  No authorization shall be valid except for the job, location, persons and time specified.
Precautions - Hand tools shall be clean, in good condition and shall be selected carefully according to the uses intended.  Electrical tools and equipment such as hand lamps and extension cords must be on a 12 volt system or plugged into a working Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter (GFCI).  In any Class I or Class II exposure (in dangerous flammable dust concentration), use air-operated power tools.  Ladders used in tanks should be lashed at the top and, if possible, at the bottom. Employees performing electrical welding in tanks shall be provided with and required to use such personal protective equipment as may be required.  Welding and cutting torches must not be taken into the tank until ready to be used and must be removed from the tank immediately after use.  Cylinders of oxygen or other gas shall never be taken into tanks, and they should be turned off at the cylinder valve when not in use. Fully charged and inspected fire extinguisher must be readily available.
Air power tools shall not be used in confined spaces if the air system is backed with nitrogen or other inert gases.
Signs - Signs posted near the vessel help keep unnecessary people away, ensure that potentially harmful operations will not be started independently nearby and help guide rescuers should to the scene if necessary.
Class I locations are those enclosed areas in which flammable, volatile liquids, gases and vapours or mixtures are or may be present in quantities sufficient to produce explosive or ignitable mixtures.
Class II locations are those enclosed areas which are hazardous because of the presence of combustible dust.
Barricades - Open pits and hazardous vessel operations require barricading with substantial railing 42" high with toe boards or equivalent protection to prevent kicking objects laying on the floor or ground into the pit.  Rope with streamers at the 42" level is recommended for closing aisles in adjacent areas.  Flashing lights on barricades are necessary when pits are open at night or in dimly lighted areas inside.
Personal Protective Equipment - Proper protection may range from specially designed, complete coverage suits for protection against highly toxic chemicals (harmful by skin absorption), chemical goggles, hard hats, gloves, and safety shoes normally worn to protect against routine hazards.  Employees should never unduly expose the skin when working in vessels, and they should wear full coverage clothing at all times.  The minimum PPE to be worn will be specified in the Confined Space Entry Hazard Analysis.
Respirators – Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA) or certified breathing air for self-contained units shall be supplied if the integrity of the atmosphere cannot be guaranteed and if there is any possibility of harmful air contamination or of oxygen deficiency while employees are within a tank.  Air-purifying half-mask respirators, which operate on the principle of chemical absorption or mechanical filtration, may not provide adequate protection and should not be used.  For specifically recommended protection from hazards of contaminating materials, refer to the Commissioning HSE Manager and the applicable MSDS sheets available on Site.
Respirators will be issued to individuals who are responsible for their safe and sanitary keeping.  Respirators shall be sanitized before they are re-issued to other individuals.
Emergency and Rescue - Where potential exposure in the vessel is acute or requires an employee to wear respiratory protection or where rescue may be difficult, the employee must be provided with a body harness with a life line attached.  It is good practice to use a body harness for any vessel entry work.  Such safety harness and life line should be used when the shape, size and location of the vessel space prohibits the safe emergency removal of workers by standby personnel without requiring entry into the tank.
When the shape, size and location of the vessel does not meet this requirement, a specific procedure for the rescue of personnel must be developed in the pre-planning stage of the vessel entry work and made a specific part of the vessel entry permits.  In some cases, it is advisable to have a block and tackle positioned on a tripod or otherwise fastened above the manhole.  For obvious reasons, manholes large enough to accommodate the man and his safety gear should be provided, such as circular manholes of 24" in diameter.  Where existing manholes are smaller than 20" in the largest dimension, consideration shall be given for possible use of wrist harnesses.
The free end of the lifeline should be secured to a fixed object and should be attended by an observer (standby man) who will keep the man in the tank always within sight or verbal communication.  The observer may pass tools, but he must have no other job which will take his attention away from the man in the vessel, which will interfere with his attempts to withdraw the victim by use of the lifeline, or which will require his leaving the vicinity of the vessel for any length of time.
In case of emergency, the outside observer must never enter the vessel until he is relieved at his post.  It is his job to summon aid immediately, to attempt to remove the victim by use of the lifeline and to perform all other necessary rescue functions from outside.  Upon arrival of help, he may enter the vessel for rescue work only when he is assured that adequate outside assistance is present and he wears all prescribed protective equipment.  Such safety observer or standby man should be well-trained in basic First Aid principles such as rescue breathing techniques.  Rescuers entering the vessel must be protected with the safety equipment required by the situation, i.e., lifeline and harness and proper personal protective and respiratory equipment.
For rescue purposes, at least one unit of self-contained breathing equipment should be located outside the confined space and adjacent to the vessel, together with harness, rope and such other emergency equipment as may be indicated.  The number of men in a tank, particularly at the time of burning or welding, must be reduced to the absolute minimum necessary to perform the work.
A clearly understood signal system shall be established prior to the start of the operation:
Three Pulls - Allow more slack in the line
Two Pulls - Take up slack in line
One Pull - Emergency, pull man from tank
No one shall enter a confined space until a properly trained and equipped standby man is present.  In emergency situations persons working in vessels should exit only through the entry point.
One additional safety line will be available for each person within the vessel so that in the event the entry man has fouled his on a structural member to such an extent that the clearing of the line to perform a rescue would be time consuming and dangerous, the life line to the man will be unsnapped.  A new lifeline will be hooked on and a rescue effected.
It will be the responsibility of the Commissioning HSE Manager to determine the environmental condition inside the vessel and prescribe the necessary precautions required for safe vessel entry as outlined herein.
A maximum number of men permitted to work within a vessel at any one time will be defined by Commissioning HSE Manager in every particular case. The standby man shall keep an updated account of how many persons are in the vessel at all times.
While personnel are inside the vessel, a qualified and certified standby man shall observe the operation from outside the tank.  Standby men shall have entry safety equipment available.  The standby man shall be aware of the condition of personnel within the vessel at all times.  The standby man shall not leave his position until the vessel is vacated or other suitably attired and certified personnel relieve him.
The supervisors concerned must be alerted before and during the job to detect and immediately correct new and different hazards, or to stop the work until the new hazards are corrected.
