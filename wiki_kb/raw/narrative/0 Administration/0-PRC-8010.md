# 0-PRC-8010 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8010_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure describes the process for an orderly response to an emergency at the  office located at 1818 Cornwall Avenue, Vancouver.
The scope of this procedure is the evacuation of floor 3 and floor 4 during an emergency that can potentially affect the structural integrity of the building (such as file, earthquake and bomb threat). This procedure assigns specific responsibilities to employees during an emergency situation, or simulated emergency situation.
A building safety plan and regular fire drills are required by local regulations.  The building owner is required to develop and administer the building safety plan.  , as one of many tenants in the building, is obliged to participate in the plan.
This procedure does not replace the building emergency procedure provided by the building owners to all tenants. Instead, this procedure focuses on the safety  employees and visitors, and on the assignment of duties and responsibilities to  employees.

## Emergency Procedures

### General Emergency
If one of your co-workers loses consciousness, experiences anything that seems like heart, stroke or other potentially serious illness or injury, use the Emergency Contact information, found on the first page of this manual, to get help.

### Fire Emergency
Be alert for signs of a fire.
If you see smoke, flames, or smell smoke, do the following immediately:
Isolate fire by closing doors.
Pull Fire Alarm to signal a building evacuation. Alarm pulls are located at stairwell exits.
Report the fire. (see Emergency contacts at the front of this manual)
Building is to be evacuated as per instructions under section “4. Building Evacuation Plan”.
Fire extinguishers are located at all floor exits, at all coffee stations, as well as at various locations through out the building. The locations of fire extinguishers in open areas are marked with a red pyramid mounted high on a building column or wall.
A fire is easy to put out with a fire extinguisher at its initial stage.
Should you decide to attempt to put out a small fire using a fire extinguisher, then
USE GOOD JUDGEMENT and PUT YOUR PERSONAL SAFETY FIRST.
DO NOT go out of your way to fight a fire.
DO NOT enter enclosed spaces to fight a fire. Instead, close doors to isolate the fire.
Be sure that your way out is clear if you decide to take action to extinguish a fire.

### Bomb Threat Emergency
Bomb threats, whether in the work place or at home, should always be taken seriously. The following information are the general guidelines for response to such threats. Remember that your life, the lives of your family, friends, and/or co-workers may depend on every action taken when faced with this dangerous situation.  Situation assessment is the key to managing the threat.
Upon receiving a threatening call:
Have a co-worker, relative, or friend listen to the conversation on another phone or extension if possible.
Keep the caller on the line as long as you can.
Remain calm and concentrate on what you must do in the moments to come.
Take notes of what you hear.
Have the caller repeat the message clearly while you write the entire message word for word.
Ask for the caller's name and location - you never know which individual(s) will give it to you.  Law enforcement authorities claim that some perpetrators want to be caught.  This is the perfect opportunity to help authorities do so.
Be sure to ask the caller what the device looks like, where it is, and when it is set to detonate.
Always remind the caller that the bomb will most likely harm innocent people, possibly even children.
Pay close attention to the caller's voice in order to determine their sex, accent, overall language ability, etc.
Listen for any background noises that could help law enforcement authorities locate the origin of the call.
Report the threat immediately to:
The police by dialling 9-911
Building management at the number on the first page of this document.
HSE manager.
DO NOT PANIC. DO NOT PULL THE FIRE ALARM. Follow any additional instructions you’re given in step 11.

### Earthquake Emergency
The severity of an earthquake largely determines the response that is required. The following are some guidelines of what to do in case of an earthquake.
During an earthquake event, all personnel shall:
DROP - COVER - HOLD.
Protect yourself by dropping to the floor and taking cover under a desk, sturdy table or other piece of furniture. Hold on to whatever you are under. If taking cover under a sturdy piece of furniture is not possible, get into a corner and facing out, bring your knees and hands up to protect yourself. Stay away (and face away) from windows (do not stand in a doorway because you become a silhouette for a shrapnel hazard). Stay away from anything that can shatter or fall on you. Count out loud (one-one thousand, two-one thousand, three-one thousand) to give yourself a time reference and to assure others around you that you are still in the equation.
Do not leave cover for at least 10 seconds after the shaking has stopped. Make sure it is not dangerous for you to come out from under cover (dangling light fixtures, broken glass, live electrical circuits in close proximity).
Cautiously leave your protected position and see if anyone needs help.
If ordered to evacuate, follow the instructions given.
After it is safe to leave their protected position, Floor Wardens shall:
Cautiously leave their protected position and begin to do an injury assessment. Try to plot your course around your floor space so that you are no more than a few steps from another safe spot (in case of an aftershock).
Do a complete check of your floor area, looking for injured or trapped persons, dangerous or shorting electrical circuits, damaged and leaking water lines, unstable walls, ceilings, or furniture. Reassure everyone and ask them to remain calm while you check for injuries.
Upon finding injured occupants, render first aid, if qualified. If not qualified, assist those rendering/requiring first aid.
Barricade off unsafe areas.
Check the integrity of the stairwells on your floor and prepare to evacuate if so ordered. An evacuation of your space will only be ordered if the building safety systems have been compromised, the structural integrity of the building has been compromised, or the building is on fire. Exterior evacuation of the building onto the street should be discouraged as it is more dangerous outside of the building following an earthquake than within it because of potential falling debris.
If ordered to evacuate, follow the instructions given.
In case of a severe earthquake, your primary evacuation assembly areas are on the lowest safe, accessible floor(s) in the building, unless full building collapse (unlikely) is imminent or the building is experiencing an uncontrollable fire on the lower floors, in which case a full evacuation to a safe area outside will be required. The exterior evacuation is a last resort and only to be used if staying inside the building is deemed to be less safe.
If faced with staying in the building for extended period, implement sanitation procedures by placing garbage cans in the washrooms with triple plastic garbage can liners to be used as temporary toilets until the integrity of the sewage systems are checked. (This is to ensure that raw sewage does not pour into the floor spaces below you, should the sewer lines be damaged.)

## Building Evacuation Plan
In the event that the building needs to be evacuated:
The fire alarm will ring. You may also be issued instructions over the public address system.
Employees are to proceed to the meeting area through the nearest emergency exit. See employee responsibilities below.

### Evacuation Meeting Area or Rally Location
In the event of an emergency evacuation of 1818 Cornwall, employees are to meet on the large grassy area to the East of the building (Peace Park), along Burrard Street.

## Responsibilities during an Evacuation
All employees have specific responsibilities during a building evacuation. Designated employees, the Floor Wardens and the Door Monitors have additional responsibilities during a building evacuation.

### Duties of employees not Assigned Specific Duties
Be sure you know the nearest exit for your location is at all times. EXIT signs identify evacuation directions and stairwell locations.
Emergency response personnel will be those people wearing an orange vest and orange hard hat.
Duties of employees and visitors when the FIRE ALARM sounds:
Leave the building immediately via a designated fire exit.  DO NOT use the elevators to evacuate.
Walk quickly, but DO NOT RUN, to the nearest exit stairwell and evacuate to the “Evacuation Meeting” location.
CAUTION – Open doors slowly and cautiously If the exit door is hot or the stairway is smokey, use and alternate emergency exit.
Keep to the right in corridors/halls.  Hold the handrail when going down the stairs. Merge alternately when two lines meet at various floor landings to keep lines moving.
DO NOT turn back at any time for any reason.
Keep conversation to a minimum. Follow all instructions from emergency personnel.
Look around you - be sure your co-workers and your visitors follow you during a building evacuation.
DO NOT loiter around the building after an emergency evacuation. Move away from the building as quickly as you can, and go to the “Evacuation Meeting” location.
DO NOT go to the parking garage and drive away.
Those who can not manage the stairs should proceed to the stairwell exit, or seek the assistance of an able person to assist in getting to the exit. The Door Monitor will provide assistance or arrange for assistance, to anyone requiring assistance descending the stairs.
Gather with those from your work group once you reach the rally location, and stay with them until notified that it is safe to return to the building.
Upon reaching the rally location, check to make sure you see the faces of the co-workers you know to be in the office. Report anyone found to be missing to the emergency staff.

### Floor Warden and Designated Backups
A Floor Warden is an employee who oversees the evacuation of a designated area during an emergency.
The primary responsibility of Floor Wardens is the evacuation process. They are not responsible of extinguishing fires.
Duties of a Floor Warden during an evacuation are:
Put on fire vest and hard hat to assist others in identifying your position as a member of the Fire Safety Team.  Carry flashlight to assist in the event of a possible power outage.
Supervise the evacuation of all employees and visitors from the designated area on your floor by "sweeping" all areas (corridors, meeting rooms, and washrooms).  Firmly, but politely, insist that all employees and visitors leave immediately. Ensure employees walk.  Check for any disabled person who may need assistance in exiting the building.
After all employees and visitors have been cleared from your area, leave the building through the nearest safe exit.
After exiting the building, report the status of your floor area to the  Chief Warden, at the front entrance to the building.  Report the presence of any disabled persons who may need assistance.  (In the absence of the Building Fire Safety Director, report to the Fire Department).
NOTE: Backup Floor Wardens are intended to provide redundancy should the primary Floor Warden be out of the office.  The duties are identical to those of the Floor Warden, and must be performed even if both the Floor Warden and backup are both present in the office. In case of an event that requires intervention causing a delay in clearing the building, the backup Warden is to proceed to the front door to the building on Cornwall Street, and report the event to the Chief Warden.

### Door Monitors
A Door Monitor is an employee who ensures an orderly exit during emergency evacuation of a floor area.  Each Door Monitor is assigned to a specific emergency exit.
Duties of a Door Monitor during a building evacuation are:
Put on fire vest and hard hat.  Carry a flashlight and go to your designated exit door.
Supervise the orderly evacuation of all employees and visitors to the rally location outside the building.
Provide assistance to anyone requiring assistance descending the stairs. If this is not possible, the door Monitor will send for help, and stay with the physically disabled person in the refuge area at the exit until the Fire Department Rescue team arrives.
Once evacuation of the floor to stairwell is complete, ensure door(s) to exit stairwell are closed (not left propped open) and exit. Report any unusual incident to Chief Warden at the front door to the building on Cornwall Street; otherwise there is no need to go to the front door.

### Receptionist
When fire alarm sounds, or when notified of an emergency requiring the evacuation of the building:
If in immediate danger:
Put the answering machine onto the nightline and unplug the receiver.  Vacate the building through the nearest exit.
Otherwise:
Inform any incoming external calls on the Switchboard:
"A fire alarm has been activated.  We are required to vacate the building.  Could you please call back in 20 minutes?” Be polite but firm.
Make the following announcement twice, with a brief pause in between (speaking calmly and clearly):
"Attention all employees - Due to a possible fire condition in the building, please evacuate your area by the nearest stairwell.  Do not use the elevators."
If time permits, change the message on the answering machine (or by inserting the "Fire Alarm" tape) to say:
"We are having a potential fire situation.  Sorry for the inconvenience.  Please call back in 20 minutes".
Put the answering machine onto the nightline and unplug the receiver.  Vacate the building through the nearest exit.
After returning to the switchboard, resume normal procedures.  Reset the answering machine message when time permits.
During a Fire Drill:
Advanced notice will be provided to the receptionist for all drills.
Same as above except do not evacuate the building and continue to operate the switchboard.
Notify callers using:
" is having a fire drill.  Could you please call back in 20 minutes?"

### Chief Warden
A Chief Warden is an employee who is the liaison between the  building emergency crew and the Chief Incident Commander for the building.
Duties of the Chief Warden:
Exit the building safely as any other employee and immediately go the ground floor storage area and put on the green vest and red hard hart, then go to the front entrance door.
Take the reports from each of the Floor Wardens as they arrive.
Check that all Floor Wardens and/or the backup Floor Warden have reported. If only one Warden is present ascertain the reason for the absence of the other. Use the checklist in Appendix A for a record.
Make a report to the Chief Incident Commander for the building.
Liaise with the Chief Incident Commander on any matters pertaining to the emergency.
Keep track of the time it took for the evacuation.
Help the Chief Incident Commander notify the people in the gathering area when the Vancouver Fire Chief has declared the building safe.
