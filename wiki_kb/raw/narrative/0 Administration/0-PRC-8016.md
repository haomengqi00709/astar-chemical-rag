# 0-PRC-8016 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8016_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this procedure is to outline the minimum training of Chemetics employees receive in company health safety, and environment procedures and requirements.

## General
Safety, quality, and efficiency depend on the proper performance of tasks which in turn depends on adequate employee training and skill development.
Employee education is an essential part of the Chemetics health, safety and environmental program.  Some types of training are required by health and safety regulatory agencies while others are determined on an as-needed basis by Management.
Also see Appendix 3.05, Decision Logic for Safety Training Program.

## Requirements

### Documentation and Recordkeeping
Records of all training will be kept.  The preferred method is to have the attendees signing training attendance records.  The records will indicate what method was used to determine trainee comprehension, i.e. written quiz, verbal quiz or observation.
All employees will complete Form DP-802.305 for each training course taken.  Record of completed courses will be added to work experience when verified with a copy of a certificate or training card issued at the end of a course.
Construction Managers / Site HSE Managers will develop and maintain a safety-training file on each management/supervisory employee using Form DP-802.305, Safety Training History Record.

### Safety Training Planning
Project Managers and Construction Managers will plan and budget safety training into all projects.  Training needs will be analyzed and identified so that management/supervisory training and education may be carried out in a timely manner for projects.  See Appendix 3.05, Decision Logic for Safety Training programs for guidance on this subject.

### Recognition of Training
Trained workers will receive a Certificate of Completion, or a decal to indicate that they have completed such training.

## General Training Requirements

### Health Safety and Environment Orientation
All new employees will go through a HSE orientation before commencing the job. This orientation will cover the Company’s commitment to Health Safety and the Environment, local health and safety information, and “must obey” safety rules for the worksite.  At the end of their orientation, all engineering and construction employees will be given a copy of the construction safety handbook.
See: Standard-0-PRC-8006 New Employee Orientation
After the standard safety orientation, employees will be trained to use the personal protection equipment provided for the tasks they will be performing.

## Other Training Requirements

### Special Employee Training
Employees will have a recognized training certificate for the equipment they will be operating, and have demonstrated capability in the operation of the equipment.  Training will be provided where necessary to achieve the necessary degree of competence.
Employees will be given all training required for the performance of new or identified critical tasks.
Other specific requirements will be addressed through task and work-group directed safety-training programs tailored to the particular needs or requirements of the employee.
A training inventory will be maintained by the HSE Manager to facilitate special needs situations.  Training programs should use lesson plans, training materials, visual aids, tests, and records of training and qualification where possible.

### Management Training
Management training provides the knowledge and skills necessary to manage control of the HSE program.
Senior Managers will receive an orientation which adequately covers its HSE responsibilities including reference to major program elements and standards, HSE leadership, the manager’s specific program responsibilities, and the critical activities of their subordinates.
Formal training should be provided to HSE Coordinators focusing on HSE Management concepts that emphasize proactive methods of controlling accidents and losses.

### Senior Management HSE Orientation
New members of senior management will be given 4 hours of orientation to the safety and health/loss control program. This training will cover:
All elements of the company's HSE program identified in the company's HSE policy document.
The purpose and practices of the program with an overview of the executive's specific accountability and responsibilities.
This briefing should occur during the first week of the senior manager's assignment and given by the senior manager's superior or his representative.  The manager will be given a copy of the company HSE Manual.

### Senior Management Formal Review Training
Ten hours of formal review training will be given to Senior Management personnel every three years following the date of original formal training.

### Supervisory/Middle Management HSE Orientation
Formal Initial Training will emphasize the applied aspects of on-the-job orientation of employees, task instruction, safety contacts, job risk analysis, employee training, group meetings, inspections, investigations, task observation as well as how to use the HSE Manual.

### Supervisory/Middle Management Review Training
Ten hours of formal review training will be given every three years following the date of original formal training.

### Continuing HSE education
Employees are encouraged to further their HSE skills and awareness through external training/educations means.  Prior to attending the company’s HSE Manager must approve courses.
