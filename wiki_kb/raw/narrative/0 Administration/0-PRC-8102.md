# 0-PRC-8102 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8102_table_1]


## Purpose
To detail the specific requirements to comply with the Aker Kvaerner “Travel Health, Safety and Security” Norm in ensuring the health safety and security of employees while on business travel.

## Authorization for Travel
Prior to travelling an employee must receive authorization for travel by submitting a travel requisition according to the process outlined in:
0-PRC-9002 Travel Authorisation

## Identification of Travel Risk
The travel requisition, once approved by the employee’s Department Manager, is distributed to a list of people which will include:
Travel coordinator
HSE Manager
Human Resources Manager
Corporate health services provider (external)
These people are responsible for reviewing the request and flagging those requests requiring further consideration as it relates to Health Safety and Security.

### Level of Risk
The level of risk associated with the travel destination will be based on the International SOS recommendations. These recommendations are found on the Web at:
http://www.internationalsos.com/Members_home/
Travel to regions of High or Extreme risk regions is to be closely controlled, and is to be approved as outlined below. Travel to regions of Moderate risk (especially for new travellers to the region) should follow this procedure.
The decision to permit travel will take into consideration the recommendations made by International SOS, the recommendations of the Government of Canada, any local intelligence information, and information that other Aker Kvaerner business units may have.

### Risk Assessment Responsibilities
The Business Unit President, Director of Technology, Director of Projects, Human Resources Manager, and HSE Manager are responsible for understanding the level of risk at any location where the company is doing business.
Travel to High or Extreme risk areas is to be approved by Business Unit President, and the Business Area Executive Vice President needs to be informed. The Director of Technology, Director of Projects, and HSE Manager will have joint responsibility for making the decision whether or not an employee is allowed to travel to High or Extreme risk area, and for determining what actions are required to minimize risk.
The Travel Coordinator will ensure the risk assessment has been done when it is required, and this information will be logged in the Travel Coordinator’s list of travellers.

## Travel Risk Management Responsibilities

### Travel Coordinator
The Travel Coordinator will ensure that a risk assessment has been made, and that the “Aker Kvaerner Travel, Health and Security Norm” has been followed.
The Travel Coordinator will also:
Maintain a list of all employees and their location while travelling
Coordinate with other business units or projects in the areas of common concern
For travellers to High risk and Extreme risk areas, send to International SOS the travellers contact information and travel details.
Communicate the International SOS security advisory to the employee
Inform the traveller about the travel safety resources available on the company Intranet

### Traveller Responsibilities
The traveller will:
Become familiar with itinerary route and contacts along the route
Take appropriate actions and precautions, such as vaccinations and risk management actions as recommended
Act in a manner that does not negatively affect the company’s reputation
Take care not to do anything that may contravene a local law
Be in possession of International SOS contact information, and other emergency contact information
Stay in contact with home office (or other regional contact) as directed by travel coordinator

### Situation Management Responsibilities
The HSE Manager, the Director of Technology, the Director of Projects, the Travel Coordinator, the Human Resources Manager, the Project Managers, and the Director of Sales are responsible for monitoring the International SOS situation alerts and to take actions as necessary to protect the Health and Safety of Company employees and contractors travelling on business.

## Safe Travel Planning Considerations
Travel to extreme risk areas should be avoided.  Other means of accomplishing the business goal will be explored first.
The following are the minimum planning/precautions to be taken when traveling to High or Extreme risk areas.

### Pre-Travel Briefing
Employees with little or no experience travelling in high or extreme risk will receive a pre-travel briefing given by the HSE Manager. This briefing will include the following minimum requirements:
General safe travel precautions
Situation update of possible risks in the area of travel, and how to minimize the risk exposure
Best safe-travel advice offered by International SOS
Evacuation plan arrangements and how to trigger the evacuation plan
Intranet resources for travellers.

### Health Considerations
The Aker Kvaerner Chemetics designated travel medical services provider will assess whether there are any vaccinations recommended for the travel destination(s) and if so, the travel medical services provider will contact the traveller directly to inform them of the recommendations and arrange an appointment as needed.  If the planned duration of the trip is longer than three months, the travel medical services provider will also contact the traveller to schedule a foreign posting medical assessment / briefing.
Employees having an existing medical condition are advised to seek medical advice prior to any business travel.

### Ground Transport
In High or extreme risk areas:
Employees are not permitted to drive a personal vehicle, outside the work compound, for business or pleasure. Travel in these areas should be pre-arranged with the client or a reliable local transport company.
Travel for pleasure (or rest and relaxation) outside of regular work hours will be coordinated with the Travel Coordinator and with the employee’s direct supervisor at home office, and the employee will take all reasonable safety precautions during his travels.
General requirements:
Employees are not permitted to operate any motorized vehicle that they are not licensed to operate, or that they do not have a certificate of competency to operate (in the jurisdiction that they are operating).
Leased cars or hire cars must have seat belts for driver and all passengers. Head rests, driver airbags and ABS brakes are recommended where available.
Driver and all passengers are to wear seat belts at all times (if car is equipped).
No driving is permitted while impaired by alcohol or drugs.
Driver shall not use hand-held mobile phone while driving. Use of hands-free mobile phone is also strongly discouraged.
Where personal driving is allowed, employees are expected to use judgment in the length of time they spend driving. As a guide, the maximum work time, including driving a vehicle should be limited to 14 hours a day. Overnight accommodation will be provided where necessary.

### Evacuation
International SOS is contracted by Chemetics to provide emergency assistance and emergency evacuation services for employees.  When the employee is travelling to High or Extreme risk region, the Travel Coordinator will verify the existence of an evacuation plan for the area where the employee will be travelling, and inform the employee of the plan.

### Travel Incidents
All travel Health and Safety related incidents will be reported to the HSE Manager.
Travel injuries, illness and incidents will be investigated in accordance with the Aker Kvaerner Incident Follow-up Norm (Norm Number: 2.2 NO 2)
