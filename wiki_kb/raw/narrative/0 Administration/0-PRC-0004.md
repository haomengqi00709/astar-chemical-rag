# 0-PRC-0004 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-0004_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure describes the style and format to be used in the drafting of textual documents, either as standards or for project-specific documents. The two major types of textual documents to which this procedure applies are:
Procedures: Documents that describe how we (Aker Kvaerner Chemetics, a division of Aker Kvaerner Canada Inc.) will perform our work.
Specifications: Documents that describe how we require others (Suppliers and Subcontractors) to perform their work.

## Format
The standard template to be used for textual documents is as follows:
0-SPC-0XXX: [Template Textual Document (Portrait)]

## Style
The following elements of style will be followed in drafting textual documents:
We will be referred to as “Aker Kvaerner Chemetics, ”
After first use of Aker Kvaerner Chemetics,  can be used
Suppliers of goods will be referred to as “Suppliers”
Suppliers of services or of goods and services will be referred to as “Subcontractor”
Our client including his representatives will be referred to as “Owner”
Numbering of Sections will preferably be at only one level such as for this document but will be at no more than two levels. Any further delineation will use bullets.
Express the imperative as “will” instead of “shall”.
Express directives in the active voice as much as possible.
Legalistic or archaic language will not be used.
Do not repeat directives within a document or in cross-referenced documents such as Data Sheets and Specifications.
Standards will be written for a singular purpose only in accordance with normal business practice, targeted at the end-user of the document.
Standards will be written to cover the 95% of circumstances normally encountered rather than be twice as long in order to cover them all.
Textual documents will be as brief as possible while preserving clarity.

## Templates
Templates are versions of documents that provide a fixed format within which project information will be placed.
Instructions to users in the body of a template that should be  will be enclosed in square brackets: [……].

## Commentary
Additional explanations and discussion of the content of a standard document will be included in an unnumbered Section entitled ”Commentary” beginning on a new page at the end of the document.

## Referencing
Within a document, reference by Section number. For example: “as described in Section 5.”
For Procedures, other documents will be referenced indirectly where possible. For example: “will be sent by the Discipline Lead (0-PRC-2001: Assignment of Project Responsibilities)”.
For Specifications, other documents will be referenced directly. For example: “as required by 0-SPC-5004: Packaging Land Transport”.

## Revisions
Revisions will be flagged on textual documents by placing a vertical line in the left margin to show the changes between the previous and the most recent version of the document.
Commentary
To Section 3: The terms “Supplier” or “Subcontractor” will be used for all directives, whether or not the supply or the service will in fact be provided by a sub-supplier, sub-subcontractor, etc.
Imperative to be “will” instead of “shall”: Although many people believe that there are distinct meanings for both terms, in law they are interchangeable. Their use is purely a matter of style, and the rule stated is the style to be used.
Active voice to be used: e.g. “The contractor will erect all towers…” rather than “All towers will be erected by the contractor…”
Legalistic or archaic language to be avoided: e.g. thereafter, hereinafter, etc.
Avoid repetition: repetition provides the greatest opportunity for errors or ambiguity in a document.
Singular purpose: e.g. rather than write a single specification for supply and installation of acid brick, write two specifications, one for the supply of acid brick and another for the installation of acid brick. This reflects the way that we typically procure the material and services.
Brevity and clarity: spend the time to make the document as short as possible.
To Section 6: Note that the references omit the word “Procedure”, “Specification”, etc, and do not mention sheet number or revision number.
Revision numbers should be included on Requisitions, Purchase Orders and Subcontract Document Lists where there will be commercial consequences from subsequent revisions.
