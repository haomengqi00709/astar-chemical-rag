# 0-PRC-8505 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8505_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this section is to establish minimum guidelines for safely opening lines and process equipment that contain or have contained process chemicals that could be hazardous to people and/or the environment.
This regulation will be followed by the Commissioning Team (Aker Kvaerner Chemetics personnel, Contractors and Vendors rep) after a circuit, any system or equipment is handed over mechanically completed for commissioning by the Construction Manager to the Commissioning Manager.
When available, a Client’s regulation may be followed if the requirements and guidelines spelled out on Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' procedures are fully covered by the Client’s regulation.
Note: This procedure is based on Aker Kvaerner’s Line and Flange Break procedure issued in January of 2003 and found in the AK’s Safety Policy Manual section 7, item 7.25.

## Scope
This procedure applies to all personnel who may be required to perform a line break or equipment opening as defined below.
For the purpose of these guidelines, the term “Line Breaking/Equipment Opening” refers to any job required to open any process line or equipment.  This includes but is not limited to: permanent and/or temporary bypass, connected fittings, valves, pumps, vessels, or tanks.  Hazardous conditions may exist in process lines or equipment that contain or have contained acids, caustics, flammables, or any other hazardous materials in the form of gases, liquids or solids.  In addition, hot materials such as heated water and steam present a significant hazard.

## Responsibilities
The hazard assessment created for the task must be understood by all members of the team involved with the line breaking or equipment opening activity.
The Commissioning HSE Manager is responsible for the administration of the Line Breaking and Equipment Opening Permit system.
In case the Commissioning HSE Manager is temporarily away from site (i.e., R&R, site leave, sick leave, etc.),  the Commissioning Manager will take over the administration of the Hot Work Permit system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will be responsible for obtaining permits from the client issuing authority.
The Commissioning HSE Manager will establish and maintain a program that adequately informs Aker Kvaerner Chemetics’ personnel of the existence, purpose and procedures of the Line and Flange Break system.
The Commissioning HSE Manager and the Work Supervisor must sign a valid permit for this program.

## Training
Each employee who is authorized to perform first breaks shall be thoroughly trained in all aspects of these guidelines. Everyone must go through Line Breaking Training provided by the Commissioning HSE Manager, either on Site or in Aker Kvaerner Chemetics office.
Training shall be documented and entered into the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics log book that the Commissioning HSE Manager keeps.
All Line Breaking/Equipment Opening incidents and near misses shall be thoroughly investigated and documented.

## Preparations
Prior to beginning any first break, the Line Breaking Permit (ON HOLD) shall be completed by the Commissioning HSE Manager with specific instructions including (but not limited to) the following:
Identification of the last known chemical(s) contained in the line, equipment, vessel, tank, etc., being opened.  If the line or equipment contained a mixture of chemicals the MSDS for each shall be reviewed and the precautions observed.
Health hazards associated with the chemical(s).
Method of detection associated with the chemical(s).
The specific PPE required to complete the task per MSDS and Aker Kvaerner work procedure(s) (i.e., respiratory protection, body protection, head/face protection, hand protection, fall protections, etc.).
Type of containment or container needed to prevent environmental contamination.
Piping and equipment that has been in service must be positively identified by an individual familiar with that specific system.  Any piping or equipment to be opened or disassembled shall be isolated by having the appropriate upstream and downstream valve(s) closed, disconnected, blinded or have a mechanical restraining device affixed and a low point bleed valve should be open to assure no stored energy or chemicals remain in the line.
“DO NOT OPERATE” tags, “ENERGY ISOLATION” tags or equivalent shall be placed at all isolation points.  It is mandatory that all valves be blinded, locked, tagged out or chained and tagged when employees are working downstream or upstream. Where potential for leaks exist it is mandatory that all valves be blinded, locked and tagged out.
Switches and/or push buttons that operate pumps, blowers or any other electrical, pneumatic, or hydraulic equipment must be locked and tagged per “Lockout and Tagging” procedure.  Pumps, motors, or other devices that are activated by mechanical devices such as floats, switches, etc. shall be operated manually to assure they are properly isolated.
Barricades shall be erected to warn other workers of the possible hazard(s). If the line breaking/equipment opening is at an elevated location all levels below where workers may walk shall be barricaded if a hazard may exist.
Coordination between Aker Kvaerner Chemetics personnel and Client operations personnel shall be such that no other process work, maintenance, or sampling is being performed on the line being opened.  If other work is being performed it shall be evaluated to assure the other activities will not endanger the personnel performing the line breaking operation.
Where eyewash/safety shower facilities are not readily available and the potential for hazardous materials exists, a potable water hose with a quick opening valve shall be located at the work site. If a shower facility is readily available it shall be tested before starting the job and the location known by all employees performing the first line break.

## Personal Protective Equipment (P.P.E.)
PPE shall be worn (refer to MSDS/task procedure) to protect the employees from the type chemical(s) that have been contained in the line, vessel, tank, equipment, etc.  For potable water or process airlines, the minimum PPE to be worn for first break shall be gloves and a face shield worn over safety goggles.
The employees performing the first break shall be trained on the proper use and limitations of the PPE required for the break.  See “PPE Requirements” identified on the permit.
The level of PPE required to carry out a first line break will depend on the relative hazards associated with the chemical(s) and the task being performed. The minimum PPE to be worn for first break shall protect the employees from the last known chemical(s) that have been contained in the line, vessel, tank, equipment, etc.

## Procedures

### Task Safety Awareness (TSA)
No line and flange break permit will be issued until the Commissioning HSE Manager completes a Task Safety Awareness (TSA) review and has it posted at the location of the work. See Form DP-802.306 (Task Safety Awareness form).

### Line Breaking Guidelines
It is difficult to prescribe a general set of rules to cover the precise and safe way for all first breaks. The safest task procedures applicable to the particular task shall be followed including:
Always treat a first line break as a pressured line. Be aware of low points in the line that may contain trapped chemicals.
A low point bleed valve shall be opened and rodded to assure that no stored energy remains in the line. Atmospheric monitoring should be done to calculate chemical concentrations or residuals that may remain in the line at the bleed point.
Bolts shall be loosened slowly and on the opposite side of the employee(s) performing the first break to prevent any escaping chemicals/material from injuring workers and/or co-workers.
Flange spreaders shall be used for opening flange joints whenever required and/or practical.  When not possible due to close clearance, size of pipe, etc., only standard wedges shall be used as an alternative. Steps shall be taken to prevent wedges flying in the event it slips from the joint.
Whenever flange bolts are severely corroded, the old bolts shall be removed one at a time, replaced with new bolts, and tightened.  The new bolts can then be loosened as a wedge is inserted.
Dismantling lines utilizing crane lifting, assure that no chemical is trapped in the line. If the possibility of trapped chemicals exists, the pipe ends shall be tightly rapped with polyurethane and sealed with duct tape prior to lifting to prevent ground contamination or inadvertent chemical contact or exposure to personnel.
Dismantled lines, fitting, pumps, etc. shall be washed thoroughly and be free of contaminates before being removed from the jobsite, even if the parts are being scrapped.
Continuous atmospheric monitoring as required.

### Note: No line or equipment containing a flammable material shall be opened when there is a “Potential Ignition Source” that may create a hazard from escaping processes.
