# 0-PRC-8011 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8011_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure outlines the actions to be taken should an employee be in the computer room when the Computer room automated fire extinguishing system is activated.

## Vancouver Computer Room Fire Extinguisher System
The Vancouver computer room is protected by an automated fire extinguishing system. This system uses FM-200 gas as the fire suppression agent. It is designed to flood the room with a high enough concentration of FM-200 gas to extinguish a fire in the room.
FM-200 gas removes heat energy from fire, and not oxygen from the environment. The product is considered safe to humans, and does not deplete the oxygen in the room.
The fire system is designed to allow people to evacuate the room before activation. However, if the system should deploy when someone is in the room, they can continue to breathe normally.

## Computer Room Entry
Do not enter the computer room if the light outside the computer room door is on. CHECK THE LIGHT BEFORE ENTERING.
If the light is on, DO NOT attempt to open the door until 90 seconds have elapsed since alarm activation, and then use extreme caution and open the door slowly.

## Pre-discharge Actions
Upon activation:
Alarm sounds in the computer room
Building fire alarm is activated
Light on the ceiling outside of the computer room is lit.
Thirty (30) second delay to discharge activated
Everyone in the room at the time of activation is to leave the room immediately and ensure that the door is closed quickly. They are to continue and evacuate the building as per Office evacuation procedures.
The ABORT BUTTON should only be used if the system is inadvertently activated.

## Post-discharge Actions
Use extreme caution when attempting to enter the room after a discharge.
Be sure that 90 seconds have elapsed since the alarm activation.
Entry into the room should be done with extreme caution. Check the door for signs of heat on the inside of the room. If no signs of heat are detected, open the door slowly and with caution.
After a discharge and the room has been declared safe by the fire marshal (the fire threat no longer exists), the room should be well ventilated before re-entering.
