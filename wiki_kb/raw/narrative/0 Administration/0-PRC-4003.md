# 0-PRC-4003 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-4003_table_1]


## Purpose

### The main purpose of Change Management and PVN/PCN procedure is to establish the policies, responsibilities and procedure to identify, track and approve (or reject) potential changes that could impact the Project’s approved budget, schedule and scope, and therefore provide trends for the forecast of project costs and schedule.
The PVN/PCN Program will be used throughout the EPC phase of the project, and will have three objectives:
It will act as a vehicle to identify, monitor, reject or authorize project changes.
It will provide an “early warning system” to identify significant changes likely to have either a positive or negative influence on the approved project budget and schedule.
It will provide the information to ensure timely, accurate forecast of total project cost and schedule.
This procedure is intended to cover administration of change management procedures both for home office and field construction.

## Scope

### The scope of this procedure includes all changes which affect the budget, manpower forecast, schedule, scope and guarantees.

## Policy and Principles

### Policy
It is a REQUIREMENT for all projects to implement a change management procedure and set up change management program to track, communicate and document changes of the project.
A change management procedure must be reviewed and agreed with client at the beginning of a project. If client has its own change management procedure, an amending procedure should be developed to incorporate this procedure with clients change management procedure.
Change Management shall be a “top priority “in the mindset of all project personnel.
The underlying project philosophy is to provide a work product which meets the scope, schedule, performance and warranty requirements of the contract. Throughout the course of the project, items may be found necessary or potentially desirable to achieve this goal in the optimum manner. Any of those things found - by any member of the team - to be different from the original scope and / or which may materially affect the scope, cost, schedule, execution plan or cash flow of the project shall be identified as a Change and dealt with accordingly.

### Principle
The essential feature of change management is timelines. Identification, notification and resolution of a change must occur early enough for decisions to be feasible without (or with minimum) rework and / or schedule disruption to the project.
Where applicable, the probable effects on the approved project parameters shall be developed to a comparable level of accuracy to facilitate decision making on the change.
The Change Management Program must satisfy the following requirements:
Early identification of any situation that may impact cost, schedule, execution plan or cash flow.
Fast evaluation of the impact on cost, schedule, execution plan, cash flow and the reasons why they or alternative solutions should be considered.
Notification to project management
Regular meetings, aiming at timely resolution of Changes (approval or rejection, acceptance, corrective or mitigating action, etc.).
Weekly updates of Change reports, recording decisions made, action items, and Change Log status.
Implementation of actions with ongoing tracking and (minimum) bi-weekly update reporting on progress of Approved Changes.
Forecasted cost, schedule or cash flow revisions when authorized.
Revisions to the project budget, baseline schedule or budgeted cash flow when authorized and supplemented by an approved Client’s Change Order
Identifying source of funding if applicable (ie. Management Reserve, Allowances, etc.).
In some cases changes below $10,000 can be accumulated before a potential trend is raised in order to reduce administrative effort. This would not be done if approval to proceed is required or if the reason for the change is an issue that needs to be managed or communicated.

## Responsibilities

### All members of the project team: are responsible for the effectiveness of the Change Management Program. All members of the project team have the responsibility to identify potential issues affecting change in any of the following approved project parameters as defined in the approved scope documents:
Scope & associated design
Cost
Schedule
Execution plan
Cash flow

### The Project Controls Specialist/Analyst is responsible for:
Administration of Change Management system of the project, all the Changes to be timely registered, tracked and documented
Assembling all required information of a Change with the help of project engineer, discipline leads, estimating and project planner/scheduler
Updating status of Changes, maintaining and issuing of Change Log for periodic review, and continuously reminding project management to timely resolve Variances
Once a Change is approved, incorporating trends into cost, schedule, progress measurement, and cash flow for timely and accurate progress and forecast.

### The Project Controls Manager is responsible for
Ensuring the implementation of change management program on each project
Reviewing and approving cost and schedule impact analysis for the proposed change
Administration of this procedure.

### The Discipline Leads are responsible for:
The proper implementation of this procedure in performing of their work assignments.
Providing Manhour estimate for the Change
Providing schedule impact analysis for the Change

### The Project Engineer is responsible for:
Coordinating change impact analysis from all affected disciplines and providing assistance for project manager to timely approve (or reject) changes.

### The Project Manager is responsible for
Ensuring all project team understand project scope basis
Timely approving, rejecting Changes
Getting Project Director’s approval for Change over 5% of contract price or budgeted Man-hours
Communicating with clients and getting clients’ approval for external Changes (PCN)

### The Discipline Managers: are responsible for:
The proper implementation of this procedure within their respective disciplines
Assisting discipline leads in project planning and Manhour estimate regarding Changes

### The Director of Projects is responsible for:
Approval of any Change over 5% of contract price or budgeted Man-hours
The proper implementation of this procedure within Chemetics
Approval of this procedure.

## Procedure

### PCN vs. PVN
There are two types of changes on a project that will impact project cost, schedule, scope of work/supply and guarantees. The first type is an external change or scope change which may be a direct change from defined scope document such as contract, scope definition or specifications. This type of change is documented as a Project Change Notification (PCN).
Changes covered by PCN are:
Any additional work or rework requested by client but not included in original scope documents
Any additional work or rework due to client’s change in their intent of project, execution plan, standards and specifications, and delivery of key information and decision
Any change in intent to better fit the objectives of the project , but deviated from original project scope, design basis, and execution plan
The PCN must be approved by the Client prior to implementation or execution.
Approved PCN will have impacts on both Approved budget and Forecast.
The second type of change is an internal change or non-scope change, which does not change the intended delivered scope. This type of change is documented in a Project Variance Notification (PVN).
Changes covered by PVN are:
Any additional work or rework due to errors or omissions
Any additional work or rework due to internal change of execution plan
Productivity loss not due to client’s impact
Internal budget transfers for better tracking cost elements (0$ cost change)
The PVN must be approved before work starts.
Upon approval of the PVN, the Revised Budget and Forecast will be adjusted.
When a change notification is initiated, it has to be firstly identified as either a scope change or a non-scope change, and consequently applied to PCN or PVN.  The PCN and the PVN utilizes the same form, but they will be registered in different logs.  The PVNs generated for internal changes may or may not be provided to the Client depending on type of contract and agreements between clients and Chemetics. For example, for Lump sum contract, PVNs will not be provided to Client, but in reimbursable contract PVNs may proceed to Clients.

### Change Initiation
Any team member may identify a change.  When a change is identified, the Originator will fill a PVN/PCN form (as in Appendix I) and submit to the Project Control’s Specialist/Project Analyst for registration and assignment of a PVN/PCN.  Further PVN/PCN development cannot proceed without a number.
The Originator should at minimal level fill the following information:
Project No.: Originator enters the job number to which the PVN applies.  This field may already be completed on the project specific template.
PVN/PCN No.: Originator will obtain a PVN/PCN number from the Project Controls Specialist/Project Analyst on the Project.
Other Job Information: The Originator will complete these data as applicable: Date, Client, Location, Originator.
The Originator will include all pertinent attachments with the PVN/PCN form.
Title of the PVN/PCN: Provide a short description of the change for quick reference.
Source of Change: Identify Source of Change. The main sources of changes include: Client, Engineering, Procurement, Construction, HS&E, Vendor, Permits, and Others.
Basis of Change: The Originator will precisely describe the requested variance and reason for it.  Scope should be in sufficient detail, allowing other disciplines affected to fully understand the changes. Additional pages may be attached, if necessary.
Revision: Revision to the original PVN/PCN will be clearly identified by Project Controls Specialist. The reasons for the revision will be briefly described as part of the detail description of the variance.  Revisions are assigned an “Alpha” character commencing with “A.” A revision cannot be made to an already approved PVN/PCN; a new PVN/PCN number must be assigned.
The Originator fills following information based on their best knowledge and assessment of the change:
Impacts: Originator can enter impacts for the project on scope, engineering, construction, and procurement. The schedule impacts and cost impacts should be input by project controls after all the impact evaluation is done. It is recommended to list all the deliverables impacted by the change. If necessary, an attached document can be used to list affected deliverables.
Recommended Action: Originator can enter recommended action based on their knowledge. This portion can be revised when more information is available after all evaluation is done.

### Change Evaluation
After a PVN/PCN is initiated, project controls/project analyst will distribute it to all related disciplines for impact analysis.
Project engineer (project manager, if no project engineer) will coordinate impact analysis from all Discipline Leads.
Engineering Discipline Leads will evaluate engineering impacts and estimate Man-hour required if the change is implemented and also make assessments on schedule impact. This assessment will be feed back to project controls for total cost and schedule impact evaluation. Discipline Leads also need to input their “Recommended Actions” for Impacts they identified.
Other Disciplines, such as procurement, construction will provide their impact analysis if affected by change.
Project Controls/Project Analyst will gather all inputs for overall evaluation and update PVN/PCN form. If a detailed estimate is required for the total cost of the change, project controls will request an estimate for the change, and request risk management review the change and provide input for estimating. Project controls will compile, summarize and issue the formal cost estimate attached to the PVN/PCN. Detail breakdown of the cost per cost code and work code will be appended to the PVN/PCN.
Project Controls will compile all documents for approval.

### Change Approval
Project Controls/Project Analyst will initiate and track PVN/PCN for approval.
Project Controls Manager will approve, amend, or reject all PVN/PCN to make sure cost and schedule impact is properly analyzed, and all the information on PVN/PCN is clear and accurate.
Project Engineer, then Project Manager will approve, amend, or reject PVN/PCN their judgments based on whether the impacts are acceptable for project execution and project schedule and cost.
If the overall cost impact of a change exceeded 5% of contract price or budgeted Man-hours, the PVN/PCN needs review and approval from Director of Projects. Director of Projects can approve, amend, or reject the change.
If it is a PCN, Client’s approval is pursued by Project Manager once Project Manager/Director of Projects has approved it.

### Change Implementation
Project Controls will update Change Log after Change has been approved or rejected, and distribute to all affected disciplines.
After a change is approved, action should be taken by affected disciplines based on Recommended Actions and Comments. No work should start before a change has been approved.
Project Controls will incorporate approved changes into project cost, schedule and progress. Approved PCN will impact project “Approved Budget”, “Revised Budget” and forecast, and approved “PVN” will impact project “Revised Budget” and Forecast. (Note: if a cost system does not have “Revised Budget” function, PVN/PCN will only impact Forecast).Project forecast trending is based on based approved PCN/PVN and “Pending” PCN/PVN.
A project Change Order is requested by project manager from Client to include one or a number of PCNs to adjust contract value or project targets.

### Administration of the PVN/PCN
It is Project Controls/Project Analyst’s responsibility to administrate PVN/PCN on a project. All PVN/PCN must be properly registered, documented, issued and tracked through a PVN/PCN Log (as in Appendix III & IV 2).
PVN and PCN are maintained in two separate logs, so their impacts on project budget are easy to track.
Project Controls/Project Analyst must timely update “Status” of each change, and issue PCN/&PVN log weekly to project manager prior to regular project meeting. Therefore, project team can review any “Pending” changes and resolve them timely.
“Status” of a PCN/PVN includes:
A – “Approved”: PCN/PVN is approved.
C - “Cancelled”: PCN/PVN is cancelled.
D - “Draft”: PCN/PVN is registered but all evaluation is not completed.
P - “Pending”; PCN/PVN is issued for approval.
R - “Rejected”: PCN/PVN is rejected.
Project Engineer will maintain PVN Log and PVN Log in the CHG folder located as required by 0-PRC-0003: File Structure.
Each PVN/PVN will have a dedicated subfolder. All PVN/PCN, including their revisions and supporting estimates, will be stored in this file.
