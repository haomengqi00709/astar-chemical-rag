### Outsourcing
All design work will be carried out in the Vancouver office with the following exceptions:
Detailed Civil and Site Preparation Design
Detailed Electrical Design
Detailed Instrument Design
The split in scope between Aker Kvaerner Chemetics and an engineering contractor is shown on:
0-LST-2001: Engineering Scope Checklist
Outsourced design will be subcontracted to qualified Australasian engineering contractors with the exception of Instrument Specification preparation which will be executed by Norpac Controls in Vancouver under the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics / Norpac Strategic Supply Agreement.
Detailed structural design will be done in-house rather than outsourced along with the civil work. This is because of the need for close interaction with the design and drafting team for the modularisation of the plant. This will require bringing in contract personnel to augment existing staff.

### Design Tools
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will maximise the use of PDMS for design work. The following drawings will be produced using PDMS:
Equipment layout
Civil criteria general arrangements
Structural general arrangements
Piping general arrangements
Piping isometrics
Cable tray layout criteria
Instrument location general arrangements
The design model will be set-up so that each plant module is a separate PDMS “area”.
One of the Contract deliverables is a PDS plant model. This will be derived by translating from the PDMS model. The translation will only be done once, at the conclusion of the project.
Other drawings will be drafted using AutoCAD. The Contract requires drawings to be presented using MicroStation. Drawings will be translated from AutoCAD to MicroStation once only, at the conclusion of the project.
Textual documents and lists will be prepared using Microsoft Office products. This format is acceptable to the Owner.

### Modularisation
The plant will be modularised at off-site fabrication yards and delivered to the site with all equipment, piping, instrumentation, cable tray, conduit, and instrument cabling installed.
Design constraints arising from this are as follows:
Preferred module dimension: corresponding to a standard 20 ft or 40 ft Container
Maximum oversized module: standard container lengths x 14 ft wide x 13 ft high
Module frames must provide enough strength and rigidity to allow handling during transportation and setting at site, as well as to withstand operational loads. The modules will be designed to sit on concrete plinths minimum of 100 mm above slab.

## Construction

### Modularisation
The plant will be modularised at off-site fabrication yards and delivered to the site with all equipment, piping, instrumentation, cable tray, conduit, and instrument cabling installed.
To minimise Industrial Relations impact on site, unionised Australasian subcontractors will do the modularisation.
Separate modularisation subcontracts will be let as follows:
Structural steel
Mechanical (equipment setting, pipe spooling and installation, instrument installation)
Electrical
