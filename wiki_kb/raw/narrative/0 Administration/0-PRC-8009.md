# 0-PRC-8009 — Procedure


[TABLE: 0-PRC-8009_table_1]


## Purpose
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics (“Company”) is committed to providing safe workplaces for its employees.  Audits and inspections will be used as a means to correct unsafe work practices and hazards, and as a means to ensure that Aker Kvaerner, Company, and statutory health and safety requirements and standards are being met.

## Scope
This procedure applies to all locations where the Company is in full control of the operation of health and safety at the worksite.  The guidelines herein will be applied to measure the level of compliance with Aker Kvaerner procedures, Company procedures and statutory HSE regulations, and as a means to guide further improvement.

## Definitions
Audit – means an in-depth review of HSE procedural requirements and the level of implementation at the particular work site.
Company – means Aker Kvaerner Chemetics
HSE – means Health Safety and Environment
Inspection – means a systematic and predetermined review of the work site and working environment aimed at identifying hazards and conditions that have negative effects on health safety and environment.

## Responsibilities

### Joint Health and Safety Committees
At work sites having Joint Health and Safety Committee the responsibility for the inspections will be assigned to member(s) of the Joint Health and Safety Committee.
The Joint Health and Safety Committee will follow local statutory requirements in handling items arising out of the inspection process.

### Company Personnel
In the absence of a designated HSE Manager at a work site, the senior work site Manager will assume all responsibilities for HSE Management and direction at the work site.
Business Unit President will ensure that:
There is a program of inspections at each of the business unit work sites.
An annual audit of the HSE Operating System takes place.
All Department Managers (or Work site Managers) will ensure that:
A planned schedule of inspections is in place for their area(s) of responsibility
Review of actions takes place and that required actions are closed out
HSE Manager for the Business Unit will ensure that:
There is a program of inspections at each of the business unit work sites.
Each work site receives an HSE Operating System Audit.
Persons assigned to carry out the inspections are competent
A record inspection is kept, and that a copy is made available to employees.
Person(s) carrying out the inspection will ensure that:
All observations are recorded and that the owner of an action and a reasonable completion timescale for completion is identified and agreed.
A follow-up takes place to ensure that actions were completed.
Employees will ensure that:
Their work area is kept clean and safe, by performing weekly inspections.

## General Requirements for Inspections
Each work site will have:
A program of regular inspections in place.
A check-list of items that will be inspected for compliance with the HSE regulations.
An inspection schedule that is appropriate for the risk level of the work (see below).
Three types of inspections are required (depending on work site)
Specific equipment inspections
Work site or work area inspections done by work site personnel
Construction sites and research laboratories will have regular inspection schedules for specific power equipment used at that location.  These inspections (check-lists and schedules) will be detailed in the specific work site safety procedures, or in the equipment operating manuals. Equipment inspections will be done by the equipment operator, or a specialized technician.

## Inspections Procedure

### Frequency
The frequency of inspections will be established by the Joint Health and Safety Committee, or by the person having responsibility for HSE at the work site.  In establishing the frequency consideration will be given to the risk level of the operation and the number of people at the location.  The established frequency will meet that required local Workers Compensation board, or any other statutory requirement.
Minimum recommended frequency of work site inspections:
Constructions sites – once per week.
Workshops and Laboratories – once per month.
Design or working office environment – every three months.
Minimum recommended frequency of equipment inspections:
The most frequent of that required by the equipment operating manual, local statutory regulations, or by workplace safety rules.

### Method
The general method is to conduct a walk-about looking for hazards and hazardous conditions on the items in the inspection check-list.

#### Inspection Report
The inspection report is the formal document that contains a list of the hazards noted during the inspection. This report will contain the following information:
Inspection date
Description of inspected area
Hazardous condition list as outlined below
As conditions will change in the work place, the inspector should always be alert to hazardous conditions not listed in the inspection check-list, and if necessary amend the list to reflect current work site place conditions.
When a hazardous deviation is found, that deviation will be noted, and the following will be assigned to the item:
Location of hazard.
Person accountable.  This person will generally be the Manager having responsibility for the area.
Risk level of item:  Low, Medium, High, Extreme
Deadline for action (Extreme risk items will always have a “Complete” in the column.

#### Recommended Action Timeframes
Extreme risk items will be fixed immediately.  Stop inspection (and work if necessary) and have the problem corrected.
High risk item will be corrected within 8 hours. Secure area and mark it clearly to indicate hazard.
Medium risk items will be corrected within 5 working days. Secure area and mark it clearly to indicate hazard.
Low risk item will be corrected within 2 weeks.

#### Recommend Follow-up Guidelines
Local statutory regulations will be followed if more strict that those outlined below.
High and extreme risk items will be followed as per completion deadline.
Medium and Low risk items be followed-up after 3 working weeks have passed, or if the frequency of inspection is less then 3 weeks, at the time of the next inspection.

## Audits Procedure

### Notification of Audits
Audits done by the business unit HSE management as part of the HSE quality assurance process will be announced to the work site management no less than 7 days prior to the visit.

### Frequency
Permanent work sites having and average of more than 25 employees will be audited annually.
Temporary construction work sites will be audited at the 15% construction completion stage, then every 6 months.  Minimally a construction site will be audit at the 15% completion stage and at the 50% completion stage.

### Method
The audit process will consist of two basic steps:
HSE Operating System Audit
Work site inspection

#### HSE Operating System Audit
The Company will conduct an annual self-assessment of the Business Unit overall HSE operations, using a consensus process led by the HSE Manager of the Business Unit, and as described in section 3.3 of Group Policy 2.2 PR 3 “HSE Operating System Procedure”.
The assessment results will be submitted to Aker Kvaerner Group auditors for their information and follow-up.
Company work sites will be assessed and scored using the HSE Operating System Assessment tool, by the HSE Manager of the Business Unit, and as described in section 3.3 of Group Policy 2.2 PR 3 “HSE Operating System Procedure”.
The Excel-based assessment tool: (2.2 DI 4 HSE Operating System Assessment)
As per section 3.3.5 of the “HSE Operating System Assessment”:
The improvement actions identified during the assessments shall be managed to completion as part of the Business Unit’s local management system.
In addition, the Company as a whole will have an annual “Learning Review” of it’s assessment with the HSE Manager of another Aker Kvaerner Business Unit.

## Audits by Representatives of Health and Safety Boards
From time to time it is possible that the local Worker’s Compensation Safety Board may audit any work sites under their jurisdiction. In case of inspection the following guidelines are recommended:
Full cooperation and courtesy will be extended to the inspector. The purpose of these inspections is to uncover potential hazardous conditions and to rectify them.
The inspector will be escorted at all times by the HSE Manager, or the senior site manager, but without restricting his movements at the worksite.
Questions regarding safety matters of interest to the inspector will be answered factually and as directly as possible, but without offering any additional information.
If the inspector uses recording devices to record issues of concern, it is recommended that the Company also use similar recording devices.  The Company escort should also take personal notes about discussion items.
At the end of the inspection, review any issues of concern to the inspector, ensure that there is common agreement on those concerns, and ensure that the inspector’s notes fairly reflect the discussion on those concerns.
If possible, get a copy of the inspector’s notes.
