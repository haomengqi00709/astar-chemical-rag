1. 
Purpose 
This document describes the general requirements for the instrumentation and control systems 
required for the new [Chloralkali Plant at Atanor’s] manufacturing site in [Rio Tercero, Cordoba, 
Argentina]. 
 
The purpose of this document is to present a summary of instrumentation design requirements 
for the instrumentation designers, and to eliminate the need for project personnel to refer 
directly to the project contract. 
 
2. 
Statutory Requirements  
The design, construction and operation of the [Chlor-Alkali Plant] shall comply with the 
Statutory Requirements required in the [Shire of Fitzroy, State of Queensland, Australia].  In 
particular the following will apply: 
 
[Development Approval] 
[Environmental Protection Act] 
[Queensland Electricity Act] 
[Queensland Workplace Health and Safety Act] 
 
3. 
Codes and Standards 
All equipment and installations will comply with the latest revision of the applicable [Australian 
Standards]. Where no [Australian Standard] exists, work will conform to the most current IEC 
Standard. 
 
Where conflict exists between any of the statutory regulations, standards, reference documents, 
and the requirements on drawings, datasheets and this specification, the most stringent 
requirement shall apply. 
 
Relevant [Australian Standards] include, but are not limited to those listed in the Appendix B.  
Relevant IEC Standards include, but are not limited to those listed in Appendix C. 
 
Instruments will be specified for [CENELEC] approval. 
 
4. 
Site Conditions 
The relevant site conditions are listed in 1-DST-0001: Site Design Conditions. 
 
In addition to these requirements, all electrical equipment will be designed to operate 
continuously at full load for 24 hours per day, 365 days per year.  Equipment may be required 
to be protected against an environment subject to abrasive and corrosive dusts and liquids and 
vapours. 
 
Some areas experience pollution in the form of chlorine gas, hydrochloric acid, liquid and 
vapour, and brine solution.   
 
In general all equipment both indoors and outdoors will be “tropic proofed”.  In particular, 
motors and similar equipment will incorporate special protection against fungal attack.  Printed

List and the individual Instrument Specification Sheets.

8.3. 
Field Bus 
Where the instruments are to be used in conjunction with plant control system equipment that 
provides a field bus for connection of field measurement and control devices, then the 
instruments will be of a type that provides the necessary functionality to connect to the field bus. 
Where field bus functionality is not available, the signal connection and type will be 4 to 20 mA, 
preferably loop-powered. 
 
8.4. 
Power Supplies 
Power supply to instruments will be as specified in the data sheets. If the instrument is 
communicating via a field bus that can also supply operating power to connected instruments, 
then this will be the preferred power supply connection. 
 
8.5. 
Reverse Polarity 
Each instrument will be immune to damage caused by reverse polarity connection and output 
short/open circuit. 
 
8.6. 
RFI Immunity 
Instruments will be RFI immune, complying with SAMA PMC 33.1C (IEC 801-3) or equivalent 
standard in the frequency range from 20 to 1000 MHz and field intensity up to 10 mV/m. 
 
8.7. 
Immunity from Magnetic Fields 
Consideration will be given for the selection of equipment located within 50 m of the d.c. Bus in 
the Cell House, this is due to the extra-ordinarily high d.c. magnetic fields. 
 
Instruments with magnets may not function correctly. Of particular interest are positioning 
equipment reed switches, inductive pick-ups, and magnetic flowmeters.

8.10. Flow Metering 
 
Liquid 
Magnetic flow meters are preferred for liquid. For liquid applications with suspended solids, the 
ideal velocity through the flow tube will be in the range of 1.5 to 3 meters per second to prevent 
sedimentation of solids and minimise wear on the liner.  Foxboro AC type magnetic flow tubes 
will be provided for service in the proximity of the cells due to high magnetic fields.  
 
Clean or low conductivity liquids and steam may be measured by vortex shedding meter.  
 
Averaging pitot tubes or orifice plates may be used as the primary measuring element on large 
diameter clean liquid pipelines and for gas flows.  
 
Positive displacement meters or turbine meters will be used where greater accuracy or greater 
rangeability is required than that obtainable with orifice or vortex flowmeters. 
 
Custody transfer metering will be accomplished using positive displacement meters or Coriolis 
flowmeters on the process fluid.

Coriolis type flow meters will be used for mass flow measurement of fluids.  The Coriolis flow 
meter will provide signals for flow, density and temperature.  Piping connected to the Coriolis 
meters will be mounted securely, the flow meter will not be used for supporting the adjacent 
piping.  Vibration will not be transferred through the piping system to the Coriolis meters. 
 
Gas 
Averaging pitot tubes or orifice plates may be used as the primary measuring element on large 
diameter piping with clean gas flows. 
 
Gas flow may be measured by the Vortex shedding meter. Vortex meters will not be used 
where the piping may be subjected to vibration. It should be noted that vortex meters require a 
minimum flow to produce a signal. This may not be acceptable for some applications during 
start-up and shutdown. 
 
Thermal Dispersion (TD) mass flow measurement technique may be applied for flow 
measurement of clean gases. These instruments will be flange-mounted, with the flange 
marked to indicate correct orientation of the device. 
 
For mass flow measurement on gas flows that use pressure differential techniques, 
multivariable transmitters with automatic temperature and pressure compensation may be used. 
 
Flow elements such as flow nozzle or venturi may be used where high capacity or high-
pressure recovery is required. 
 
Flowmeter Installation 
When the flow is controlled by a variable speed pump, the flowmeter is to be installed after and 
as close as practical to the pump. 
 
When the flow is controlled by a control valve, the flowmeter is to be installed upstream to and 
as close as practical to the valve. 
 
dp Transmitter Installation 
Seals and condensate pots will be used where necessary. 
 
Flowmeter Orifice Plates 
The standard primary element will be square edge concentric orifice plate in accordance with 
AS 2360, except that a quadrant edge orifice, in accordance with BS 1042, will be used when 
the Reynolds number is below 20 000. 
 
Flange taps will be used. D-D/2 taps may be used if the line size is 400 NB or larger. 
 
Material of orifice plates as minimum will be type 316 stainless steel, unless special materials 
are required by the process fluid. 
 
Orifice plates will have the instrument tag number, material type, flow direction identification 
and bore size clearly stamped on the upstream side of the plate handle. 
 
Straight-run lengths of pipe upstream and downstream of orifice plate installations and orifice 
plate installations will conform to the requirements defined in AS 2360.1 and as required to 
meet the specified accuracy.

8.11. Level Measurements 
 
Design and Selection of Level Instruments 
The preferred level measurement devices will be Differential Pressure Level measurement for 
clean liquids.  As applications require, ultrasonic, radar, magneto-restrictive or capacitance 
level transmitters will be used.   
 
Differential Pressure Level 
Where differential pressure transmitters are used on atmospheric tanks, the transmitter will be 
provided with a 3” integrally mounted flange diaphragm on the high-pressure port.  Where 
differential pressure transmitters are used on sealed tanks, the transmitter will be provided with 
a 3” integrally mounted flange diaphragm on the high pressure port and a remote mounted 3” 
flange diaphragm connected t the low pressure port. 
Differential pressure transmitters without diaphragm seals will be fitted with 3-valve manifolds 
to enable isolation of the transmitter from the process, and equalisation of the high and low 
pressure sides of the transmitter. 
 
Ultrasonic 
Ultrasonic level sensors may be used for measurement of level in material handling 
applications and vented vessels, tanks, sumps, etc. containing slurries or liquids. Other 
methods may be necessary in applications where foam/froth, condensation, dusts or steam are 
prevalent. Transducers will be selected for adequate acoustic power under the most arduous 
process conditions. Four wire devices are preferred. 
 
Only ultrasonic level instruments with built in temperature measurement and compensation will 
be used. 
 
Radar 
Radar level sensors may be used for measurement of level in material handling. They will be 
used in applications of pressurised vessels, including vacuum and applications containing 
foam/froth, heavy condensation, and where the potential for gas or product vaporisation may 
occur. They may be used in extremely dusty applications and where dust bursts are present 
during product filling. The sensor will be fitted with a horn or antenna, manufactured from 
material chemically compatible with the process environment.  Preferred power supply is loop 
powered. 
 
Capacitance 
Capacitance probes may be used where no alternative method is satisfactory. 
 
Magnetic Level Gauge 
Magnetic-coupled float level gauges may be used in preference to gauge glasses for fluids 
where solids build up can occur.

Where pulsation is expected, and the process is non-clogging, suitable adjustable dampers will 
be used. Dampers will not be used in slurry applications. 
 
Local and Remote Seals 
Preferred diaphragm seal type is 80 mm RF Flange.   Fill liquid will be selected having regard 
to process conditions; glycerine or glycol/water are preferred for most applications.

Temperature Instruments 
Pt100 Resistance Temperature Detectors will be used for applications where the temperature

programmable head-mounted transmitters, unless this arrangement is not practical.  Where 
multiple transmitters are required such as Electrolyzer temperature monitoring, the transmitters 
will be din rail mounted in a separate junction box.

thermocouple failure.
