# 7-SPC-0005 — Specification


[TABLE: 7-SPC-0005_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this Engineering Standard is to define the minimum requirements for the shop application of Yellow Jacket and field application of Polyken tape to joints and fittings.

## Scope
This Engineering Standard applies when called for on purchase order or referenced documents.
Where end user and/or consultants specifications are also applicable, the more restrictive condition will apply.
The contractor will be responsible for all requirements relating to the supply, installation, welding and testing of the coated pipe and coating/testing fittings, including backfilling.  Excavation and supply of fittings may be the responsibility of others, depending upon project requirements.

## Shop Coating
Shop Coating System
The coating will be suitable for a maximum temperature of 150 F. The coating system com comprises the following:
Pipe cleaned by shot blasting
Flame preheat and degreasing
Undercoat sealant
Corrosion resistant jacket (yellow jacket)
Controlled cooling
Electronic holiday inspection
Ends cut back, for welding
Thickness check
Coating Material
The pipe will be shop-coated with X-Tru-Coat (extruded yellow polyethylene over butyl rubber adhesive), as applied by Standard Pipe Protection, or a similar approved system, with a 6-inch cutback.  Polyethylene jacket thickness over a 10-mil thickness of adhesive will be manufacturer’s standard for pipe diameters below 4” and 60 mils for pipe 4” through 24”.  Primer will be one coat of Polyken No. 927.  Maximum continuous operating temperature: 150 F.  Maximum continuous operating temperature for optional orange polypropylene copolymer jacket: 190 F.

## Surface Preparation
Prior to any mechanical cleaning, all mill varnish, oil, grease, paint and other surface contaminants will be removed from the pipe using a suitable solvent.
Shop coated pipe will be mechanically cleaned to SSPC-SP-6 (NACE 3), Commercial Blast Cleaning, in accordance with the requirements of AWWA Specification No. C209, Cold-Applied  Tape Coatings.
Mechanical cleaning to SSPC-SP3, Power-Tool Cleaning, by use of power-tool wire brushing and other equipment is acceptable for all field joints, fittings and repairs.
Weld spatter, slag, sharp edges, burrs, slivers and knurls will be removed from pipe surface by filing, rasping, grinding, wire brushing, sanding or other suitable means.
Pipe joints and fittings will be primed and wrapped on the same day as cleaning is carried out.  Any flash rusting, surface oxidation, or other foreign contaminants will be removed by reprocessing through the necessary cleaning steps specified above.

## Field-Coating And Wrapping Of Weld Joints And Fittings
Weld joints and fittings for pipe sizes through 24” coated with X-Tru-Coat will be wrapped as follows:
Prior to welding, 6 inches of adhesive and plastic outer jacket will be removed from the cut end of field-cut pipe for joint cutback.  The plastic jacket will be wire-brushed at least 3 inches back form the bare pipe on both sides of the joint.
After welding the wire-brushed jacket and bare pipe will be given a uniform coat of Polyken 927 primer at a coverage of about 400 square feet per gallon.  Fittings will be similarly coated after cleaning.
The primer will be allowed to dry until the primer surface is tacky.  Manufacturer’s recommendation will be followed carefully.  Tape will not be applied over the primer until the primer is tacky.
Before the joint is wrapped, but after the primer is dry, two turns of Polyken 930-35 tape will be applied over the weld.
Beginning at a point 3 inches back on the plastic jacket, apply Polyken 930-35 tape with a 50 percent overlap to a point 3 inches past the beginning of the jacket on the adjacent length of pipe.
Spiral Wrap
A tension of five to ten pounds per inch width will be used in wrapping tape on the pipe.  After the last spiral lap has been made, sufficient tape will be cut from the roll on the down-side of the pipe to allow the tail portion of the tape to relax from the application tension before it is wrapped onto the coated pipe surface.

## Shop Inspection and Testing
It is the responsibility of the shop-coated vendor to visually inspect and electrically test the pipe prior to shipment.
Visual inspection will be made to ensure freedom of pipe coating from defects (surface blisters, craters, laminations and pinholes), and proper coating and wrapping of the pipe according to these specifications.
In addition to visual inspection, the coated pipe will be 100 percent holiday-tested using approved electrical holiday detection equipment in accordance with NACE Standard RP-02-74.  The detector will be set to acceptable voltages as recommended by the manufacturer.  The detector will travel at a rate not to exceed 1 foot per second, nor will it be allowed to remain stationary while the power is on.
All voids, pinholes and defects will be clearly marked, repaired and holiday-tested to restore the integrity of the coating system.

## Field Inspection, Repairs and Testing
The protective coating system will be visually inspected in the trench prior to placement of pipe bedding material and backfill.
In addition to visual inspection, the coated and wrapped pipe will be 100 percent holiday-tested using approved electric holiday detection equipment in accordance with NACE Standard RP-02-74.  The detector will be set to acceptable voltages as recommended by the manufacturer.  The detector will travel at a rate not to exceed 1 foot per second, nor will it be allowed to remain stationary while the power is on.
All dirt, scale and damaged coating will be removed using wire brushes, grinders or other suitable means.  Adjacent coating areas will be feathered out.  All dust will be wiped from the pipe.
Repairs will be in accordance with the weld joint material and application requirements previously described.
Repair areas will be holiday tested to ensure the integrity of the repaired coating.

## Handling Coated and Wrapped Pipe
Coated and wrapped pipe will be handled at all times with wide, nonabrasive slings, belts or other equipment to prevent damage to the coating.  Use of ropes of any kind, hooks, chains or cables will not be permitted on the coating.  End hooks may be used only on full-length pipe with 6-inch cutback at the ends.
During transport by rail, truck or ship, pipe will be supported at the ends on padded cradles or sawdust-filled bags of adequate strength.  Under no circumstances will wrapped pipe be allowed to rest directly on the ground or on unpadded hard surfaces.
Coated and wrapped pipe will be stored on skids or on wide blocks with resilient pads to prevent coating damage.
Stack heights for storage and transportation will be in accordance with recommendations of pipe coater.

## Backfill
Backfill adjacent to the coated and wrapped pipe will be free of scraps, sticks, rocks or other hard debris which could damage the coating.
