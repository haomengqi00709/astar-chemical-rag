1. 
PROCEDURE PURPOSE  
 
The purpose of the travel procedure is to ensure that we effectively manage the cost of 
travelling and ensure employees travel in a safe manner. Employees should make a 
commercial judgement as to whether the objective of the visit can be met without the need 
for travel. Only journeys which are essential for business and which have been properly 
authorised may be undertaken. This applies to journeys by road, rail or air. The number of 
employees involved in any journey must be kept to a minimum.  Travel to meetings 
involving only Group employees is to be discouraged and alternative communication 
methods considered. At all times, we will only book reservations with reliable suppliers to 
ensure we provide a quality service and give proper regard to travellers’ health and safety 
needs.  
 
Behave as though you were paying for the journey yourself – you are then helping to create 
a better financial result for the Aker Kvaerner Group and improved job prospects for all of 
your colleagues. 
 
2. 
PROCEDURE APPLICATION  
 
This procedure applies to all Operating Businesses and Corporate Staff Departments, 
including – to the extent Group can enforce application – joint ventures, partnerships and 
companies in which Group has a participating interest.  
 
Local statutory laws and regulations shall always be complied with. If anything in this 
Procedure is in conflict with such local statutory laws or regulations, the latter shall prevail.  
Business Unit Manager and the relevant Policy Owner shall be notified in writing of the 
conflict and necessary deviations taken from the Procedure to comply with the local 
statutory laws or regulations 
 
3. 
RESPONSIBILITIES 
 
3.1 
Line Management  
Will be responsible for applying this procedure and the authorisation, within the 
procedure's limits, of travel and related items such as car and hotel requirements. 
Each Company will specifically name a small number of nominated individuals, no 
more than 6-8, who are authorised to accept a higher cost, than that offered by the 
travel agency, where special requirements or circumstances exist. 
 
3.2 
Employees  
Each employee has a responsibility and a duty to follow the travel procedure and 
minimise travel expenses of his/her Company. Any employee not following the 
travel procedure may lose some or all of the travel expense reimbursement. 
Employees are requested to give notice of travel and accommodation requirement as 
far in advance of the intended date of travel as possible, ideally no less than seven

3.3 
Group Human Resources (HR) 
Will monitor the effectiveness of the procedure and be responsible for proposing

Business Area and Company HR

The Group Travel Network  
Will be chaired by Group HR and will be comprised of key individuals from

across the Group.  Their role will be to review the travel procedure, its effectiveness 
and to manage the negotiations with the main suppliers to ensure we maximise our 
purchasing power in the market. 
 
3.6 
Travel Agencies  
The Company will have a minimum number of nominated travel agencies, who will 
act as our booking agents and they must be utilised because they fulfil two major 
functions: 
 
To act as your full service bureau for all travel needs. They are to provide a level of 
service that is equal to, or better than, that available from any other external source. 
 
To play an active role in supporting our travel negotiations, through providing 
consolidated volume and location data, to ensure we achieve the best deals available 
with hotels, airlines, car rental companies, etc. 
 
The Travel Agency is instructed not to book a more expensive travel option unless 
and until they receive approval from one of the nominated representatives at the 
Business Area concerned. 
 
4. 
PROCEDURE DESCRIPTION 
 
4.1 
General 
Everyone should be aware that business travel is a huge item of expenditure for the 
Group. We have offices and projects in all parts of the world and extensive 
travelling. Before you plan a journey, always ask yourself if the journey is really 
necessary, or if you could perhaps do it by alternative means, such as video, Net 
Meeting or telephone conference call.

4.2 
Air Travel 
Airline ticketing and fare structures can be complicated and are subject to constant 
change.   The travel agency is authorised by the Company to select and recommend 
the most cost-effective airline and routing to meet the demands of the traveller and to 
maximise the cost saving opportunities for the Company. For example use of short-
term airline promotion deals, best discounts, low cost airlines, volume rebates, etc. 
  
ALL travel will be Economy Class for all domestic and international journeys, 
except for journeys of more than nine hours continuous flying, or twelve hours total 
flying time. In such situations, business class seating, by most economic route, will 
be permitted. Transatlantic flights may be booked in the most economic business 
class.  
 
However, world wide travel for mobilisation, demobilisation and holidays, related 
overseas assignments and secondments, will be economy class travel.  See 
International Assignment Procedure for further details. 
 
From time-to-time there may be special promotional fares offered by the airlines. If 
the cost difference between a return economy/coach class ticket and business class is 
insignificant, typically meaning around NOK 500, then the higher fare may be 
booked. 
 
The travel agency may recommend flights involving a connection, to achieve lowest 
cost airfares, but this will not normally exceed a stopover time of 2 hours. 
 
4.3  
Group and Meetings Travel  
Fares for groups of employees and meetings facilities can result in additional savings 
and these are generally offered by airlines for groups of passengers travelling to the 
same destination. When travel requirements, or meetings, fit this profile, particularly 
if meetings are in hotels, meeting arrangers must co-ordinate through our nominated 
travel agencies with a request for group or meeting rates.  Company premises, even 
if not part of the traveller’s Business Area, should be utilised whenever possible. 
 
4.4 
Rail Travel  
Rail travel will be standard class in all European and Western countries.  Alternative 
levels of travel, similar to standard class in European and Western countries, may 
only be utilised in underdeveloped countries with appropriate approval by a 
nominated person.

4.6 
Car Rental  
Currently the Group has entered into a global agreement with Avis and they are to be 
used exclusively, unless they do not exist at the location where transport is required. 
In such instance the designated travel agency will provide recommendations for best 
options. 
 
Employees should behave in the best commercial interests of the Company and in 
principle car rental should be kept to a minimum and the smallest practical size of 
car should be rented. If two or more employees are travelling together, then a Group 
B or C (medium size) car should be used. 
 
Travellers renting vehicles for business purposes in the United States, with Avis, 
should decline all additional insurance coverage offered. Avis rates are inclusive of 
insurance in the United States, with the exception of Alaska. Travellers should be 
aware what insurance coverage might be provided via their Business Area or 
Company, before accepting additional insurance from the rental company. 
 
The fuel tank should be refilled before returning the rental car to avoid extra 
administration charges over and above the cost of fuel replacement. 
 
4.7 
Safety 
The Group only deals with reputable, safe and reliable suppliers. All suppliers, 
including low cost airlines, hotels or other service providers are selected based upon 
their achievement of good industry standards. 
 
No more than three SVPs or VPs and their direct reports may travel on the same 
aeroplane, without prior approval of the Business Area EVP or local Company 
President/ Managing Director.

4.8 
Special Travel Arrangements  
On occasion, due to the remoteness of the destination or for operational reasons, 
special travel arrangements, such as the hire of charter aircraft, may be the most cost 
effective method of transporting a number of employees/customers/clients to the 
same location. Any such special travel arrangement must be justified on a case by 
case basis and must have the prior written approval of the Business Area EVP or 
local Company President/Managing Director. 
 
4.9 
Travel Expenses  
Where employees are eligible for daily travel and expense allowances (in lieu of 
receipted reimbursable expenses), these values are set by the Company in accordance 
with local custom and practice, usually within limits imposed by tax legislation. A 
schedule of allowances, by country, will be provided by the Business Area or 
Company travel or other nominated department. In all other circumstances, expenses 
will only be recoverable based on receipts. 
 
4.10 
Corporate Credit Card  
The Group has selected American Express as the global corporate credit card 
provider. Therefore all company expenses including, but not limited to, airline, 
hotel, car hire, meals and transportation, are to be charged to the individual corporate 
American Express cards, unless the American Express card is not accepted by the 
establishment.  In some locations, airline travel will be booked via a central billing 
American Express card.  
 
The guideline is that corporate credit cards should be issued to all employees who 
incur Company expenses in excess of Euro 400 per annum. 
 
Managers responsible for controlling the costs of their business will follow up on 
any significant cost discrepancies, with individual employees, to ensure that the 
procedure is being correctly applied.
