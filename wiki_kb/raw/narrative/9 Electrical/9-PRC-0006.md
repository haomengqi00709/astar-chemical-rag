# 9-PRC-0006 — Procedure


[TABLE: 9-PRC-0006_table_1]


## Scope
This Engineering Standard will be used on all Aker Kvaerner Chemetics projects located in countries that follow the IEC standards for the purpose of area classification of plants constructed in those countries.
These recommendations deal with the special precautions necessary to ensure the safe use of electricity in Aker Kvaerner Chemetics designed plants where flammable materials are manufactured, processed, handled or stored.  They are concerned with sources of ignition arising from the use of electricity, that is to say electrical arcs and sparks as well as heating effects.

## Intent
The intent of this standard is that electrical equipment and systems in hazardous (classified) locations should not provide a means of ignition for an ignitable mixture that may be present.

## Governing Regulations
These recommendations are based on the requirements and recommendations laid out in the following codes, regulations and recommended practices:
IEC	International Electrotechnical Commission, International Standard # 79–10, 1995.
This part of IEC # 79 is concerned only with the classification of hazardous areas where
flammable gas or vapours may exist.

## Assessment of Risk
In plants where flammable gases or vapours are present, a flammable atmosphere may be formed if these are released.  A flammable atmosphere may also exist inside plant equipment if air or oxygen is present together with a flammable material.  In the design and layout of the plant and plant equipment, a prime requirement must be the avoidance, as far as possible, of the creation of a flammable atmosphere.
For an ignition to take place at all, both a flammable atmosphere and a source of ignition need to exist simultaneously.
The preliminary step in classifying a plant then is to determine the existence of a flammable atmosphere.

## Area Classification Responsibility
The responsibility for the area classification of a plant rests jointly with the process and electrical departments as well as any other department responsible for plant safety and operation.
When assessing the area classification of a plant, the properties and process conditions of the materials to be handled, stored, processed or evolved, the volumes and dispositions and the possibilities of escape of flammable material, must be made available.  Practical experience regarding the conditions likely to exist during start-up and normal running shall be obtained where possible.  The foregoing information coupled with knowledge of design and construction details of the plant; the proposed ventilation, drainage, plant topography and other relevant factors will provide the basis for the classification.

## Definitions
The following definitions as per the IEC publication # 79 – 10:
Hazardous area: an area in which an explosive gas atmosphere is present, or may be expected to be present, in quantities such as to require special precautions for the construction, installation and use of apparatus.
Non-hazardous area:  an area in which an explosive gas atmosphere is not expected to be present in quantities such as to require special precautions for the construction, installation and use of apparatus
Hazardous areas are classified into zones:
Zone 0	An area in which an explosive gas atmosphere is present continuously or for long periods.
Zone 1	An area in which an explosive gas atmosphere is likely to occur in normal operation.
Zone 2	An area in which an explosive gas atmosphere is not likely to occur in normal operation and, if it does occur, is likely to do so only infrequently and will exist for a short period only.
Refer to the IEC publication # 79 – 10 for additional definitions and terms used regarding area classification of plants.

## Method of Classification (basic guidelines)

## Classification of Hazardous Area (for gases and vapours)
Classification of Hazardous Areas (for gases and vapours)
Refer to IEC publication # 79 – 10, clause # 3 titled, ‘Safety and area classification’.
Extent of Hazardous Areas
Refer to IEC publication # 79 – 10, clause # 4, titled, ‘Area classification procedure’.
The review and classifying of the plant requires input from the process, mechanical and electrical engineering personnel familiar with the plant and the equipment layout.
The following drawings and documentation must be reviewed to determine the area classification of the new plant and new equipment in an existing plant:
P&ID drawings;
Plant layout showing locations of all possible sources of hazards, (best in isometric projection) including all piping containing hazardous gases or vapours;
Plant site layout;
Civil / Structural drawings;
Drainage system information;
Owner’s existing plant area classification;
Plant site prevailing wind direction and mean speed;
Owner’s plant insurance company guidelines and requirements;
The process engineer must identify the equipment that may contain the flammable gases, vapours and/or liquids;
Studies done by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for similar existing plants.
Abbreviations:
UEL:  Upper explosive limit.
LEL:  Lower explosive limit
When the pieces of equipment and piping have been identified as containing flammable gases, vapours and /or liquids, then all possible sources of a leak to atmosphere must be identified.  Generally when flammable gases and vapours of liquids mix with the atmosphere, this mixture may become explosive depending on the UEL & LEL of the mixture.  The intention is to prevent the ignition of this explosive mixture by containing and/or eliminating any and all possible sources of ignition.
Each source of a leak must be reviewed and graded either:
continuous;
primary;
secondary.
The release rate at each source of a leak must be studied and classified.
The extent of a hazardous area depends on the estimated or calculated distance which a flammable atmosphere is capable of travelling before it disperses to a concentration in air below its LEL.
The dispersal is affected by the quantity escaping, the velocity of escape, the gas or vapour density and diffusion rate, the ventilation air currents and the topographical features of the plant involved.
With gases or vapours lighter than air, an escape at low velocity will disperse fairly rapidly upwards in a well-ventilated situation.  However, with gases or vapours heavier than air, an escape at low velocity will tend to flow downward any may travel long distances over the ground before it is safely dispersed by atmospheric diffusion.
The extent of the classified location may be defined by applying, with sound engineering judgement, the distances recommended in the diagrams shown in the IEC publication # 79 – 10.
Effects of New Equipment Added in an Existing Plant
When new equipment and/or piping is added to an existing plant, the area classification of the existing plant must be reviewed.  New equipment and/or piping may change the existing plant area classification from non-hazardous to a hazardous area or change the existing zone within the hazardous area.
If there are any changes to the existing plant area classification and the affected areas have been re-classified, then the electrical and instrumentation devices and equipment must be reviewed as to their suitability in the new area classification.  Any devices and equipment found not suitable for the new area classification must be replaced with approved units.
In locations where the costs are excessive to replace the electrical and instrumentation devices and equipment with the approved units; or where the devices and equipment are not manufactured to meet the new area classification, then the mechanical layout and selection of equipment should be reviewed and action taken as necessary.
Area Classification Documentation
The last step in the classification process is to produce an area classification drawing.  The different classified areas and their extent are shown on this drawing.
For each zone shown on the drawing, the gases and/or liquids that may exist due to a leak in each zone must be listed on the drawing.
Together with the area classification drawing and, if required due to the complexity of the many different zones, a written document to supplement the drawing mentioned above should be prepared.
When the area classification drawing(s) and the written documents are complete, these should be reviewed with the process, mechanical, civil/structural, instrument and electrical engineering personnel working on the project for any process changes that may alter the area classification of the plant.
The plant Owner may forward a copy of the drawing(s) and documents to the insurance company insuring the plant for comments and/or approval.  The area classification of the plant may affect the insurance premiums.
After the area classification drawing(s) and documents have been reviewed and approved by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics and the plant Owner, then copies must be given to the engineering personnel responsible for specifying, selecting and requisitioning the mechanical, instrumentation and electrical equipment to ensure the purchased equipment is suitable for the area classification in which it will be located

## Recommended Electrical Area Classification for Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Chemical Plants (Chloralkali, Sodium Chlorate and Chlorine Dioxide Plants)
In a typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics plant (NaOH, NaClO3 or ClO2), hydrogen containing water vapour (in all plants) and small amounts of oxygen and chlorine (in NaClO3 and ClO2 plants) is produced in the electrolytic cells and piped to its final destination (boiler, HCl unit etc.) or is vented to atmosphere.
Based on the IEC publication # 79-10 and Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' previous experience handling of hydrogen in chloralkali, sodium chlorate and chlorine dioxide plants, these plants are considered generally non-hazardous with the exceptions listed below.
The hydrogen gas is contained (i.e. non-classified) in the vessels and piping except for the following areas where hydrogen is or may be present. In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics plants, Hydrogen is transported in seamless welded pipes.
Chloralkali Plants
Hydrogen sample points which will normally be isolated with either blind flanges or double valves.
Seal leg drains which will be sealed with water.
Electrolyzer cells connecting flanges. Note: electrolyzer body not included.
PRV valve on top of catholyte tank which will be piped outside the building and which will be normally closed (function like rupture disk).
Main vent stack where hydrogen will be released continuously.  This vent is piped outside.
Tail gas scrubber vent (in case there is an HCl unit attached).  This vent is piped outside.
NaClO3 and ClO2 Plants
Hydrogen sample points which will normally be isolated with either blind flanges or double valves.
Main vent stack where hydrogen will be released continuously.  This vent is piped outside.
Tail gas scrubber vent (in case there is an HCl unit attached).  This vent is piped outside.
Hydrogen vent after the hydrogen scrubber where hydrogen will be released continuously.
Rupture disks which will normally be intact.
However as a precautionary measure, piping and instrumentation will be installed in such a manner that no spark producing device will be located closer than one (1) metre (3’-0”) from any hydrogen pipe flange.  Also, no spark producing in line devices will be used.  Lighting on the degasifier floor in a Chlorate plant should be vapour-tight and installed with top of fixture 300 mm min. (1’-0”) spacing from roof. Note that the boilers, HCl units etc. and their immediate areas are classified as non-hazardous with respect to electrical equipment and instruments.
Good natural building ventilation must be ensured at all times the Chlorate or Chlor-Alkali cell
ventilation and in case of an enclosed building the wall louvres must be open at the same time.
If roof vents can be closed, an interlock to the process should be provided to prevent the
Transformer / Rectifier from operating.
In chlorate plants the chlorate crystal and dust is flammable.  In the areas where the dust is
present, the equipment must be dust-tight.
These areas are classified as Zone 1 for a 1.5m (5’-0”) radius sphere around the vents.  These vents must be located outdoors.
The main vent stack (chloralkali)
The hydrogen scrubber vent (sodium chlorate and chlorine dioxide)
The tail gas scrubber vent (HCl plant)
The area from the degasifier and reactor rupture disc elevations to the building roof is classified as Zone 2.  Except for lighting fixtures, no electrical equipment will be located in these areas.  In the event of a rupture disc blowing and releasing some hydrogen gas, the plant will automatically shutdown and the hydrogen system will be purged with inert nitrogen to atmosphere.

## Recommended Electrical Area Classification for Aker Kvaerner Chemetics NCG Plants
In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics NCG plants, reduced sulphur gases (TRS) in concentrated form are collected, motivated with steam ejectors and eventually destroyed in incinerators, lime kilns or boilers.
The TRS gases are contained in the vessels and piping except for the following areas where TRS gases will or may be present.
Sample Points:  These sample points are equipped with a valve and are normally plugged or will have a gauge mounted.
Seal Leg Drains (on sealed sewer systems):  These seal legs are normally sealed with water and the high points are vented outside.
Rupture Disks:  The rupture disks are normally intact.  The downstream side is vented to the outside of the building.
Pressure/vacuum Breakers (PVB's):  These will be normally sealed with the vent line located outside of the building
Vent Stack(s):  Every time a NCG system shuts down, the vent valve opens and TRS gases will be present.  This is a normal operation.  This vent stack is always located outside and high up.
A list of the gases and the percentages of each will be used for assessing the above areas.
Based on the previously mentioned code (IEC) and Aker Kvaerner Chemetics previous experience in handling TRS gases, these plants are considered non-hazardous with the following exception:
The vent stack(s):  The area around the top of the stack is classified as Zone 1 for a 1.5 m (5’-0”) radius sphere.
Note:	Good natural building ventilation must be ensured at all times around the NCG ejector station.
