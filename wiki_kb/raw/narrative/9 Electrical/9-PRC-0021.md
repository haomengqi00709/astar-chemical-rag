# 9-PRC-0021 — Procedure


[TABLE: 9-PRC-0021_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure describes the requirements and documents for plant electrical numbering and identification of documents, drawings, electrical equipment, cables and conductors.

## Document numbering
Documents will be numbered in accordance with 0-PRC-0002: Document Numbering.

## Document and Drawing Numbers
Documents and drawing numbers will be numbered in accordance with 9-LST-0001: Electrical Document Numbering Standard.

## Electrical Equipment Identification
Electrical equipment identification will be numbered in accordance with 9-PRC-0001: Electrical Equipment Identification.  Electric motors will be numbered in accordance with 4-PRC-0001:  Plant Equipment Identification.

## Electrical Cable Numbering
All single and multi-conductor cables will be identified at each end.
The cable identification number tags will be comprised of four components, A-B-C-D.
A:	The starting location of the cable such as MCC, distribution panel, etc.  The number will be the equipment identification number.
B:	The final location of the cable such as a motor, control panel, etc.  The number will be the equipment identification number.
C:	The purpose of the cable, such as power, control, low voltage signal, etc.
D:	The sequential number of the field device or equipment the cable is connected to.
Examples:
9923E1-7875P1-P-1
9932E1:  motor control centre # 9923E1,
7875P1 :  motor number on the mechanical equipment,
P:   indicates power conductors,
1:  indicates one power feeder for this motor, 2 would indicate two power feeder cables.
9923E1-7875P1-C-1
C: indicates control conductors,
1: indicates one control cable for this motor, 2 would indicate a second control cable.
9903E-9920E-P
9903E:  switchgear # 9903E,
9920E:  power transformer # 9920E,
P:  power feeder cable.

## Electrical Conductor Numbering
All conductors in single and multi-conductor cables will be identified with a number at each end.
The conductor identification number tags will be comprised of two components, E-F.
E:  the equipment identification such as a motor,
F: the terminal number of the motor starter.
Examples:
7875P1-X2
7875P1:  the motor number,
X2:  the terminal number in the motor starter, origin of the conductor.
7875P1-3
7875P1: the motor number,
3:  the terminal in the motor starter, origin of the conductor.

## Instrument Signal Cables
See 8-PRC-0001: Plant Instrument Numbering for more information.

## Control System Input / Output Identification
See 8-PRC-0001: Plant Instrument Numbering for more information.
Commentary
To Section 3:  The list indicates the range of drawing and document numbers available for each for a project.  The PRC, SPC and DST document numbers are controlled by the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Electrical department and will not be used without the authorization of the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Electrical department manager.
The DTL, GAD, LST, SCM, MAN and CAL drawings numbers will be used as required on a project basis.  There are a range of numbers available for each type of drawing and any changes to these ranges of drawing numbers requires the authorization of the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Electrical department manager.
To Section 4:  The electrical equipment will be identified in accordance with the procedure noted.  Electric motors will be identified by a mechanical department procedure as the motors use the same number as the mechanical equipment driven by the motor.
To Section 5:  The cable numbering sequence is designed to have the first number as the origin of the cable and the destination as the second number.  The intent is to have all cables originating at a motor control centre and connecting to motors and other loads to carry the cable numbers showing the motor control centre identification number as the first number and load identification as the second number.
To Section 6:  The intent is to have a conductor of one continuous length without splices and terminations carry one conductor number and the number changes when there is a change of potential.  Conductor numbers may change on the other side of terminal strips as a result of a change of equipment identification.  Spliced conductors may carry the same conductor number when the splice is between the two terminal strips.
To Section 7:  The instrument signal cables require numbers and the numbering system is described in the document listed.
To Section 8:  The inputs and outputs between electrical equipment and the DCS require identification and this is described in the document listed.
To 2005 Name Change Conversion:  Name change has been implemented.  No new revision number has been used and changes have not been marked.
