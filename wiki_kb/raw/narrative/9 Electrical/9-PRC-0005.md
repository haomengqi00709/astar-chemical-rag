# 9-PRC-0005 — Procedure


[TABLE: 9-PRC-0005_table_1]


## Scope
These recommendations deal with the special precautions necessary to ensure the safe use of electricity in Aker Kvaerner Chemetics designed plants where flammable materials are manufactured, processed, handled or stored.  They are concerned with sources of ignition arising from the use of electricity, that is to say electrical arcs and sparks as well as heating effects.

## Intent
The intent of this standard is that electrical equipment and systems in hazardous (classified) locations should not provide a means of ignition for an ignitable mixture that may be present.

## Governing Regulations
These recommendations are based on the requirements and recommendations laid out in the following codes, regulations and recommended practices:
NFPA 70	National Electrical Code (USA)
(NEC)		Article 500: Hazardous (Classified) locations
NFPA		National Fire Protection Association
CEC		Canadian Electrical Code Section 18: Hazardous Locations
NFPA 497	Recommended Practice for the Classification of Flammable Liquids, Gases, or Vapours and of Hazardous (Classified) Locations for Electrical Installations in Chemical Process Areas.

## Assessment of Risk
In plants where flammable gases, liquids or dusts are present, a flammable atmosphere may be formed if they are released.  A flammable atmosphere may also exist inside plant equipment if air or oxygen is present together with a flammable material.  In the design and layout of plant and plant equipment, a prime requirement must be the avoidance, as far as possible, of the creation of a flammable atmosphere.
For an ignition to take place at all, both a flammable atmosphere and a source of ignition need to exist simultaneously.
The preliminary step in classifying a plant then is to determine the existence of a flammable atmosphere.  See paragraph 3 for more details.

## Area Classification Responsibility
The responsibility for the area classification of a plant rests jointly with the process and electrical departments as well as any other department responsible for plant safety and operation.
When assessing the area classification of a plant, the properties and process conditions of the materials to be handled, stored, processed or evolved, their volumes and dispositions and the possibilities of escape of flammable material, must be made available.  Practical experience regarding the conditions likely to exist during start-up and normal running will be obtained where possible.  The foregoing information coupled with knowledge of design and construction details of the plant, the proposed ventilation, drainage, plant topography and other relevant factors will provide the basis for the classification.

## Definitions
Hazardous area locations are defined in three different categories:
Class I		In which the combustible material is gas or vapour
Class II		In which the combustible material is dust
Class III		In which the combustible material is easily ignitable fibres or flyings
Note:	This procedure will deal with Class I hazardous locations only.
Within each of the classes mentioned above, two degrees of hazard are recognised:
Division 1	In Division 1, an ignitable mixture is likely to be present continuously or intermittently under normal conditions of operation.
Division 2		In Division 2, an ignitable mixture is likely to be present under abnormal operating conditions, such as failure of process equipment or flange leakage.
Non Classified	This definition can be applied to locations that are adequately ventilated, where combustible materials are contained within suitable, well maintained, closed piping systems. Locations that are not adequately ventilated (*) can also be defined as non classified if the piping systems are without flanges, valves, fittings and similar accessories that may be prone to leaks.
(*)	Adequate ventilation defined by NFPA 497 (1997 edition): a ventilation rate that affords either 6 air changes per hour, or 1 cfm per square foot of floor area, or other similar criteria that prevent the accumulation of significant quantities of vapor-air concentrations from exceeding 25% of the lower flammable limit.

The most important question to ask at this point is:
How well is the plant ventilated?
The extent of a hazardous area depends on the estimated or calculated distance which a flammable atmosphere is capable of travelling before it disperses to a concentration in air below its lower flammable limit.
The dispersal is affected by the quantity escaping, the velocity of escape, the gas or vapour density and diffusion rate, the ventilation air currents and the topographical features of the plant involved.
With gases or vapours lighter than air, an escape at low velocity will disperse fairly rapidly upwards in a well-ventilated situation.  (Typical hydrogen in chloralkali and sodium chlorate plants).  However, with gases or vapours heavier than air, an escape at low velocity will tend to flow downward any may travel long distances over the ground before it is safely dispersed by atmospheric diffusion (typical for gases/vapours in NCG plants).
NFPA 497 (1997) recommended practice for classification of Class I hazardous (classified) locations for electrical installation in chemical process areas shows typical examples with regards to the extent of the hazardous areas in diagram form for all conceivable Class I, Division 1 & Division 2 situations possible in a chemical plant.
The extent of the classified location may be defined by applying, with sound engineering judgement, the distances recommended in the diagrams mentioned above.

## Recommended Electrical Area Classification for Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Chemical Plants (Chloralkali, Sodium Chlorate and Chlorine Dioxide Plants)
In a typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics plant (NaOH, NaClO3 or ClO2), hydrogen containing water vapour (in all plants) and small amounts of oxygen and chlorine (in NaClO3 and ClO2 plants) is produced in the electrolytic cells and piped to its final destination (boiler, HCl unit etc.) or is vented to atmosphere.
Based on the National Electrical Code (NEC) Article 500 and 501, the Canadian Electrical Code (CEC) Section 18, as well as NFPA 497 "Recommended Practice for Classification of Flammable Liquids, Gases, or Vapors and of Hazardous (Classified) Locations for Electrical Installations in Chemical Process Areas" and Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' previous experience handling of hydrogen in chloralkali, sodium chlorate and chlorine dioxide plants, these plants are considered generally non-hazardous with the exceptions listed below.
The hydrogen gas is contained (i.e non classified) in the vessels and piping except for the following areas where hydrogen is or may be present. In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics plants, hydrogen is transported in seamless welded pipes.
Chloralkali Plants
Hydrogen sample points which will normally be isolated with either blind flanges or double valves.
Seal leg drains which will be sealed with water.
Electrolyzer cells connecting flanges. Note: electrolyzer body not included.
PRV valve on top of catholyte tank which will be piped outside the building and which will be normally closed (function like rupture disk).
Main vent stack where hydrogen will be released continuously.  This vent is piped outside.
Tail gas scrubber vent (in case there is an HCl unit attached).  This vent is piped outside.
NaClO3 and ClO2 Plants
Hydrogen sample points which will normally be isolated with either blind flanges or double valves.
Main vent stack where hydrogen will be released continuously.  This vent is piped outside.
Tail gas scrubber vent (in case there is an HCl unit attached).  This vent is piped outside.
Hydrogen vent after the hydrogen scrubber where hydrogen will be released continuously.
Rupture disks which will normally be intact.
However as a precautionary measure, piping and instrumentation will be installed in such a manner that no spark producing device will be located closer than one (1) metre (3’-0”) from any hydrogen pipe flange.  Also, no spark producing in line devices will be used.  Lighting on the degasifier floor in a Chlorate plant should be vapour-tight and installed with top of fixture 300 mm min. (1’-0”) spacing from roof. Note that the boilers, HCl units etc. and their immediate areas are classified as non-hazardous with respect to electrical equipment and instruments as per NFPA 497 (1997) Section 3.3.3.
Good natural building ventilation must be ensured at all times the Chlorate or Chlor-Alkali cell
room is in operation (i.e. the transformer/rectifier is energized).  The roof ridge ventilation and in
case of an enclosed building the wall louvres must be open at the same time.  If roof vents can
be closed, an interlock to the process should be provided to prevent the Transformer / Rectifier
from operating.
In chlorate plants the chlorate crystal and dust is flammable.  In the areas where the dust is
present, the equipment must be dust-tight.
The following areas are classified as Class I, Division 1 for a 1.5m (5’-0”) radius sphere around the vents.  These vents must be located outdoors.
The main vent stack (chloralkali)
The hydrogen scrubber vent (sodium chlorate and chlorine dioxide)
The tail gas scrubber vent (HCl plant)
The area from the degasifier and reactor rupture disc elevations to the building roof is classified as Class 1 Division 2.  Except for lighting fixtures, no electrical equipment will be located in these areas.  In the event of a rupture disc blowing and releasing some hydrogen gas, the plant will automatically shutdown and the hydrogen system will be purged with inert nitrogen to atmosphere.

## Recommended Electrical Area Classification for Aker Kvaerner Chemetics NCG Plants
In typical Aker Kvaerner Chemetics NCG plants, reduced sulphur gases (TRS) in concentrated form are collected, motivated with steam ejectors and eventually destroyed in incinerators, lime kilns or boilers.
The TRS gases are contained in the vessels and piping except for the following areas where TRS gases will or may be present.
Sample Points:  These sample points are equipped with a valve and are normally plugged or will have a gauge mounted.
Seal Leg Drains (on sealed sewer systems):  These seal legs are normally sealed with water and the high points are vented outside.
Rupture Disks:  The rupture disks are normally intact.  The downstream side is vented to the outside of the building.
Pressure/vacuum Breakers (PVB's):  These will be normally sealed with the vent line located outside of the building
Vent Stack(s):  Every time a NCG system shuts down, the vent valve opens and TRS gases will be present.  This is a normal operation.  This vent stack is always located outside and high up.
A list of the gases and the percentages of each will be used for assessing the above areas.
Based on the previously mentioned codes (CEC, NEC, NFPA 497) and Aker Kvaerner Chemetics previous experience in handling TRS gases, these plants are considered non-hazardous with the following exception:
The vent stack(s):  The area around the top of the stack is classified as Class I, Division 1 for a 1.5 m ( 5’-0” ) radius sphere.
Note:	Good natural building ventilation must be ensured at all times around the NCG ejector station.
