# 4-SPC-0024 — Specification


[TABLE: 4-SPC-0024_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this Standard is to define the workmanship and quality requirements for Zirconium (Grade 60702) welded fabrications purchased by/or on behalf of .

## Scope
This Standard applies when called for on purchase orders and referenced documents.
This Standard covers the welding and other fabrication requirements for vessels, heat exchangers, tanks, piping and miscellaneous welded items in the ASME P-61 & P-62 Material Classification.

## Definitions
Codes, etc., will be taken to mean latest edition ruling at date of contract.
Agreement in Writing - Where prior approval in writing is required by this Standard, this will be taken to mean submission in writing by letter and not merely by inclusion in approval drawings.
Inspector -  Quality Assurance Inspector or other Authorized Representative.

## General Requirements

### Sub-Contracting
No Sub-Contracting of welding is permitted without  written approval.

### Supervision
The fabricator will ensure that there is regular and systematic supervision of all work both in-shop and on-site by competent staff.

### Procedure Submittal
All welding procedures will be submitted by the fabricator for review and agreement by  Materials and Quality Assurance Group prior to commencement of any work.  The ASME Section IX PQR Form 483 will form part of this package.
A change in essential variables as outlined in ASME Code. Section IX will require re-qualification and re-submission of procedure(s).

### Welders Qualifications
All welders to be employed on  work will be suitably qualified in accordance with relevant codes and standards for every procedure he is to use.
The fabricator will, upon request, supply  with objective evidence of current welder qualifications.
All qualified welders will be allocated an identification symbol which will be identified on parent material adjacent to his work.

### Cleanliness
The surface and edges of any Zirconium material which is to be heated for any purpose (e.g. cutting, forming or welding) will be cleaned free from all traces of foreign matter (e.g. marking paint, oil, etc.) by disc grinding, wire brushing with a stainless steel brush and/or non-chlorinated solvent cleaning immediately prior to the application of heat.
The fabricator will take the controls necessary to prevent contamination of weldments at all stages of fabrication.  These controls will include the segregation of wire brushes, grinding wheels, etc., for use on Zirconium only.
On completion of work, items will be cleaned internally and externally. All fabrication markings, oxidation, weld spatter, etc., will be removed prior to crating/shipment.
All Zirconium welding will be carried out in a clean area within the shop, which is independent of, or suitably screened from, the rest of the fabrication facilities, so that dust ingress, iron contamination, etc., is minimized.  The screened working area  will be maintained free from drafts.

### Drawings
All work will be carried out in accordance with the  "Approved For Construction" shop detail drawing(s).  Deviations from the weld geometry as shown on approved drawing(s) are not permitted without the prior approval of ' QA & Materials Group.

## Materials

### Base Materials
All materials used will be as specified in the purchase order and referenced documents and conform to ASTM/ASME. Codes.  All Zirconium plate, sheet & strip will satisfy ASME/ASTM SB551.  All piping, tubing and fittings will conform to  ASME/ASTM SB523, SB658 and SB493, respectively.

### Filler Materials
All weld filler metals or consumables will meet the requirements of Section 8 of this Standard.
Filler materials should be kept warm, dry and clean.  If dirty, the wire will be wiped clean with a non-chlorinated solvent such as acetone or alcohol.  Only clean cellulose sponges or lint-free cloth will be used to apply solvents.
All fabricators will ensure and demonstrate to the Inspector that the manufacturer's instructions regarding the storage, drying and use of weld consumables are being fully implemented both in the shop and on site; in particular that all wet electrodes are discarded and not baked and re-used.
Prior to commencement of welding, the fabricator will furnish  with the brand name of the filler materials to be used.
Autogenous welding (i.e. no filler metal) will not be used.

### Certification
Mill Test Certification will be supplied with all materials including welding consumables.

### Traceability
The fabricator will maintain full traceability of materials back to original mill certification.
As a minimum the fabricator's internal controls will be such as to prevent the use of unspecified materials.
Lack of adequate identification or certification will be cause for rejection of the specific component or item.

## Preparation Of Edges For Welding

### Cutting to Size
Material will be cut to size by:
Machining
Shearing - Material over 3/16" thick will have extra material left on sheared edge to allow machining, grinding, etc., to ensure that any defects caused by shearing are removed.  Material over 1" thick will not be sheared.
Grinding - Utilizing silicon carbide or alumina type discs.
Thermal Cutting - It is preferable that thermal cutting will be carried out by the Plasma Arc Process and the welding edges lightly ground to bright metal.  Any other process will require that on completion of thermal cutting a minimum of 1/8" of metal be removed by mechanical means to ensure that all slag, scale, other irregularities and contaminants, including thermal effects, have been removed.
Forming - Material will be cold formed as far as practical operation.
Where forming is necessary, ideally, all forming tools will have been employed only on Zirconium.  Tools which have been used for other materials will be cleaned to remove surface contamination prior to their use with Zirconium.  All burns and indentations will be removed by grinding, to provide smooth surfaces prior to any forming operation.
Unless otherwise agreed, suitable protective interlayers will be used between tools and the Zirconium, to minimize metallic pick-up.

## Welding Processes

### Welding Methods
The Tungsten Inert Gas Welding Process is the preferred method of welding Zirconium.  Other process will be evaluated on submission of full details including procedure and exact location of proposed weldments.
Gas tungsten arc welding rods will satisfy the requirements of SFA-5.24 and fall within the F-61 Filler Metal Group Classification.
Preferred electrode is thoriated tungsten as per SFA-5.12, Class EWTh-2.
High frequency (HF) voltage will be used to start the arc.
Weld metal thickness will not exceed 1/8"/pass.
Where necessary a back-up gas (normally 100% argon with 99.9% purity) will be used to prevent root pass oxidation and contamination.  This requirement is mandatory for root pass welds on piping or other items where access to the internal weld surface is limited or non-existent.

### Postweld Heat Treatment
Preheat is not required.  However, when adverse weather conditions give rise to condensation or wetness on the surfaces of the component, the material will be dried prior to welding by heating to a temperature just sufficient to prevent condensation.
The fabricator will submit his procedure in writing for approval prior to commencement of work.  This procedure will include information on the number and location of thermocouples and supports and satisfy the following requirements.

### Stress Relieving and Anealing
Where specified, zirconium welding will be post weld heat treated in a neutral or oxidising atmosphere as follows:
Prior to heat treatment all zirconium components will be degreased 9chlorinated solvents are not allowed).
Once the Zr components have been degreased they should not be handled or otherwise contaminated with bare hands.  Clean gloves or stainless steel tongues will be used.
Where furnace heat-treatment is used, the article(s) will be loaded into the furnace at a temperature below 315°C (600°F).
Heating and cooling rates will not exceed 315°C/hour between 315°C and 482°C  (600°F and 900°F), or 93°C (200°F) / hour above 482°C 500°C (1050 °F).
For Stress Relief Annealing:  Soak at 550°C  (1050°F) + 10°C for 1 hour/25 mm thickness 930 min. minimum), followed by furnace cooling to 315°C  (600°F).
Where recrystallization is required - for example after heavy cold work:  The zirconium will be heated to 790°C (1450°F) for 1 hour/25 mm thickness 930 min. minimum), followed by furnace cooling to 315°C (600°F).
Furnace recorder charts will be supplied.

## Workmanship

### Procedure Tests
The welding procedure and associated tests will be agreed (Refer Para 5.3) upon before any production welding is begun.

### Welding Position
As far as practical, all welds will be made in the downhand (flat) position.
Vertical welds will be made vertical up (uphand).

### Weather Protection for Welding on Site
Suitable enclosures will be provided to keep welds and welders free from draughts, rain or other inclement weather.  Welding will not be performed on wet surfaces.

### Fusion Faces
The fusion faces and the adjacent metal, both internal and external, will be free from moisture, scale, paint, grease or other foreign matter immediately prior to welding.

### Alignment of Joints
The alignment of welding edges at butt joints will be in accordance with relevant code, specification, etc., but maximum values for misalignment (High - Low) will be as follows:
For plates up to and including 1/4" in thickness, 1/16"
For plates above 1/4" in thickness, 25% of the plate thickness or 1/8" whichever is smaller.
For pipe, will be in accordance with ASME B31.3.
Prior to welding a slip-on flange, the sum of the gaps on any diameter will not exceed 3/16" (5mm).

### Assembly for Welding
To maintain the specified alignment and gap (where used) during welding, the parts to be welded will be securely held in position by mechanical means, or by tack welding.  Reinforcing rods and other shaped items will closely conform to their matching curvature.

### Mechanical Means
Where a jig or other suitable device is used, the construction of the jig will allow adequate access for welding and cleaning and will be such that external loading is not applied to a joint during welding.

### Tack Welds
Tack welds employed for assembly purposes may form part of the completed weld provided that complete fusion is effected at the root of the weld and that the welding procedure used is the same as that employed for the remainder of the root run.
Cracked and unsound tack welds will be cut out prior to welding.
Sound tack welds must be thoroughly fused into the root run of the main weld. It is sometimes necessary to thin down the ends of tack welds to make sure this is possible.
Where this is necessary tack welds should preferably be at least one inch long to ensure that the remaining part of the tack weld is strong enough to hold the plate or pipe in place.

### Backing Bars
The use of permanent back-up rings or strips or consumable joint inserts is not permitted unless specifically approved in writing by  QA and Materials Group.

### Striking of Welding Arc
Every effort will be made to confine arc strikes to the surface on which weld metal is to be deposited.  If such a strike should occur outside this area the spot will require to be ground, PT examined per Para. 10.3 and, if necessary, repaired.
Burn marks from grounding clamps will be treated as arc strikes.

### Cleaning and Re-Starting
Each run of weld metal will be thoroughly cleaned and all slag and foreign matter removed before the next run is deposited.  On re-starting, care will be taken to ensure proper fusion with the weld metal previously deposited.

### Electrode Touch Down
If a tungsten electrode accidentally makes contact with the work, the section of weld and the tip of the electrode will be examined.  Where tungsten loss has occurred, the tungsten pick-up in the weld will be ground out and the electrode reground to the correct profile before re-starting.

### Peening and Hammering
Peening of weld runs or hammering of finished welds is not permitted except for light peening to remove slag.

### Form of Welds
Each weld will be uniform in width and size throughout its full length.  Each layer of welding will be smooth and free of slag, inclusions, cracks, porosity, lack of fusion, and undercut except to the extent permitted in the referenced codes.  In addition, the cover pass will be free of coarse ripples, irregular surface, non-uniform bead patterns, high crown, and deep ridges or valleys between beads.
Buttwelds will be slightly convex, of uniform height, and have full penetration.
Crevices will not be permitted on the process side of completed weldments.
As far as is practicable, fillet welds will be deposited in not less than two layers and their surfaces will be free from marked irregularities.
Dimensions of fillet welds will be in accordance with the approved drawing.

### Dressing of Welds & Repair Areas
Grinding or gouging will not be carried out in such a manner as to reduce the adjacent base metal thickness.
Grinding wheels will be iron-free and of the resin bonded alumina or silica/silicon type.
Wire brushes or chipping tools will be made of austenitic stainless steel and must not have been previously used to clean surfaces other than zirconium.

## Quality Control
The Inspector will have free access to the Vendor's work and to his Subvendor's, if work is sublet by approval, at all reasonable times.
He will be at liberty to inspect the work during fabrication at any stage in manufacture and to reject any material or workmanship, which does not conform to this Standard or the applicable code.
Inspection by  does not relieve the fabricator or erector of responsibility for providing materials, components and workmanship in accordance with the requirements of this Standard, engineering design and the applicable code.

### Chemical Analysis
Supplier must make available to purchaser's inspector copies of chemical certification for batch of electrodes or wire coils used.  If electrodes, wire, etc., cannot be identified to ' satisfaction, samples will be removed for chemical analysis.  This will be to supplier's account.
Where specified spot check analysis will be performed on production welds.
The weld(s) to be checked will be selected at random by the ' Inspector.
Where results of chemical analysis show that a specific weldment falls outside of the approved specification or code requirements, the supplier will promptly advise  in writing of his proposed course of action.  Such action will include details of any additional testing necessary to fully define the extent of the non-conformance.  All repairs and retests will be to the supplier's account.

### Production Weld Tests
Production Weld Testing will satisfy the requirements of para UNF 95 of the ASME Code Section VIII Div. 1

### Dye Penetrant Inspection (PT)
Items fabricated in accordance with the ASME Code Section VIII, Div. l, will be PT tested on all welds prior to Heat Treatment.
Unless otherwise specified on the purchase order, approved drawings or Standards, non-coded items will have butt and fillet welds, nozzle attachment welds, etc., 100 percent PT examined on all surfaces exposed to the process prior to heat treatment, for cracks, porosity, slag inclusions, significant undercutting or other major defects.
will reserve the right to select the location of the above inspection.
Tube to tubesheet welds will be l00% PT examined.
Heat treated welds will receive a 10% dye penetrant inspection.  This inspection will be representative of each type of heat-treated weld detail and will be increased to 100% coverage upon revelation of any rejectable defects.
The PT exam will be carried out in accordance with a written procedure and in general, the limit of acceptability will satisfy the ASME Code Section VIII, Division 1, Appendix VIII, except that linear indications will be considered unacceptable.
All excavated areas will be PT examined prior to building up flush with the surface of the base material using the welding procedure previously approved by  for the main welding details.
All weld repair surfaces will be l00% PT examined per the above requirements.

### Radiographic Inspection (RT)
Equipment fabricated in accordance with the ASME Code will have all butt welds 100% radiographed in accordance with Para. UW-5l of Section VIII, Division 1 of the ASME Code.
For piping fabricated in accordance with ASME Codes and this Standard, the minimum standard of radiography will be random radiography as defined in Para. 341.4.1 (b) of ASME B3l.3 except that 20% of all butt welds will be radiographed.
For tankage fabricated in accordance with API Standard 620 and this Standard, the minimum requirements will be spot radiography.
Items other than those described above, fabricated to this Standard only (i.e. not ASME, etc.), will if required by  engineering drawings be spot radiographed in accordance with UW-52 (b) or 20% random radiographed B3l.3 in case of piping.
RT Exam will be carried out in accordance with a written procedure by personnel qualified to SNTC-1A.
Where additional radiography is required, as defined in UW-52 Sub-para. (d), costs of this radiography including all repair costs and re-radiography will be to the fabricator's account.
Junctions between circumferential and longitudinal seams will be considered as preferred locations for spot radiography.

### Helium Mass Spectrometer Test
Tube/tube sheet joints and tube bundles will be tested using the helium mass spectrometer (reverse probe method) per ASME Code Section V, Article l0.

### Documentation
The fabricator will furnish a report containing all the data pertaining to each vessel or item as follows:
Prior to ' final inspection of each unit, the fabricator will have prepared in finished form, three sets of records, except final test data, as set forth in this paragraph.
Material mill test reports
Nameplate rubbing
Hydrostatic test report
A copy of all inspection and test reports
Manufacturer's Affidavit
Inspection/Release Form
These reports, together with final test data, will be assembled into three complete sets for each item.  In no case will a documentation package pertain to more than one vessel or item number.
The Inspector will at the time of witnessing the final tests and certifying correctness of forms, collect the appropriate number of documentation packages for each item tested.
