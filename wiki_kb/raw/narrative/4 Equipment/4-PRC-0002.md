Safety and Environmental
The consideration of personnel safety during construction, ongoing operation and maintenance, in the design, selection and installation of mechanical equipment is paramount.
Protection will be provided to prevent operations and maintenance personnel from coming into contact with exposed items of plant where the surface temperature is assessed as being likely to cause personal injury. Any exposed surface exceeding 60C is considered to be a hazard and will be subject to a risk assessment.
The containment and control of dust produced by the normal operation of some equipment (such as conveyors) will be considered with respect to unwanted ingress into other Plant Areas and equipment
Site Conditions
Mechanical Equipment will be suitable for operation outdoors, in direct sunlight and exposure to all natural elements and in an environment as described in the datasheet 1-DST-0001: Site Design Conditions (as applicable).  Indoor environment(s) will be considered as applicable.
Noise
See 4-SPC-0001: General Equipment Requirements

### Material and Component Selection
All materials used for permanent facilities will be new and of merchantable quality.
Designers will consider both internal and external environments, fugitive emissions and plant fluid spills when selecting materials and protective coatings.
Aluminum and copper alloys will not be used in direct contact with the process and will in general be avoided wherever possible (the obvious exception is electrical and control equipment).
Asbestos containing materials are prohibited.
Galvanic action via contact of dissimilar metals will be prevented.
The componentry of all equipment that is not permanently sealed from the atmosphere will be reviewed and selected for its compatibility with the environment, including all fugitive emissions.

### Design
General
Mechanical plant and equipment will be suitable for the particular environmental conditions in which it will be operated. Particular plant areas may be subject to abrasive dust-laden atmospheres, corrosive conditions, acidic fluids, hose-down by process water, and direct sunlight and rain.
Equipment will be designed to prevent the accumulation of spilled material, dust and water.
Hydraulic systems will be designed and manufactured such that they are fully sealed, allowing no ingress of air or foreign matter into the system.
For general service conditions, the hydraulic fluid will be mineral based. For high temperature systems the generation of hazardous areas will be avoided by the use of synthetic phosphate ester fluids.
Piping connections will be ANSI or equipment manufacturer’s standard.  Steel bolts for tanks and vessels will be heavy hex hot dip galvanized.
The minimum size for vessel and tank nozzles will be DN25.
Duty
Equipment will heavy duty, industrial standard and be designed for operation under the full range of operating conditions non-steady state, start-up, shutdown and turndown.

### Maintenance and Operational Issues
General
All equipment design will take into consideration the general access requirements for ongoing operation and maintenance. Equipment and components of equipment subject to wear, corrosion, erosion or any physically depleting effects, will be easily removable for changeover. For example, such flexibility would allow agitators to be removed from tanks without the need for confined space entry and with adequate lifting access.
Buildings, enclosures and cabinets housing equipment will be designed with consideration of all maintenance operations, in terms of dismantability and accessibility within.
Permanent facilities for pumpout, steamout, purging and flushing will be included on all equipment where necessary.
Equipment will be designed so that safe isolation under a permit system may be performed with ease.
In hydraulic systems test points will be provided in all actuator lines, pressure supply lines to directional control valves and at points necessary to test the fluid pressure in hydraulic accumulators. Hydraulic test points will be incorporated into air valving manifold blocks for testing respective pressures.
Access
Permanent access to mechanical equipment will be provided based upon the requirement of regular access by operational and maintenance personnel.  Purposes such as plant operation, inspection, maintenance, condition monitoring, isolation under a permit system, overhaul and cleaning will be considered.
Make provision for the collection of potential process contaminants, such as residual process fluids or hydraulics oils, in drip trays, and for subsequent removal through drain points. Guarding for rotating machinery will be accessible, and designed to facilitate tachometer readings.
The minimum size of access manways considered safe and practical for general access requirements is 600 mm. If tanks are too small in diameter to comply with this manway size requirement then top entry will be provided via a full diameter removable tank top.
Permanent storage tanks will be provided with a minimum of one access manway and a roof vent large enough for air ventilation during confined space entry.
An access stairway will be provided to elevated equipment when:
Plant operating or maintenance personnel require access on a daily basis, or more regularly
The particular item of equipment is critical to plant operation
Scheduled maintenance or unscheduled repair requirements of the particular item of equipment is labour intensive or requires heavy tools to be hand carried.
