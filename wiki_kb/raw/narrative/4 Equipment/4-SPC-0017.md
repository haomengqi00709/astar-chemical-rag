# 4-SPC-0017 — Specification


[TABLE: 4-SPC-0017_table_1]


## Purpose
The purpose of this Standard is to define the workmanship and quality requirements for 6 percent Moly austenitic stainless steel (UNS S31254) welded fabrications purchased by/or on behalf of Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.

## Scope
This Standard applies when called for on purchase orders and referenced documents.
This Standard covers the welding and other fabrication requirements for vessels, heat exchangers, tanks, piping and miscellaneous welded items in the ASME P-8 Material Classification Group 4.
Stainless steels such as Avesta 254 SMO® fall into the above ASME classification.

## Definitions
Codes, etc., will be taken to mean latest edition ruling at date of contract.
Agreement in Writing - Where prior approval in writing is required by this Standard, this will be taken to mean submission in writing by letter and not merely by inclusion in approval drawings.
Inspector - Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Quality Assurance Inspector or other Authorized Representative.

## General Requirements

### Sub-Contracting
No Sub-Contracting of welding is permitted without Aker Kvaerner Chemetics written approval.

### Supervision
Fabricator will ensure that there is regular and systematic supervision of all work both in-shop and on-site by competent staff.

### Procedure Submittal
All welding procedures will be submitted by Fabricator for review and agreement by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Materials and Quality Assurance Group prior to commencement of any work.  The ASME Section IX PQR Form 483 will form part of this package.
A change in essential variables as outlined in ASME Code. Section IX will require re-qualification and re-submission of procedure(s).

### Welders Qualifications
All welders to be employed on Aker Kvaerner Chemetics work will be suitably qualified in accordance with relevant codes and standards for every procedure he is to use.
Fabricator will, upon request, supply Aker Kvaerner Chemetics with objective evidence of current welder qualifications.
All qualified welders will be allocated an identification symbol which will be identified on parent material adjacent to his work.

### Cleanliness
The surface and edges of any austenitic steel which is to be heated for any purpose (e.g. fusion cutting, forming or welding) will be cleaned free from all traces of foreign matter (e.g. marking paint, oil, etc.) by grinding, wire brushing with a stainless steel brush and/or non-chlorinated solvent cleaning immediately prior to the application of heat.
Fabricator will take the controls necessary to prevent contamination of weldments at all stages of fabrication.  These controls will include the segregation of wire brushes, grinding wheels, etc., for use on stainless steels only.
On completion of work, items will be cleaned internally and externally. All fabrication markings, oxidation, weld spatter, etc., will be removed prior to shipment.
Passivation and Ferroxyl testing where required by Purchase Order/referenced documents, will be carried out in accordance with written procedures which will be approved by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics prior to use.

### Drawings
All work will be carried out in accordance with the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics "Approved For Construction" shop detail drawing(s).  Deviations from the weld geometry as shown on approved drawing(s) are not permitted without the prior approval of Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' QA. & Materials Group.

## Materials

### Base Materials
All materials used will be as specified in the purchase order and referenced documents and conform to ASTM/ASME Codes.  All austenitic steel plate will be in the hot-rolled, annealed and descaled condition per ASTM A240.  All piping and fittings will be in the annealed condition and conform to  ASTM A312 and A182, respectively.  All tubing will conform to ASTM A269.

### Filler Materials
All weld filler metals or consumables will meet the requirements of Section 8 of this Standard.
Fabricator will ensure and demonstrate to the Inspector that the manufacturer's instructions regarding the storage, drying and use of weld consumables are being fully implemented both in the shop and on site; in particular that all wet electrodes are discarded and not baked and re-used.
Prior to commencement of welding, Fabricator will furnish Aker Kvaerner Chemetics with the brand name of the filler materials to be used.
Autogenous welding (i.e. no filler metal) will not be used.

### Certification
Mill Test Certification will be supplied with all materials including welding consumables.

### Traceability
The fabricator will maintain full traceability of materials back to original mill certification.
As a minimum the fabricator's internal controls will be such as to prevent the use of unspecified materials.
Lack of adequate identification or certification will be cause for rejection of the specific component or item.

## Preparation Of Edges For Welding

### Cutting to Size
Material will be cut to size by:
Machining
Shearing
Material over 3/16" thick will have extra material left on sheared edge to allow machining, grinding, etc., to ensure that any defects caused by shearing are removed. Material over 1" thick will not be sheared.
Thermal Cutting
It is preferable that thermal cutting will be carried out by the Plasma Arc Process and the welding edges lightly ground to bright metal.  Any other process will require that on completion of thermal cutting a minimum of 1/8" of metal be removed by mechanical means to ensure that all slag, scale, other irregularities and contaminants, including thermal effects, have been removed.
Grinding

### Forming
Material will be cold formed as far as practical.  When hot forming is necessary, the procedure to be adopted will be agreed to in writing by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics prior to commencement of work.  A solution anneal will be necessary after the hot working operation.

## Welding Processes

### Welding Methods
The following processes are the preferred methods of welding 6 percent Moly. austenitic steel.  Other process will be evaluated on submission of full details including procedure and exact location of proposed weldments.
Shielded Metal Arc Welding	(SMAW)
Gas Tungsten Arc Welding		(GTAW)
Gas Metal Arc Welding		(GMAW)

### Shielded Metal Arc Welding
Shielded metal arc welding electrodes will satisfy the requirements of SFA-5.4 and contain a minimum of 9% molybdenum e.g. Avesta P12R.
The use of Type 15 electrodes for normal fillet and butt welds is recommended with DCRP-Electrode Positive, power source.
Minimum practical amperage, stringer beads rather than weave and minimum arc length are recommended in order to minimize heat input.

### Gas Tungsten Arc Welding
Where the root of GTAW welded pipe is exposed to process conditions, then a minimum of 9% Molybdenum is required for filler.
Consideration will be given to implementation of guidelines in "TAPPI TI5 GTAW root pass welding of 6% Molybdenum Austenitic Stainless Steel Pipe - open root joint with hand fed filler".
Gas tungsten arc welding rods will satisfy the requirements of SFA-5.14 e.g Avesta P12.
Preferred electrode is thoriated tungsten as per SFA-5.12, Class EWTh-2.
High frequency (HF) voltage will be used to start the arc.
Weld metal thickness will not exceed 1/8"/pass.  Heat input will be kept low by using stringer bead technique.
Where necessary a back-up gas (normally 100% argon with 99.9% purity) will be used to prevent root pass oxidation and contamination.  This requirement is mandatory for root pass welds on piping or other items where access to the internal weld surface is limited or non-existent.

### Gas Metal Arc Welding
Welding Electrodes will satisfy the requirements of SFA-5.9 or SFA-5.22 unless otherwise approved by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.
GMAW will only be used for the root pass of butt welds and for fillet welds unless specifically agreed otherwise.  Gas backing is required where accessibility to the internal weld surface is limited.

### Submerged Arc Welding
Use of this process must be reviewed and approved in writing by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics prior to start of fabrication.
Since the flux wire choice is critical, a chemical analysis of the finished weld deposit must be included as part of the weld procedure qualification.  Production chemical analysis will also be performed on a random basis (twice/vessel).
Fabricator must provide satisfactory evidence of continuous shop use of this process in the fabrication of austenitic stainless steel.

### Preheat, Interpass Temp. & Postweld Heat Treatment
Preheat is not required.  However, when adverse weather conditions give rise to condensation or wetness on the surfaces of the component, the material will be dried prior to welding by heating to a temperature just sufficient to prevent condensation.
Heat input during welding should be kept to a minimum.  Interpass temp. should not exceed 250o F.
Postweld Heat Treatment is seldom required.  In the event that PWHT is specified the fabricator will submit his procedure in writing for approval prior to commencement of work.  This procedure will include information on the number and location of thermocouples and supports.
Attachments to thin walled vessels etc. will be cooled by auxiliary methods (e.g. water spray on opposite surface to that being welded) in order to minimize heat affected zone effects which could impact on corrosion performance.

## Workmanship

### General

#### Procedure Tests
The welding procedure and associated tests will be agreed (Refer Para 5.3) upon before any production welding is begun.

#### Welding Position
As far as practical, all welds will be made in the downhand (flat) position.
Vertical welds will be made vertical up (uphand).

#### Weather Protection for Welding on Site
Suitable enclosures will be provided to keep welds and welders free from draughts, rain or other inclement weather.  Welding will not be performed on wet surfaces.

#### Fusion Faces
The fusion faces and the adjacent metal, both internal and external, will be free from moisture, scale, paint, grease or other foreign matter immediately prior to welding.

### Alignment of Joints
The alignment of welding edges at butt joints will be in accordance with relevant code, specification, etc., but maximum values for misalignment (High - Low) will be as follows:
For plates up to and including 1/4" in thickness, 1/16"
For plates above 1/4" in thickness, 25% of the plate thickness or 1/8" whichever is smaller.
For pipe, will be in accordance with ASME B31.3.
Prior to welding a slip-on flange, the sum of the gaps on any diameter will not exceed 3/16" (5mm).

### Assembly for Welding
To maintain the specified alignment and gap (where used) during welding, the parts to be welded will be securely held in position by mechanical means, welded-on bridge pieces or by tack welding.  Reinforcing rods and other shaped items will closely conform to their matching curvature.

#### Mechanical Means
Where a jig or other suitable device is used, the construction of the jig will allow adequate access for welding and cleaning and will be such that external loading is not applied to a joint during welding.

#### Bridge Pieces/Support Spiders
Welded-on bridge pieces or support spiders will be of austenitic steel on the surface welded in contact with the vessel or heat exchanger.
Where welded-on bridge pieces or support spiders are used, the plate surface will not be left in a damaged condition after they are removed.

#### Tack Welds
Tack welds employed for assembly purposes may form part of the completed weld provided that complete fusion is effected at the root of the weld and that the welding procedure used is the same as that employed for the remainder of the root run.
Cracked and unsound tack welds will be cut out prior to welding.
Sound tack welds must be thoroughly fused into the root run of the main weld. It is sometimes necessary to thin down the ends of tack welds to make sure this is possible.
Where this is necessary tack welds should preferably be at least one inch long to ensure that the remaining part of the tack weld is strong enough to hold the plate or pipe in place.

#### Backing Bars
The use of permanent back-up rings or strips or consumable joint inserts is not permitted unless specifically approved in writing by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics QA. and Materials Group or shown on the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Drawings.

### Striking of Welding Arc
Every effort will be made to confine arc strikes to the surface on which weld metal is to be deposited.  If such a strike should occur outside this area the spot will require to be ground, PT examined per Para. 10.3 and, if necessary, repaired. Burn marks from grounding clamps will be treated as arc strikes.

### Cleaning and Re-Starting
Each run of weld metal will be thoroughly cleaned and all slag and foreign matter removed before the next run is deposited.  On re-starting, care will be taken to ensure proper fusion with the weld metal previously deposited.

### Electrode Touch Down
If a tungsten electrode accidentally makes contact with the work, the section of weld and the tip of the electrode will be examined.  Where tungsten loss has occurred, the tungsten pick-up in the weld will be ground out and the electrode reground to the correct profile before re-starting.

### Peening and Hammering
Peening of weld runs or hammering of finished welds is not permitted except for light peening to remove slag.

### Form of Welds
Each weld will be uniform in width and size throughout its full length.  Each layer of welding will be smooth and free of slag, inclusions, cracks, porosity, lack of fusion, and undercut except to the extent permitted in the referenced codes.  In addition, the cover pass will be free of coarse ripples, irregular surface, non-uniform bead patterns, high crown, and deep ridges or valleys between beads.
Buttwelds will be slightly convex, of uniform height, and have full penetration.
Crevices will not be permitted on the process side of completed weldments.
As far as is practicable, fillet welds will be deposited in not less than two layers and their surfaces will be free from marked irregularities.
Dimensions of fillet welds will be in accordance with the approved drawing.

### Dressing of Welds & Repair Areas
Grinding or gouging will not be carried out in such a manner as to reduce the adjacent base metal thickness.
Grinding wheels will be iron-free and of the resin bonded alumina or silica type.
Wire brushes or chipping tools will be made of austenitic stainless steel and must not have been previously used to clean surfaces other than stainless steels.

## Quality Control

### General
The Inspector will have free access to Vendor's work and to his Subvendor's, if work is sublet by approval, at all reasonable times.
He will be at liberty to inspect the work during fabrication at any stage in manufacture and to reject any material or workmanship, which does not conform to this Standard or the applicable code.
Inspection by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics does not relieve the Fabricator of responsibility for providing materials, components and workmanship in accordance with the requirements of this Standard, engineering design and the applicable code.

### Chemical Analysis
Supplier must make available to purchaser's inspector copies of chemical certification for batch of electrodes or wire coils used.  If electrodes, wire, etc., cannot be identified to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' satisfaction, samples will be removed for chemical analysis.  This will be to Supplier’s account.
A spot check analysis as outlined in Section 8.5 will be performed on production welds of critical equipment such as pressure vessels, heat exchangers, pressure piping and certain proprietary items.  This will also include non-pressure retaining equipment in dangerous or demanding services.
The weld(s) to be checked will be selected at random by the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' Inspector.
Where results of chemical analysis show that a specific weldment falls outside of the approved specification or code requirements, Supplier will promptly advise Aker Kvaerner Chemetics in writing of his proposed course of action.  Such action will include details of any additional testing necessary to fully define the extent of the non-conformance.  All repairs and retests will be to Supplier’s account.

### Dye Penetrant Inspection (PT)
Unless otherwise specified in the purchase order, approved drawings, specifications/codes, etc., nozzle attachment weld surfaces, all butt weld surfaces including skirt to shell welds will be 100% PT examined for their complete length for cracks, porosity, slag inclusions, significant undercutting or other major defects.
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics will reserve the right to select the location of the above inspection.
Unless otherwise specified in the Purchase Order or approved drawings, all welds between dissimilar metals will be 100% PT examined on the root pass and completed weld surfaces.
Tube to tubesheet welds will be 100% PT examined.
The PT exam will be carried out in accordance with a written procedure and in general, the limit of acceptability will satisfy the ASME Code Section VIII, Division I, Appendix VIII, except that linear indications will be considered unacceptable.
All excavated areas will be weld built-up flush with the surface of the base material using the welding procedure previously approved by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for the main welding details.
Weld repair surfaces will be PT examined per the above requirements.
Aker Kvaerner Chemetics reserves the right to accept or reject any doubtful or questionable indications.

### Radiographic Inspection  (RT)
Where specified on Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' Engineering drawings, items fabricated in accordance with this Standard and the ASME Code will be spot radiographed in accordance with Para.  UW-52(b) of Section VIII, Division 1 of the ASME Code which clearly defines the following requirements:
Minimum extent of Spot Radiographic Examination
Standards for Spot Radiographic Examination
Evaluation and Retests
For piping fabricated in accordance with ASME Codes and this Standard, the minimum standard of radiography will be 5 percent random radiography as defined in Para.  341.4.1(b) of ASME B31.3.
RT Exam will be carried out in accordance with a written procedure by personnel qualified to SNTC-lA.
Where additional spot radiography is required, as defined in UW-52 Sub-para.  (d)(2), costs of this radiography including all repair costs and re-radiography will be to the fabricator's account.
Where more than one welder or welding operator has been used, spot radiography will be performed in such a manner that the welding of each welder or welding operator can be examined.
Junctions between circumferential and longitudinal seams will be considered as preferred locations for spot radiography.

### Helium Mass Spectrometer Test
When required by purchase order or referenced drawings tube/tubesheet joints and tube bundles will be tested using the helium mass spectrometer (reverse probe method) per ASME Code Section V, Article l0.

### Documentation
Fabricator will furnish a report containing all the data pertaining to each vessel or item as follows:
Prior to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics' final inspection of each unit, Fabricator will have prepared in finished form, three sets of records, except final test data, as set forth in this paragraph.
Mill test reports
Nameplate rubbing or facsimile
A copy of all inspection and test reports
Manufacturers affidavit
Shipping/Release form
These reports, together with final test data, will be assembled into three complete sets for each item.  In no case will a documentation package pertain to more than one vessel or item number.
The Inspector will at the time of witnessing the final tests and certifying correctness of forms, collect the appropriate number of documentation packages for each item tested.
