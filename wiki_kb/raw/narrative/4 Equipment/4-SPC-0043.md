# 4-SPC-0043 — Specification


[TABLE: 4-SPC-0043_table_1]


## Purpose
This Specification defines the requirements for shop-fabricated dual laminate (thermoplastic lined) fibreglass reinforced plastic (FRP) equipment such as liquid storage tanks or vessels.
This Specification gives Aker Kvaerner Chemetics requirements in the form of exceptions and additions to RTP-1 (1999 Edition of ASME RTP-1: Standard for Reinforced Thermoset Plastic Corrosion Resistant Equipment) referred to herein as RTP-1, and its Mandatory Appendix M-12, Dual Laminate Vessels.  Due to the present lack of RTP-1 accredited fabricators RTP-1 accreditation is not required to fabricate equipment to this Specification.

## General Requirements
The FRP structural wall and outer layers of the dual laminate vessels will be designed to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics standard 4-SPC-0042 “Fiberglass Reinforced Plastic (FRP) Equipment,” with the exception that the inner FRP corrosion liner will be omitted.  Other exceptions to RTP-1 and its mandatory appendix M-12 are as follows:

### User's Basic Requirements Specification (Para. 1-200)
This Specification and the information contained in the specifications, drawings or data sheets form the basis for design.  The User's Basic Requirements Specification (UBRS) will not be completed.

### Service Restrictions (Para. 1-210)
This Specification will not be used for vessels which are in a critical service.

### Accreditation (Para. 1-520)
Formal accreditation by ASME is not required to supply vessels to this Specification.

## Design

### Design will substantially be in accordance with Part 3 - Design and Non mandatory Appendices NM-2, NM-3, NM-4, NM-5, NM-12 and NM-13.

### Wall Thickness (Appendix NM-13)
All calculations for and references to wall thickness will apply to the structural layer of the laminate only.  The thermoplastic liner and the outer surface will be ignored in the design calculations.

### Minimum Thickness (Para. 3-200(f))
The minimum average structural wall thickness of any pressure containing part is to be 7mm (9/32”). The thermoplastic liner and the outer surface will be additional to this.

## Fabrication

### Fabrication will substantially be in accordance with Part 4 - Fabrication and Mandatory Appendix M-12, Dual Laminate Vessels. The equipment supplier will use the fabrication details given in Part 4 - Fabrication and Mandatory Appendix M-12, and required by this Specification regardless of vessel size or if Subpart 3B design rules are employed.

## Article B – Materials

### Scope (Para. M12B-100)
Materials will be substantially in accordance with Article B – Materials.
Unless agreed otherwise by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics, the FRP structural laminate applied to dual laminate vessels manufactured to this Specification will be RTP-1 Type II only.

### Thermoplastic Lining Materials (Para. M12B-200)
Unless permission is given by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics, the colour of the thermoplastic lining material is to be consistent in all vessel areas.
In each vessel only one of each thermoplastic sheet, tube and shape supplier will be used.
Within a vessel, all thermoplastic components must employ the same backing/bonding system.
Thermoplastic lining and welding filler materials are to be from Aker Kvaerner Chemetics approved sources only. The plastic supplier, point of origin and technical data sheets will be supplied with the fabricator’s quotation.
Only seamless (moulded or extruded) pipe, duct, angle, rectangular and square tube, piping fitting, etc. thermoplastic shapes will be used in vessels fabricated to this Specification.

### Fiber Backing Materials (Para. M12B-300)
Unless specified otherwise, organic fibre backing such as polyacrylonitrile (PAN) will be used in caustic service where service temperature permits.  Glass backing/bonding materials will be employed on fluoropolymer liners.

### Welding and Joining Materials (Para. M12B-400)
Welding filler materials will be supplied from the same supplier and raw material batch as the corresponding thermoplastic sheet.
The melt flow index of the weld filler material must closely match that of the parent materials to be joined.

### Filler Materials, Pigments, Processing Aids and Conductive Materials (Para. M12B-500 (b) and (d))
Conductive materials for spark testing targets will use carbon/graphite filled resin and a conductive surfacing veil.
Materials Receiving Procedures (Para. Strict adherence to the RTP-1 format is not required, as long as material traceability, thermoplastic supplier material test reports/Certificates of Compliance and visual, bond strength, liner thickness, spark test target conductivity, and component weldability testing are properly controlled and documented. Documentation will kept at the fabricator’s works and be accessible to the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Inspector.

Bonding Resin (Para. M12B-630)
The type and supplier of bonding resin used will be kept consistent within each vessel. Unless approved otherwise, the bonding resin will be produced from a Aker Kvaerner Chemetics approved source. Visual Inspection of Thermoplastic Shapes (Para. M12B-654.2 (b) and (c))
Visible contaminants inside the shapes are not acceptable.

## Article C – Design

### Sheet Map and Weld Placement (Para. M12C-300 (a), (b), (c) and (d))
The total amount of process exposed thermoplastic liner weld will be minimised. The fabricator will optimize layout and use the largest commercially available sheet to achieve this. The minimum acceptable sheet size will be 1.22m (4 ft.) by 2.44m (8 ft.) and continuously rolled thermoplastic sheet material is preferred. The fabricator’s sheet layout and weld map will be submitted to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for review and approval prior to the start of fabrication.
All sheet to sheet weld splicing made prior to hot forming, vacuum forming or cutting larger lining component blanks (shell courses, heads, bottoms, etc.) will be made by the automatic butt fusion/hot plate process only. Shell course longitudinal closing seams will also be made with the automatic butt fusion process.
Where the thermoplastic liner is supplied in sheets, the long side of the thermoplastic sheets (for example 2.44m (8 ft.) will be oriented parallel with the main vessel cylindrical axis, to minimise the number of manually made shell course welds. This requirement is waived when the thermoplastic material is supplied on a continuous roll.
Dished head and flat bottom liners will be hot formed in one piece with integrally connected formed radiused knuckles. Conical/toriconical type head liners may be hot/ vacuum formed in sections, but their head to shell junctions must employ a radiused knuckle. Dished heads are preferred to conical/toriconical heads, as manual welding is not required in dish liner fabrication.
To minimise the number of manual shell course welds, the fabricator will adjust/extend the straight flange length on the bottom and top heads (up to 225mm (9”)) to suit.
For smaller diameter vessels, the fabricator will attempt to source seamless thermoplastic ducting/piping to minimise the number of manual circumferential shell welds. Where possible, circumferential liner welds will be made with a large bore pipe/duct automatic butt fusion welding machine in this type of construction.
Longitudinal seams between thermoplastic liner shell courses will be staggered by 225mm (9”) minimum.
All corners and contours in the vessel thermoplastic lining will be radiused by heat forming. With the exception of nozzle to shell and flange face welds, 90 degree or sharp corners in the liner are forbidden.
Sheets and welds will be arranged to eliminate (or if unavoidable minimise) the need to put weld on top of weld.

### Wall Attachments (Para. M12C-400 (c), (e), and (f))
All mixing tank baffles will be fully sealed/encapsulated against the process to prevent any internal deposit build-up.
Unless size restricts, dip pipe supports will be as shown in Mandatory Appendix M-12, Fig. M12D-9.
Internal ledges and support rings will be FRP cored and conceptually as shown in Mandatory Appendix M-12, Fig. M14C-1. The fabricator will design the ledges and rings for the service requirements and the expected loading conditions. The number of circumferential liner segments in the ledges/rings will be minimized and when possible the thermoplastic covers will be vacuum formed in one piece.
Where possible, internal attachments and their reinforcement pads will be located to clear liner shell welds. Where intersections must occur, the shell welds will be buffed flush and the reinforcement pad continuously welded to the shell.
The thermoplastic reinforcing pads required at wall attachment points will be fully seal welded against the process.

### Design Stress Limitations (Para. M12C-500 (b) and (d))
All raw, un-fabricated thermoplastic shapes and sheet will be stress relief heat treated by their manufacturer or by the vessel fabricator prior to use.
Thermoplastic cold forming is not permitted in vessels manufactured to this Specification. All liner forming operations will be performed hot (thermoforming), above the softening temperature of the material.
If in any situation the fabricator believes that a post fabrication stress relief heat treatment of a complete liner or liner section is required, then the treatment will be performed.

## Article D – Fabrication

### Welding (Para. M12D-400)
Flow fusion and extrusion welding awill not be employed without prior evaluation and approval by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.

### Welding Procedures (Para. M12D-420 (b))
Misalignment of butt welds between sheets of different thickness will be tapered before or after welding so the thick to thin transition is smooth and gradual.

### Hot Gas and Extrusion Welding (Para. M12D-421 (i))
Where possible, single bevel, manual, hot gas welds will have the cap oriented to face the FRP overlay and the narrower root area exposed to the process. Both sides of the weld bead are to be dressed to remove any irregularities, flow lines, potential stress risers and to give the weld a smooth and uniform appearance. Visual inspection by the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Inspector and/or the fabricator’s QC representative will be performed prior to any weld dressing or FRP overlay operation.

### Hot Plate Fusion Welds (Para. M12D-422)
Both sides of the automatic fusion weld bead are to be dressed flush with the parent thermoplastic sheet after welding. Visual inspection of the dressed weld by the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Inspector and/or the fabricator’s QC representative will be performed prior to any FRP overlay operation.

### Flow Fusion Welding (Para. M12D-423)
Flow Fusion welding will not be employed without prior evaluation and approval by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.

### Cap Strip Welding (Para. M12D-424)
Cap Strip welding will not be employed without prior evaluation and approval by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.

### High Voltage Spark Test (Para. M12D-510)
All weld areas will have conductive spark testing targets installed under the thermoplastic liner. The targets may also be extended to cover the complete under surface of the vessel liner.
All welds will be spark tested a minimum of 1) after welding, 2) after the dual laminate vessel is complete and 3) following hydrostatic/pressure testing.
All spark test indications will be repaired as agreed between the fabricator and the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Inspector. The minimum acceptable repair is completely removing the weld in the defect area and re-welding the joint.

### Fabrication Options (Para. M12D-610)
When commercially available, seamless nozzle neck liner pipes will be used. Manway nozzle neck liners may be fabricated from sheet stock, as long as the longitudinal seams are made by the automatic butt fusion/hot plate welding technique.

### Shell-Neck and Flange-Neck Designs (Para. M12D-620(a), (b) and (c))
Typical nozzle details will be as given in Figs. M12D-6 and 7, except the “Alternative Weld Design” in Fig. M12D-6 will not be used.
The flush mounted bottom drain nozzle detail given in the upper part of Fig. M12D-8 is acceptable, but only the FRP structural reinforcement detail for the pump suction nozzle shown in the lower part of Fig. M12D-8 is to be used.
All nozzle welds will be spark tested.

### Testing Bond Strength Between Liner and RTP Overlay (Para. M12D-920)
To ensure that the bond between the liner and the FRP is stronger than that of either of the individual components, the fabricator will perform production bond strength tests. The testing will be performed on coupons produced from vessel cut outs, nozzle neck extensions or made on the bench at the time of fabrication with identical construction and technique to that of the actual vessel. The production bond strength tests required are:
Chisel Test: The “chisel” test consists of jamming a chisel or screwdriver into the Thermoplastic/FRP bond. Clean bond separation with no fracture in the thermoplastic or FRP indicates a test failure. The fabricator will test a minimum of (3) vessel cut-outs. The Aker Kvaerner Chemetics inspector may or may not witness these tests, but the results will be made available to him on request. Chisel test failures will require additional testing to satisfy Aker Kvaerner Chemetics that the vessel is fit for service.
Lap Shear Test (BS 4994 B-10 or ASTM D3846-mod.): At least one series of production lap shear bond strength tests will be performed on coupons taken from the actual vessel or made at the time of production using the exact same materials, construction and techniques as those in the vessel. The coupon sampling location and type will be agreed with the Aker Kvaerner Chemetics Inspector. The thermoplastic/FRP bond will have a minimum strength of 7 MPa (1015 psi.). Testing results will be made available to the Inspector and failures (results less than 7 MPa (1015 psi.)) will require additional testing to satisfy Aker Kvaerner Chemetics that the bond strength is acceptable.
DIN 53769 ring shear test may be used on small diameter (less than 75 mm dia.) dual laminate piping.

### Inspection (Para. M12D-1000)
Complete and comprehensive internal and external visual inspections will be performed by the fabricator 1) after welding, 2) after the dual laminate vessel is complete and 3) following hydrostatic/pressure testing.

## Article E – Inspection and Test

### Scope (Para. M12E-100)
The secondary FRP corrosion liner must meet the requirements of this Specification and RTP-1, Part 6 - Inspection and Tests.

## Article G – Shop Qualification

### Fabricator’s Facilities and Equipment (Para. M12G-300 (a) and (c))
To avoid weld and bonding surface contamination, the fabricator will have a separate thermoplastic welding and fabrication shop, complete with raw material storage area.

## Specific Requirements for Specific liner materials

### For PVC/FRP vessels
PVC liner material will be Darvic 505 material or approved equal
Minimum liner thickness is 4.0 mm
PVC liner material and welding consumables will be pigmented to protect against UV degradation.
Sheets to be as large as possible
Fusion bonding (hot-plate welding) is preferred and its use will be maximized
Hand welding will be done only where absolutely necessary.
Fabricator will submit a weld map to show where fusion welds and hand welds are made
The PVC sheet will be abraded (by sanding) and treated with a suitable bonding resin to allow the development of adequate bond strength between the PVC and the FRP laminate
Vendor will retain and clearly identify all nozzle cutouts for a minimum of three years and submit them to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics on request.
PVC/FRP nozzle liner pipe will conform to ASTM D1785 Sch. 80 for sizes less than or equal to 50mm (2”) and Sch. 40 for larger sizes.
Shipping and storage is not allowed at temperatures at or below 0°C (32F).

### For CPVC/FRP vessels
CPVC liner material will be produced by Noveon.  Material from other suppliers will be considered by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for approval
Minimum liner thickness is 3.0 mm
CPVC liner material and welding consumables will be pigmented to protect against UV degradation.
Sheets will be as large as possible in order to reduce the number of welds required.
Fusion bonding (hot-plate welding) is preferred and its use will be maximized
Hand welding will be done only where absolutely necessary.
Fabricator will submit a weld map to show where fusion welds and hand welds are made
The CPVC sheet will be abraded (by sanding) and treated with a suitable bonding resin to allow the development of adequate bond strength between the CPVC and the FRP laminate
All CPVC welds will be stress relieved.
Vendor will retain and clearly identify all nozzle cutouts for a minimum of three years and submit them to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics on request.
Shipping and storage is not allowed at temperatures at or below 0°C (32F).

### For PP/FRP vessels
PP liner material will be from an approved Aker Kvaerner Chemetics source. Material from other suppliers will be considered by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for approval
Minimum liner thickness is 3.0 mm
PP liner material and welding consumables will be pigmented to protect against UV degradation.
Sheets will be as large as possible in order to reduce the number of welds required.
Fusion bonding (hot-plate welding) is preferred and its use will be maximized
Hand welding will be done only where absolutely necessary.
Fabricator will submit a weld map to show where fusion welds and hand welds are made
The PP sheet or liner pipe will be will be backed with either glass cloth or Polyacrylonitrile (PAN).  Selection of backing will be confirmed with Aker Kvaerner Chemetics before work commences.
Vendor will retain and clearly identify all nozzle cutouts for a minimum of three years and submit them to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics on request.
Shipping and storage is not allowed at temperatures at or below 0°C (32F).

### For FEP/FRP Vessels
All welds will be hot-plate fusion welds. Hand welding is not allowed
Loose liners are allowed and often required by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics
Where backing is required glass backing will be used
Sheets will be as large as possible in order to reduce the number of welds required.
Minimum liner thickness will be 2.5 mm

### ECTFE (Halar)/FRP Liners
ECTFE liner material will be from an approved Aker Kvaerner Chemetics source. Material from other suppliers will be considered by Aker Kvaerner Chemetics for approval
Minimum liner thickness is 2.5 mm
Sheets will be as large as possible in order to reduce the number of welds required
Fusion bonding (hot-plate welding) is preferred and its use will be maximized
Hand welding will be done only where absolutely necessary
Where hand welding is done, nitrogen will be used as the cover gas
Fabricator will submit a weld map to show where fusion welds and hand welds are made
Vacuum forming will not be used to form ECTFE
The ECTFE sheet or liner pipe will be will be backed with either glass cloth or Polyacrylonitrile (PAN).  Selection of backing will be confirmed with Aker Kvaerner Chemetics before work commences
Vendor will retain and clearly identify all nozzle cutouts for a minimum of three years and submit them to Aker Kvaerner Chemetics on request.
