# 4-PRC-0009 — Procedure


[TABLE: 4-PRC-0009_table_1]


## Purpose
This procedure outlines requirements for mechanical calculations of acid plant radial flow gas/gas heat exchangers.

## Confidentiality
The contents of this procedure are strictly confidential and as such are not to be duplicated or divulged to persons outside the employ of Aker Kvaerner Chemetics.

## Information Required
The following documents must be available before mechanical calculations can be done:
Exchanger sizing, operating conditions (e.g. temperatures, pressures) and configuration are determined by 4-CAL-0001: TGAS/2.
Site specific requirements (e.g. wind, seismic) are given on 1-DST-0001: Site Design Conditions.
Additional equipment requirements are given in 4-SPC-0001: General Equipment Requirements.

## Design Criteria
Design criteria must be established as outlined below.  The designer must consider all operating conditions including startup, shutdown and plant upset to determine the worst combination of pressure/temperature present.

### Design Pressures
Components forming the pressure boundary will be designed for the maximum  pressure.  Components not on the pressure boundary will be designed for the maximum differential pressure present considering all operating conditions including startup and shutdown.

### Design Temperatures
Design and mean metal temperatures are determined by 4-CAL-0002: Gas/Gas Hx Design Temperatures. This calculation must be performed for all operating cases (gas strengths) to determine the worst case for design metal temperature of individual components.  Temperatures are calculated for the following components:
Top vestibule
Top tubesheet
Top expansion plate
Shell
Bottom expansion plate
Bottom tubesheet
Bottom vestibule (tubeside)
Bottom vestibule (shellside)
Tubes
Base ring

### Stress Criteria
Primary membrane stresses are evaluated per ASME(1) Section VIII, Division 1.  Local and secondary stresses including stress combinations (i.e. membrane + bending) are evaluated per ASME(2) Section VIII, Division 2 (Figure 4-130.1).

### Materials of Construction
Generic shellside and tubeside materials are specified on the process data sheet.  The designer is responsible for specifying the particular material specification for each component.

## Mechanical Design Calculations
Mechanical design of gas/gas exchanger components is a combination of proprietary methods and ASME Section VIII, Division 1.  Allowable stress values and physical properties are taken from ASME(3) Section II, Part D.

### Pressure Boundary
Main pressure parts (shell, vestibules, heads) are modelled using the PVElite® pressure vessel design software.  The following points must be considered.
The weight of the tube bundle (including acoustic baffles) is accounted for by adding a packed section of the appropriate density.
For nozzle reinforcement, the required shell thickness is based on the greater of that due to internal pressure or longitudinal bending.
Loads due to duct reactions will be entered for each duct connection.  Horizontal forces/moments are assumed to act in the same direction.  Vertical forces are assumed to act downwards.  Horizontal moments will be entered as negative values.  Vertical moments (torsion) will be neglected.  Loads will be combined using the “Algebraic” method.  Design loads (per meter of duct diameter) are 30 kN vertical force (Fy), 30 kN horizontal force (Fx) and 100 kN*m overturning moment (Mz).
The appropriate thickness and density (including allowance for cladding/hardware) of insulation must be entered.
Wind and seismic loads do not act simultaneously.
Wind, seismic and duct loads will be considered in combination with and without internal pressure present.  Duct loads will not be considered when checking nozzle reinforcement.
The wind load multiplier will be 1.0.
The allowable longitudinal stress multiplier for wind and seismic loads will be 1.2.

### Tubesheets
Tubesheets are designed using 4-CAL-0003: Gas/Gas Hx Tubesheets.  The tubesheet is modelled as an annular plate under uniform load, simply supported at both edges.  Loading is due to pressure, bundle and shell weight (including insulation and vertical duct loads) and differential expansion.  Operating conditions to consider are normal operation, maximum pressure and hot shut down (full operating temperature, no pressure).  Where shellside and tubeside streams are pressurized by different sources (e.g. SO3 cooler, preheater) tubesheets should also be checked for full pressure on one side and no pressure on the other side.  Resultant stresses are grouped as primary stresses (pressure, weight) and primary plus secondary (thermal) stresses.

### Expansion Plates
Differential expansion must be calculated for all cases as described in Section 4.2.  There are two methods for accommodating differential growth between the shell and tubes.
The standard method for accommodating thermal growth is to use annular flat plates at each tubesheet.  Design is per 4-CAL-0004: Gas/Gas Hx Expansion Plates.  Loading is due to internal pressure, shell weight (including insulation and cladding) and differential movement.  For the line loads (weight and differential movement), the plates are modelled as fixed inside/free outside.  For internal pressure, the plates are modelled as fixed inside/simply supported outside.  Resultant stresses are grouped as in Section 5.2.
Where thermal growths cannot be accommodated by annular plates,  a triple convolution expansion joint at the upper tubesheet is used.  Design is per 4-CAL-0007: Gas/Gas Hx Expansion Joint.  Only differential movement and internal pressure are considered as the weight of the shell is supported at the bottom.  Spring rate calculations are per TEMA(4) Section 8, while stress calculations are per the method of Kopp & Sayre(5).
Operating conditions to consider are normal operation, maximum pressure and hot shut down

### Floor Plate
Type 4 bottom vestibules have a sloped floor plate.  This plate is exposed to the shellside pressure and must be designed to withstand this.  Calculations for the plate and stiffeners are done per 4-CAL-0006: Gas/Gas Hx Floor Plate.  Deflection should be limited to half the plate thickness.

### Anchor Chairs
Anchor chairs and anchor bolts will be calculated per 4-CAL-0019: Anchor Chair.  Calculations will be performed for both wind and seismic conditions.  Duct loads will be included for both conditions.

### Lift Lugs
Lift lugs and tailing lugs will be designed per 4-CAL-0023: Gas/Gas HX Lift Lugs.

### Miscellaneous Components
Miscellaneous components will be modelled using the CodeCalc® design software.  Specific components to analyze are as follows:
Internal dished head (type 2, 3 or 4 shell) subject to pressure on concave and convex side as applicable.
Cone to cylinder junction for type 4 bottom vestibule.  Axial load on the cylinder is calculated per 4-CAL-0011: Gas/gas Hx Load Distribution.
Tubes will be checked for external (shellside) pressure.
Shell nozzle on type 4 top vestibules will be checked for external (tubeside) pressure.

COMMENTARY
To Section 1: This procedure applies to external gas/gas exchangers only.  Exchangers internal to the converter are covered by a separate procedure.
To Section 5: Gas/gas exchangers are not considered pressure vessels by ASME rules.  When the equipment is considered a pressure vessel by the local jurisdiction (e.g. Australia, Europe) the design methods described in this procedure must be modified to reflect the particular jurisdictional requirements.
To Section 5.1: Duct loads and orientations will normally not be available at the start of equipment design.  For conservatism, loads will be assumed to act in the same direction.  Horizontal moments (Mz) are entered as negative values because positive horizontal forces (Fx) produce negative resultant moments.  Design loads are average loads based on results from previous projects.
